# Modules

Knotworking is a **platform** plus **modules**. The platform is always on: sign-in, workspaces, members and invitations, teams, API tokens, the sync log, attachments, notifications and search. Everything else is a module that can be switched on per environment and granted per member.

The first four are the original workspace features and default to the member's role. `items`, `recipes`, `sops`, `stock`, `customers`, `despatch`, `demand`, `production`, `haccp`, `quality`, `purchasing`, `costing`, `site`, `labels`, `specifications`, `testing`, `npd`, `defence` and `governance` are manufacturing modules and `people`, `training` and `attendance` are workforce modules. Any business can use `assets`, `maintenance` and `checklists` independently of the manufacturing modules. All of them are opt-in: nobody but owners and admins can use them until they are granted.

The manifest is `packages/core/src/modules.ts`. Server, web app and MCP server all read it; nothing else decides what a module is.

## Hard and soft dependencies

A module declares two kinds of dependency in the manifest:

- **Hard** (`dependsOn`, the table above): modules it cannot work without. They must be active for it to be switched on (`KNOTWORKING_MODULES`, the workspace's module settings) and readable by the member for it to be open to them (`resolveModuleAccess`). They come earlier in `moduleIds`.
- **Soft** (`enhancedBy`): modules it integrates with but works without, so a workspace can switch on only what it needs. They never affect activation or access and have no ordering rule. When one is active and the caller can read it, extra features appear; otherwise the code checks at runtime and degrades: the server refuses a link to the other module's records (`404 module_inactive` when it is off, `403 module_forbidden` when the caller can't read it), leaves linked records out of responses (`hideUnreadableLinks`: the link's id stays, the record is `null`), refuses whole sub-features that make no sense without it, or reports a `basis`; the web app hides the pages, tabs and fields (`requireModule` layouts, `useWorkspace().can`); the MCP server leaves out tools that need it (`requiresModules`). Workspace settings show them as "Works better with".

## Customers and despatch (Phase 3.1)

- **Customers**: shared customer reference data: unique code (case-insensitive), business name/address, contact name/email/phone, an optional tax number (VAT or other registration number, printed on new invoices) and active/archive status. Read and write need the corresponding customer module level. No deletion route; archive a referenced customer. Queries are paginated, with name/code search. Only id, code, name, status and update time enter sync; contacts and addresses stay in protected query responses.
- **Despatch drafts**: a customer, delivery address, despatch date, external order reference, notes and up to 100 lot/location/quantity lines. Only active finished products; combine duplicate lot/location lines. Creating/replacing a draft needs `write`, supports API tokens and withdraws no stock. Customer and lot selection are searched, not limited to a fixed customer catalogue.
- **Confirm**: `POST /despatch/:id/confirm` is an attestation by a signed-in person with `manage`, never an API token. It rechecks the customer, active locations/items, QA release, expiry (best-before as well as use-by), unreserved balances and the workspace's current day. Expiry today is allowed; expiry before today or future despatch dates are refused. Production reservations apply even when that module is hidden from the caller. All lines withdraw together under the workspace lock or none do. Confirmation also requires `expectedUpdatedAt`: a changed draft must be reloaded and reviewed before a manager can confirm it. A required UUID `commandId` makes a same-person retry with identical input return the original result; reuse for a different record/action is refused.
- **Historical facts**: confirming snapshots the then-current customer business/contact details, the delivery address chosen in the draft, item code/name/display unit, lot code, location and expiry. Master-data edits cannot rewrite a confirmed delivery. Confirmed headers and lines cannot be edited. The physical despatch date is a workspace business date; ledger timestamps record when the confirmation was entered.
- **Void**: `POST /despatch/:id/void`, with `commandId` and reason, is a signed-in manager's correction. A confirmed despatch gets opposite ledger entries referring to its originals; both records stay. A draft may be voided without stock movements. Voids correct recording mistakes; physical customer returns and quarantine are separate future work. An archived source location must be reactivated before a confirmed record can be voided. The generic stock reversal route refuses despatch movements.
- **One stock ledger**: `stock_movements.type = despatch` records outbound quantities; reversals use the same type with the opposite sign. Stock-only readers see a delivery-note reference and quantity, not customer contacts or addresses. Production mass balance and mock-recall prefill include net despatched quantities. Customer/despatch changes are module-filtered invalidation metadata, not bootstrap collections.
- **Recipient query**: `GET /despatch/lots/:lotId/recipients` is paginated and returns confirmed direct deliveries with their original customer/address/contact snapshots. It excludes voided records and explicitly reports `scope: direct_lot_only`. It does not yet traverse ingredient-to-finished-product genealogy; the production trace remains a separate view. Do not present it as a complete recall impact report.
- **Other API routes**: `GET/POST /customers`, `GET/PATCH /customers/:id`; `GET/POST /despatch`, `GET/PUT /despatch/:id`. List despatches by status, customer and date range. Both modules have inactive/forbidden route protection and token allowlist enforcement.
- **Web**: `/<workspace>/customers`, `/<workspace>/despatch`, `/despatch/new`, `/despatch/:id`, `/despatch/:id/edit`, `/despatch/:id/print`, `/despatch/recipients?lot=<uuid>`. Customer and despatch navigation are grant-gated. Delivery notes mark drafts and voids prominently. Stock lot pages optionally show recipients only when the viewer can read Despatch (and therefore Customers).
- **MCP**: `list_customers`, `create_customer`, `update_customer`, `list_despatches`, `get_despatch`, `draft_despatch`, `get_lot_recipients`. No confirmation or void tool. UUID references are explicit; APIs enforce all permissions on every call.
- **Forward impact (Phase 3.1.2)**: `GET /despatch/lots/:lotId/impact` and `/impact.csv` follow net production consumption/output into descendant lots and confirmed despatch lines, in one repeatable-read read-only snapshot. They additionally require Production read access; the existing direct recipient query does not. A despatch's affected lines occur once each even when several paths reach their lot. Totals count distinct despatches and customer accounts. Quantities stay in each item's unit; no contamination fraction is inferred. The report returns `coverage`, affected `lots`, `batches`, `summary` and paginated `deliveries` with original contacts. Depth is 1–20 (default 10); hard caps are 2,000 lots, 2,000 batches and 10,000 output links. Coverage is explicitly partial for untraced depth/resources, open batches, missing output or missing lot metadata. Zero deliveries in a partial trace never means no affected customers. CSV includes coverage notes before its contact rows and refuses over 10,000 known delivery lines. No writes, new tables or bootstrap/sync collections. Web: `/despatch/impact`, with a historical lot-code search (including empty lots), refresh/depth controls, delivery paging and CSV download. MCP: `get_distribution_impact`, offered only when Despatch and Production are both visible.

## Module activation and access

**1. The environment decides which modules are available.** `KNOTWORKING_MODULES` is a comma-separated list of ids (`tasks,docs`). Unset means every module except those flagged off by default (`calendar`, `accounting`, `bi`); `all` includes those modules; `none` means the platform alone. An unknown id or a missing dependency (`time` without `tasks`) stops the API at startup. The API and MCP server read the same variable.

**2. Owners and admins decide which available modules the workspace uses.** First-run setup offers **General Business** for work, documents, customers and people operations, and **Food Manufacturing** to use all server-enabled modules. Existing workspaces also follow the server until an admin saves a custom selection under **Workspace settings → Modules**. Enabling a module in the form selects its dependencies. Clearing a module also clears the modules that depend on it. To resume following the server, select **Food Manufacturing** and save.

Tables of inactive modules still exist (there is one migration history). Switching a module off hides it. Its routes answer `404 module_inactive`, and its data is left out of bootstrap, sync responses, search and the MCP tool list. Switching it back on restores access. Manufacturing checks still use evidence from server-enabled modules, including when a workspace hides Testing, Specifications, or Labels. Lot release responses withhold evidence details when the viewer lacks access to the source module.

**3. Admins decide who may use each active module.** Settings → Members → Module access. Each member has a level per module:

| Level    | Meaning                                                                       |
| -------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `none`   | The module doesn't exist for this person                                      |
| `read`   | Safe methods only                                                             |
| `write`  | Everything the module's ordinary routes offer                                 |
| `manage` | Module-specific privileged actions (approvals, adjustments); see the contract |

Rules (`resolveModuleAccess`):

- Owners and admins always have `manage` on every active module.
- Otherwise the explicit grant in `member_module_access` applies, or the module's `defaultAccess`: `role` (members `write`, guests `read`: how the four original modules behave) or `none` (opt-in: an admin must grant it; the ERP modules will use this).
- Guests never get more than `read`.
- A module is `none` for a member when a module it depends on (`dependsOn`) is `none`. Soft dependencies (`enhancedBy`) never close a module.

API tokens act with their owner's module access, optionally narrowed to a module allowlist chosen when the token is created (`modules` on `POST /v1/me/api-tokens`; it can only take modules away). The MCP server offers only the tools of modules that are active and that the token's user can read.

## API access for person-only actions

Some workspace routes refuse API tokens (and so the MCP server and integrations) and need a signed-in person. They come in two kinds. Code: `packages/core/src/token-actions.ts`, `apps/server/src/{middleware,services,routes}/token-actions.ts`, `token-actions.test.ts`.

- **Toggleable operational actions** (`tokenActions` in core, guarded by `personOrToken("<module>.<action>")`). An owner or admin may open each to tokens for the workspace: `purchasing.send`, `purchasing.close`, `purchasing.cancel`, `stock.waste`, `stock.adjust`, `stock.stocktake`, `production.cancel`, `attendance.events`, `attendance.visit_sign_out`, `attendance.payroll_export`, `bi.publish` and `bi.board_packs`.
- **Always a person** (`personOnlyActions` in core, guarded by `forbidTokens`). They can never be switched on, whatever the setting. There are four kinds, each with the reason the settings page shows:
  - **Sign-offs and approvals**: approving suppliers, recipes, SOPs and HACCP plans, reviewing a HACCP plan's hazard analysis, signing off a day's CCP monitoring (one plan or several at once), QA lot release, completing a batch, training sign-off, CCP log review, closing corrective actions, verifying cleaning, closing complaints and non-conformances, completing audits, recording recall tests, approving product labels, approving supplier invoices for payment, and on `site` reviewing storage readings, closing corrective actions, releasing an area after a breakage, the hygiene clearance after maintenance and signing off pre-start and changeover checks.
  - **Records that attest a person did something**: SOP acknowledgement, issuing and calling in controlled SOP copies, cleaning and calibration records, correcting a CCP log entry, and on `site` glass checks, completed maintenance, pre-start and changeover checks and corrections of site records.
  - **Corrections of the ledger**: reversing stock movements and production records, voiding clock events, training records and recall tests.
  - **Safety and security**: roll calls, kiosk devices and pairing codes, staff PINs and unlocks, attendance settings, and the invoice match tolerances (they decide which invoices need a reason to be paid).

`route-classification.test.ts` fails for a `forbidTokens` route that is not covered by an entry in `personOnlyActions`, and for a `personOrToken` route whose action is not in `tokenActions`.

**The toggle.** Settings → API access (`/<workspace>/settings/api-access`) lists the toggleable actions of the active modules, each with its label, what a token could then do, the access level it still needs, and who switched it on and when. It is per workspace and **off by default**. Only owners and admins signed in with their own account can change it: `PUT /v1/workspaces/:slug/token-actions/:action` with `{ "enabled": boolean }` (`requireAdmin` and `requireSessionCredential`, so a token can never widen its own powers). A real change is recorded in the sync log as a `token_action` platform change (`{ id, action, module, enabled, enabledBy, enabledAt }`, the same shape the list gives) so open clients refresh; switching to the state it is already in changes nothing. `GET …/token-actions` (any member, tokens allowed) returns the actions of the active modules the caller can read with `enabled`, `enabledBy` and `enabledAt`, the always-a-person list with reasons, and `canManage`. The MCP server reads it to decide which tools to show.

**What a token still needs.** Switching an action on only removes the "person only" rule. The route's other checks apply as they do to a session: the token's owner must have the route's module level (`write` or `manage`, as listed in the action's `level`), the token's module allowlist must include the module (`403 module_forbidden` otherwise), and a read-scope token can still only make GETs, so of the eleven actions only `attendance.payroll_export` works with one.

**Audit log.** Every request a token makes through a switched-on action is appended to `api_token_action_log` once its response is decided, outside the handler's transaction, with the action, method, path, response status, the token (its name is kept even after the token is deleted) and its owner. That includes calls then refused or failed (403, 400, 409), because the attempt matters: the app-level `tokenActionAudit` middleware runs before the module gate, so refusals by the module level or the token's module allowlist are recorded too. Calls refused because the action is off are not logged, and sessions are never logged. Admins read it, newest first, at `GET …/token-actions/log?limit=&offset=&tokenId=` (sessions only) and in a paged table on the API access page. It is never synced.

**The refusal.** A token calling a toggleable route while its action is off gets `403 token_not_allowed` with `details.action` set to the action id and a message saying an owner or admin can allow it under Settings → API access. Always-a-person routes answer `token_not_allowed` with "API tokens can't be used for this" and no details.

**Inactive modules.** Actions of a module that is not active are not listed and cannot be toggled (`404 module_inactive`).

**Per-token allowlists.** The workspace toggle says what tokens may do at all; each token also carries its own list (`api_tokens.token_actions`, set in Settings → API tokens under "Person-only actions", or `tokenActions` on `POST /v1/me/api-tokens` and `PATCH /v1/me/api-tokens/:id`, sessions only, so a token can never widen itself). A token may use an action only when the workspace has it on **and** the token's list names it. Decisions: a new token starts with an empty list (least privilege); a token made before this existed has no list (`null`) and keeps behaving as before, every action the workspace allows, until someone edits it (the edit sends an explicit list). A token the workspace allows but its own list refuses gets `403 token_not_allowed` with `details: { action, reason: "token_allowlist" }`, and the call is audited like any other. `GET …/token-actions` adds `allowedForToken` to each action when a token asks (`null` for people), and the MCP server shows a gated tool only when `enabled` and `allowedForToken` are both true.

**Limits.** A token may also carry `max_order_value` (`maxOrderValue`, per token, no currency: the workspace has none yet): the largest purchase order it may send with `purchasing.send`. Over the cap, or an order with an unpriced line whose value can't be checked, is `403 token_limit_exceeded` (`details: { action, limit, value, valueComplete }`), decided inside the send transaction under the workspace lock, so nothing is sent. The refusal is written to `api_token_action_log` with its message in `note`, and shows on the API access page. People are never capped. No other limits exist yet.

**Notifications.** A successful token call through a toggled action creates a `token_action_used` notification (see [notifications](notifications.md)) for the token's owner and every owner and admin of the workspace, after the response is decided. Refused and failed calls are in the audit but notify no one.

**Not built yet**: limits other than the order value (for example a daily count or a stock-adjustment size), and a workspace-wide default for new tokens' allowlist.

## Contract for a new module

A module is done when all of this is true. The tests named here fail if a step is skipped.

1. **Manifest**: an entry in `packages/core/src/modules.ts` (id, name, `dependsOn`, `enhancedBy`, `defaultAccess`, `syncEntities`). Put a module in `dependsOn` only if this one can't work without it; integrations that only add value go in `enhancedBy` (`[]` when none), checked at runtime with `canRead(c, "<id>")` (see [Hard and soft dependencies](#hard-and-soft-dependencies)). New specialist modules use `defaultAccess: "none"`. Shared workspace workflows may use role defaults with record-level access checks. Dependencies must come earlier in `moduleIds` (`modules.test.ts`).
2. **Schema**: tables in `packages/db/src/schema/<module>.ts` (re-exported from `schema.ts`, column helpers from `schema/helpers.ts`), each with `workspace_id`; edit the schema only. Run `pnpm db:generate` once, on the integration branch, when the module merges (parallel branches each generating a migration is what forced renumbering in the past).
3. **Validation**: zod schemas in `@knotworking/core` (`schemas.ts`), shared by server, web and MCP. No DOM or Node APIs.
4. **Services**: every write in a transaction that calls `recordChanges`. Only small reference data belongs in the sync log and bootstrap; ledgers, plans and history are paginated query endpoints. Quantities and money are `numeric`, never floats; anything that checks then writes a balance takes a row lock or `lockWorkspaceLog`.
5. **Routes**: chained Hono routes mounted at `/v1/workspaces/:slug`, with a rule in `apps/server/src/modules/routes.ts` (`modules.test.ts` fails for an unowned route) and a classification in `route-classification.test.ts`. Privileged actions use `requireModuleLevel("<id>", "manage")`; actions that must be a person (approvals, sign-offs) also use `forbidTokens`. A route that tokens cannot use by default is one of two things (see "API access for person-only actions"): an operational action an owner or admin may open to tokens, which uses `personOrToken("<module>.<action>")` with an entry in core `tokenActions`, or an action that must always be a person's, which uses `forbidTokens` with an entry in `personOnlyActions`. `route-classification.test.ts` fails otherwise.
6. **Leaks**: if the module's data can surface through a platform feature (search, notifications, attachments, mentions), filter it there by `canRead(c, "<id>")`.
7. **MCP**: tools in `apps/mcp/src/tools/<module>.ts` (a `register…Tools(ctx)` function called from `tools.ts`), each with `module: "<id>"`, written against the API client, with a fake in `apps/mcp/src/testing/fake-<module>.ts` and tests beside the tools. No tools for sign-offs. A tool for a toggleable action also declares `tokenAction: "<id>"` and is shown only while that action is on. A plain list of records or one record by id is a **resource** (`resource: { name, op: "list" | "get" }` in its `define()`), read through `find_records` / `get_record`; a get takes exactly one required parameter (its id). Writes, reports and plans stay named tools, reached in catalogue mode through `describe_module` and `run_module_tool`. Pin a tool (`pinned: true`) only when agents need it in nearly every conversation. See [mcp.md](mcp.md#choosing-tools). Prefer a few coarse tools over one per route: every tool costs context.
8. **Web**: pages under `apps/web/src/app/[workspace]/<route>/` with a `requireModule` layout, an entry in `apps/web/src/lib/sidebar-nav.ts` (the sidebar area for the module: its pages and the access each needs; `sidebar-nav.test.ts` checks that every link is a real page and every top-level route belongs to an area), new top-level routes added to `RESERVED_WORKSPACE_SLUGS` and `deploy/Caddyfile`, components shown on `/design`.
9. **Dates**: decide every business date in the workspace's time zone (`todayIn(c)`, `workspaceToday`, the `@knotworking/core` `time-zone.ts` helpers; see [architecture.md](architecture.md#the-workspace-time-zone)), never with `toISOString().slice(0, 10)` or the browser's zone.
10. **Docs**: a section here and in `docs/data-model.md`; tools listed in `docs/mcp.md`.

## General business workflows

- **Project templates.** Open Projects → Use template. Employee onboarding and Client kickoff create an ordinary project and dated tasks together. Choose the team, project lead and start date. Tasks initially go to the project lead and can be reassigned or edited normally. The template action requires Tasks write access.
- **Purchase requests (`purchase_requests`).** Open Operations → Purchase requests. A teammate submits a description, estimated amount, reason, optional needed-by date and an approver with Manage access. The requester can withdraw a pending request. The designated approver records approval or rejection and a reason for any rejection. Requests notify the approver on submission and the requester on decision. The requester, designated approver and module managers can read the record. Request details stay out of the workspace replica. An approval is an internal spending decision. Orders, receipts and payment happen separately.
- **Client services (`client_services`).** Open Operations → Client services and create a fixed-fee or hourly quote for a customer. A manager issues the quote, records the customer's acceptance and creates a linked delivery project. Hourly delivery can approve up to the lesser of the quoted time and finished time logged on that project. Fixed-fee delivery records the milestone and evidence. Sign-off freezes the billing amount and makes a CSV billing draft available. Finance reviews tax and posts or sends the invoice in its accounting system. The module needs Customers and Time, and signs decisions in a session. Its sync records carry an ID and revision only.

The General Business preset includes these modules when the server makes them available. Give teammates the required module grants under **Members → Module access**. Roles default to write for Purchase requests, while Client services is opt-in. A workspace with a saved custom module selection can enable them under **Workspace settings → Modules**.

## Attachments on module records

Files (certificates, specification sheets, photos, reply letters) can hang on a module's records, not just on tasks and documents. It is one mechanism with a registry, so a module adds a target without new tables, routes or panels.

| Target (`entityType`)      | Module           | Kinds                                | Locked (files can't be deleted) when                              |
| -------------------------- | ---------------- | ------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `training_record`          | `training`       | certificate                          | signed off or voided                                              |
| `supplier`                 | `items`          | certificate, specification           | approved                                                          |
| `item`                     | `items`          | specification                        | never                                                             |
| `quality_complaint`        | `quality`        | photo, reply letter                  | closed                                                            |
| `quality_nonconformance`   | `quality`        | photo, document                      | closed                                                            |
| `site_glass_breakage`      | `site`           | photo                                | released                                                          |
| `haccp_corrective_action`  | `haccp`          | photo, document                      | closed                                                            |
| `purchase_order`           | `purchasing`     | document                             | closed or cancelled                                               |
| `purchase_invoice`         | `purchasing`     | document                             | approved or superseded                                            |
| `quality_audit`            | `quality`        | document, photo                      | completed or cancelled                                            |
| `quality_recall_test`      | `quality`, `stock`        | document, photo                      | always (recorded final)                                           |
| `purchase_receipt`         | `purchasing`     | document, photo                      | never                                                             |
| `purchase_receipt_line`    | `purchasing`     | photo, document                      | never                                                             |
| `haccp_calibration_record` | `haccp`          | certificate, document, photo         | always (append-only)                                              |
| `haccp_cleaning_record`    | `haccp`          | photo, document                      | verified                                                          |
| `sop`                      | `sops`           | photo, document                      | never as a whole; a file a draft or version uses can't be deleted |
| `specification`            | `specifications` | specification, certificate, document | never (a new issue's sheet is filed beside the old ones)          |
| `testing_sample`           | `testing`        | certificate, document, photo         | complete or cancelled                                             |
| `npd_project`              | `npd`            | document, photo, specification       | launched or cancelled                                             |
| `defence_exercise`         | `defence`        | document, photo                      | never                                                             |

- **Registry.** `attachmentTargets` in `@knotworking/core` (`attachments.ts`) gives each target's sync entity type, module, label and allowed kinds; `apps/server/src/services/attachment-targets.ts` gives its table and when it is locked. Adding a target is one entry in each (plus a panel on its page). The route, the sync filter, the MCP tool and the web panel read from them.
- **API.** `POST /attachments` takes `entityType`, `entityId` and an optional `kind` (default: the target's first kind) beside `file`. `GET /attachments?entityType&entityId` lists a record's files and says whether the record is `locked`. `GET /attachments/:id`, `…/content` and `DELETE` work as for task files. Every route finds the record inside the workspace (another workspace's, a missing or a soft-deleted record is a 400 on upload, a 404 elsewhere) and checks the record's module: `read` to list and download, `write` to attach (guests never), and to delete either the uploader with `write` or anyone with `manage`. A locked record answers `409 attachment_locked` to a delete, whoever asks, but still takes new files as evidence. Tokens act with their owner's module access and the usual scope: a write token can upload where the owner can write.
- **Sync.** Metadata is the `attachment` entity (with `entityType`, `entityId`, `kind`, never the storage key). `filterChangesByModule` keeps it only for readers of the record's module; tombstones carry no data and pass. Attachments are not in the bootstrap or search.
- **Data.** The `attachments` table has nullable `entity_type`, `entity_id` (no foreign key: it is polymorphic, so the server checks the record) and `kind`, with a check that at most one of task, document or record is set.
- **MCP.** One read-only `list_<module>_record_attachments` tool per module with targets: metadata and the lock state, no bytes. `get_attachment_link` (a platform tool) turns a listed file into a short-lived signed URL for the person to open; see "Download links" below.
- **Web.** `RecordAttachments` (`components/record-attachments.tsx`) is the panel: the record's detail page or dialog (complaints, non-conformances, purchase orders, supplier invoices, items, suppliers, glass breakage "Photos", HACCP corrective actions, a training record's "Files"). Records that are only a table row (audits, recall tests, deliveries, calibration and cleaning records) get a "Files" button (`RecordFilesButton`) that opens the panel in a dialog. It reuses `AttachmentRow`, `UploadRow` and `uploadAttachment`.
- **Invoices**: the supplier's invoice (PDF or scan) hangs on the invoice version it was attached to. A revision is a new record, so it starts with no files; the earlier version keeps its own and stays readable.
- **Audits, recall tests, calibration and cleaning**: audit files (checklist, report, photos) can be added while the audit is planned and stay once it is completed or cancelled. A mock recall test and a calibration record are append-only (a test is voided, a calibration never changes), so their files (trace sheets, calibration certificates) are locked from the start: they can be added, never removed. A cleaning record's files are locked once a second person verifies it.
- **Deliveries**: a delivery is several `purchase_receipt` rows sharing a `deliveryId`, and a file must hang on one row, so the delivery's files (delivery note, photos of the load) hang on its first receipt row (oldest `createdAt`, then `id`, the order the order page lists them in; the page puts the button on the delivery card). They are never locked: receipts are append-only and corrected by reversing on the lot, but a wrong photo may be deleted by its uploader or a manager. A file on one line only (a rejected item) is filed on the delivery too.
- **Delivery lines.** `purchase_receipt_line` is a second target on the same `purchase_receipts` table: photos (or documents) of one line of a delivery, filed on that line's own receipt row, apart from the delivery's files (`purchase_receipt`, on the first row). Never locked. The order page puts a "Photos" button on each receipt line.
- **Download links.** `GET /attachments/:id/link` (tokens allowed, read scope too) needs the same `read` as the bytes and answers `{ url, expiresAt, filename, contentType, size }`. The URL is `<APP_URL>/api/v1/public/attachment-links/<token>`: an HMAC-signed (key derived from the server secret) payload of attachment, workspace and person, valid for `ATTACHMENT_LINK_TTL_SECONDS` (5 minutes), nothing stored. Redeeming it needs no sign-in but re-checks everything a session would: signature and expiry, the person still an active member, still `read` on the file's module, the file and its record still live; any failure is one 404. Served like the content route (sandbox CSP, `nosniff`, download unless a verified image) with `Cache-Control: private, no-store`. Public routes share the unauthenticated rate limit.
- **Replacing a file.** `POST /attachments/:id/replace` (multipart `file`) adds the new file on the same record with the same kind and `replacesId` set to the old one. Unlocked record: the old file is deleted in the same transaction (needs the right to delete it: the uploader with `write`, or `manage`; otherwise `403`). Locked record, or an SOP image a version uses: both stay (`replaced: false`) and anyone who may attach may add the version. Only files on module records (400 otherwise). Answers `{ attachment, replaced, previousId }`. The web panel has a "Replace" button on each file.
- **Not built yet:** virus scanning.

## Printing and exports

Print-ready pages use the browser's print CSS, not a PDF library: choose "Save as PDF" in the print dialog for a file. They add no API routes. Each reads the data its module already exposes, so the module's `requireModule` layout and the API's module rules apply as for any page.

- **Shell**: `PrintPage` (`apps/web/src/components/print-page.tsx`) is the app header with a Print button (`window.print()`) around an A4 sheet. `@media print` rules in `packages/ui/src/styles/globals.css` hide the sidebar, header, assistant and toasts, set A4 with 15 mm margins, and print black on white whatever the theme. Tables repeat their header row across pages and rows don't split. Shown on `/design` under "Print & export".
- **Pages**:
  - Product label: `/<workspace>/labels/<id>/print` (labels). The label as it reads on pack (name, ingredients with allergens in bold, allergen statement, nutrition per 100 g, storage, date wording, net quantity), with the status underneath. Unless the label is approved and current it carries a boxed **DRAFT**, **NEEDS REVIEW** (approved, but the recipe or an ingredient changed) or **SUPERSEDED** notice and a watermark (`labelPrintState`).
  - Purchase order: `/<workspace>/purchasing/orders/<id>/print` (purchasing). Supplier (address and contact from the supplier register), delivery location and workspace name, reference, order, expected and line dates, lines with price and value, total, notes, and who raised and sent it. A draft or cancelled order is marked as such. Prices are in the workspace currency; there is no VAT setting.
  - HACCP monitoring record: `/<workspace>/haccp/plans/<id>/audit?from=&to=` (haccp). The plan's CCP checks in the range with reading, limit, batch, who checked, the review (approved, rejected, awaiting) and any corrective action opened, then the corrective actions that answer the plan's deviations (raised, closed or about a check in the range) with action, root cause, prevention and who closed them, then the cleaning and calibration records in the range (a corrected record stays, struck through, followed by its correction and the reason). It starts, when the plan has steps, with the process flow and hazard analysis of the version in force, and lists the supervisor sign-offs in the range and the days with checks that were not signed off. Those two are site-wide (areas and equipment are not tied to a plan), so every plan's print lists them: cleaning with who did it and who verified it, calibration with reference, reading, tolerance, result and the corrective action a failure opened.
  - Site records: `/<workspace>/site/records?from=&to=` (site; the "Audit records" tab). Storage readings, pre-start and changeover checks (each item's result, the sign-off), maintenance (who did it, the clearance and who gave it) and pest visits (findings, recommendations).
  - Quality records: `/<workspace>/quality/records?from=&to=` (quality; the "Audit records" tab). Internal audits (those completed in the range, and planned or cancelled ones planned in it) with their findings and sign-off, and mock recall tests conducted in the range (a voided one stays on the page, with who voided it and why).
  - Production batch record: `/<workspace>/production/batches/<id>/print` (production; the "Print record" button on the batch page). The recipe and version it was made to, planned against actual quantity, materials required against consumed, every lot consumed and produced with location, who and when (a reversed line stays, struck through), the produced lots' status and expiry, and sign-off lines (planned, started, signed off by). A planned, in-progress or cancelled batch is boxed as such. The **CCP checks** recorded against the batch are included only for people who can read `haccp`, and the **cost** (actual, per unit, yield, variance) only for people who can read `costing`.
  - Lot barcodes: `/<workspace>/stock/lots/print?ids=<lot ids>&format=&copies=` (stock; "Print barcode" on a lot, "Print lot barcodes" under a batch's produced lots). One block per lot and copy: item, lot code, expiry and a barcode, up to 40 lots and 20 copies. `format=code128` (default) encodes the lot code; `gs1-128` encodes FNC1, the expiry as AI 17 (use by) or AI 15 (best before) as YYMMDD, and the lot as AI 10 (last, so no separator is needed). A lot code GS1 can't carry (over 20 characters, or outside the GS1 set) falls back to Code 128 with a note. There is no GTIN (items have none), so no AI 01. **Rendering**: drawn as SVG by `LotBarcode` from `@knotworking/core` `barcode.ts` (the 107-pattern Code 128 table, subsets B and C, checksum; checked against the JsBarcode table). **No dependency was added**; the symbology is small enough to own. The production consumption scan field reads a Code 128 lot code as before, and also a GS1-128 scan (with or without `]C1`, group separators or brackets): if no lot has the scanned text as its code, `parseGs1` takes the AI 10 lot and looks that up.
- **Corrections on paper**: record prints list the records oldest first and keep every one: a record that a later one corrected is struck through and marked replaced, and the correction says what it replaced and why (it quotes the original, or notes that the original is before the start of the range). Ranges are inclusive days in the workspace's time zone, defaulting to the last 30 days up to today there; a form with plain GET parameters sets them, so a range can be bookmarked. A list of more than 2,000 records of one kind is cut short, and the page says so.
- **CSV**: built in the browser by `toCsv` from `@knotworking/core`, which quotes fields and prefixes a text cell starting `=`, `+`, `-`, `@`, a tab or a carriage return with a single quote (spreadsheet formula injection). The file starts with a byte order mark so Excel reads UTF-8, has CRLF line breaks, ISO dates and numbers as numbers (`apps/web/src/lib/csv-export.ts`, the `ExportCsvButton`). Complaints, purchase orders and batch costs fetch every page matching the list's filters when clicked; the HACCP, site and quality record pages export what the range holds (CCP checks, corrective actions, storage readings, checks, maintenance and pest visits). Attendance payroll, the time report and demand lines already had CSV, from the server. Lists without one: stock lots, non-conformances, SOPs, people and training.

## Assets module

One register of the business's areas, equipment, vehicles, IT, tools and other assets, usable by any business. Code: `packages/core/src/assets.ts`, `packages/db/src/schema/assets.ts`, `apps/server/src/{services,routes}/assets.ts` (other modules use `services/ports/assets.ts`), `apps/mcp/src/tools/assets.ts`, and the web pages `/<workspace>/assets` (the register, filtered by kind, area, status and text) and `/<workspace>/assets/<id>` (one asset; for an area, what is in it). Needs nothing. `defaultAccess: "none"` like the other operational modules: admins grant it.

- **An asset** (`assets`): a kind (`area`, `equipment`, `vehicle`, `it`, `tool`, `other`), an optional code (unique, case-insensitive, among the workspace's live assets: `409 code_taken`), a name, the area it is in (`parent_area_id`, an asset of kind `area`; an area can sit in another area, never in itself or one of its own areas), a free-text location, manufacturer, model, serial number, purchase date and cost (`numeric`, the workspace currency), warranty expiry, the member who looks after it (`custodian_id`) and notes. `revision` goes up on every change; `PATCH /assets/:id` may send `expectedRevision` and is refused with `409 asset_changed` when someone changed it since.
- **An area stays an area.** Other modules point at areas (where a pest device, glass item, storage unit or checklist is; an asset's area), so an asset can't change between `area` and another kind (`409 kind_fixed`); other kinds can change among themselves. Equipment with linked cleaning or calibration schedules, history or instrument references stays equipment.
- **Archived, never deleted.** `POST /assets/:id/archive` and `POST /assets/:id/restore` need `manage`. Archived assets keep their history; HACCP and Site record nothing new against them.
- **Routes**: `GET /assets` (`q` over name, code, serial, manufacturer and model; `kind`; `areaId`; `status` `active` (default), `archived` or `all`; `limit` up to 500 and `offset`; returns `total`), `GET /assets/:id` (with `assetsInside` for an area), `POST /assets`, `PATCH /assets/:id`, and the archive and restore actions. Reading needs `read`, adding and changing `write`. API tokens may keep the register (no sign-offs here).
- **Extensions.** Modules that need more about an asset keep it in their own table keyed by the asset's id: HACCP's cleaning and calibration schedule (`haccp_assets`) and Site's calibration instrument link (`site_assets`). Cleaning and calibration records, Site's areas and maintenance, production devices (`haccp_asset_id`) and HSE permits point at `assets`.
- **Sync and leaks.** The sync log carries `asset` with metadata only (kind, code, name, status, area, revision); cost, serial number, custodian and notes are read through the endpoints. Assets are not in search, notifications or attachments (HACCP's cleaning and calibration notifications are HACCP's and hidden from members who can't read it).
- **MCP**: `list_assets` and `get_asset` (resources), `save_asset` (add or change; an area by name or code). No archive tool.
- **Migration** (`0097_assets`). HACCP assets keep their IDs and codes. Maintained assets become equipment in their existing areas. A Site asset sharing an ID with a HACCP asset receives a new ID and its live maintenance and permit references follow it. Codes that collide get the first available maintenance suffix, within the 40-character limit. The old tables stay as extension tables (their generic columns dropped). Members with a grant in `haccp` or `site` got the same level (the higher of the two) in `assets`, and a workspace module selection, API token allowlist or assistant module list naming `haccp` or `site` names `assets` too. An installation that sets `KNOTWORKING_MODULES` explicitly must add `assets` beside `haccp` or `site` (the API refuses to start otherwise).

Not built yet: files on an asset (manuals, warranty documents), warranty expiry reminders, depreciation, check-out of tools and vehicles, and merging two assets that were the same thing in both old registers.

## Items module

Master data the other manufacturing modules build on. Code: `packages/core/src/items.ts`, `packages/db/src/schema/items.ts`, `apps/server/src/{services,routes}/items.ts`, `apps/mcp/src/tools/items.ts`, and the web pages `/<workspace>/items` and `/<workspace>/suppliers`.

- **Items** have a unique stock code, a type (ingredient, packaging, work in progress, finished good), a unit, allergens (contains and may contain, from the 14 declarable in the UK), storage condition, shelf life, a standard cost per unit and their suppliers. Retire an item by archiving it. An item or supplier that other records point at can't be deleted (`409 item_in_use`, `409 supplier_in_use`), only archived: stock lots and movements, recipes, demand lines, production batches and records, purchase orders, receipts and invoices, complaints and non-conformances.
- **Units**: mg, g, kg (weight), ml, l (volume), each (count). Other modules store quantities in the base unit of the item's kind (g, ml, each), so an item's unit can change within its kind only. Optional density (g/ml) and weight each (g) let `convertQuantity` cross kinds; without them it returns `null` rather than guess.
- **Suppliers** carry contact details, certification and certificate expiry, and an approval status (pending, approved, suspended, rejected) with a review date. An approval decision needs `manage`, must be made by a signed-in person (API tokens are refused, and there is no MCP tool) and records who decided and when.
- **Reading**: `GET /items` (filters `q`, `type`, `status`, `allergen`, `supplierId`; `limit`/`offset`, with `total`) and `GET /suppliers`. Items are not in the bootstrap; writes still go to the sync log (`item`, `supplier`, `item_supplier`), filtered by module access.

- **CSV import** (`POST /items/import` and `POST /suppliers/import`, `write`; API tokens allowed, as for the demand import): CSV text (comma, semicolon or tab separated), parsed in core by `parseItemsCsv` / `parseSuppliersCsv` (shared helpers in `csv-import.ts`), so the web app and agents share the rules. A header row names the columns in any order. Up to 5000 rows. `dryRun: true` checks every row and returns the problems by spreadsheet row and column, and a preview of what would be created, updated or left unchanged (with the fields an update changes); nothing is written. Without it the import writes every row in one transaction, through `recordChanges`, or answers `400 invalid_rows` and writes nothing. The same code or supplier twice in a file is an error.
  - **Items** are matched by stock code, ignoring case (the stored code's case is kept). `code` is required; a new item also needs `name`, `type` and `unit`; optional `description`, `density` (g/ml), `unit weight` (g), `allergens` (contains) and `may contain` (names such as `milk; eggs`, or `none`), `storage`, `shelf life` (days), `standard cost` (a leading £ is dropped) and `status`. A missing column leaves existing items alone; an empty cell clears the value (an empty allergen cell means none), except `name`, `type`, `unit`, `storage` and `status`, which keep it. An existing item's unit can't change dimension (as in the app). Supplier links are not imported.
  - **Suppliers** are matched by `code`, else by `name` (ignoring case); a name-matched supplier with no code gets the row's code. `code` and/or `name` columns are needed (a new supplier needs a name); optional `contact name`, `email`, `phone`, `address`, `notes`, `certification`, `certificate expiry` (`2027-03-31` or `31/03/2027`, day first). A name shared by several suppliers, or a code row whose name belongs to a supplier with another code, is an error. **Approval is not importable**: new suppliers start `pending`, existing approvals are untouched, and an `approval`/`status` column is ignored and reported.
- **MCP**: `import_items_csv`, `import_suppliers_csv` (with `dry_run`).

- **Currency and dates**: every cost and price is shown in the workspace currency (`workspaces.currency`, an ISO 4217 code, `GBP` by default; owners and admins change it under Workspace settings, `PATCH …/settings`, refused to tokens; it is on `GET …/settings` and the bootstrap's `workspace`). Amounts are plain `numeric` everywhere: the currency only decides how they are written, so changing it converts nothing. One formatter writes it: `formatMoney` (`@knotworking/core` `currency.ts`, wrapped by `apps/web/src/lib/format.ts`), with `useWorkspaceCurrency()` in client components and `getWorkspaceCurrency(slug)` in server ones. Dates are UK everywhere a person reads one: `08/11/2026` (`formatLongDate`, `printDate`; `formatShortDate` is `08/11` where the year is clear) and 24-hour times `12:41` with the zone's name (`formatDateTime`, `printDateTime`), all from `formatUkDate`/`formatUkDateTime` in core `format-date.ts`, in the workspace's time zone. CSV exports and the API keep ISO dates. Weekday headings on the production schedule (`Mon 9 Nov`) keep their wording.

### Supplier controls

Risk assessments, required controls, questionnaires and service providers beside supplier approval (Phase 3, milestone 3.2.4). Code: `packages/core/src/supplier-controls.ts` (`supplierCompliance`), `apps/server/src/services/supplier-controls.ts`, `apps/mcp/src/tools/supplier-controls.ts`, and the web page `/<workspace>/suppliers/<id>`.

- **Category**: a supplier is `goods` or a `service` provider (pest control, laboratory, cleaning, transport), with the service it provides. The list filters by `category`.
- **Risk assessments** (`supplier_risk_assessments`): for one item the supplier provides, or for the supplier as a whole (a service provider is only ever assessed as a whole). Each records the risk level, hazards (microbiological, allergen, physical, chemical, authenticity, other), the rationale, the controls required (valid certification, an accepted questionnaire, an approved specification, an audit, a certificate of analysis with each delivery, testing on receipt) and a future review date. Append-only: a new assessment of the same supplier and item supersedes the current one (one current per pair, by partial unique index), which is kept as history. Recording one needs `manage` and a person ("Assess supplier risk").
- **Questionnaires** (`supplier_questionnaires`): sent, then received, then accepted (with the date it stands until) or rejected (with a reason). Logging and marking received is ordinary write work (tokens too); accepting or rejecting needs `manage` and a person ("Accept or reject supplier questionnaires"). Files hang on the questionnaire (`supplier_questionnaire`), locked once reviewed.
- **Compliance** (`supplierCompliance`): each required control is judged from the evidence. Certification needs a recorded certificate in date (expiring within 30 days is a warning). A questionnaire needs an accepted one in date. A specification needs an approved one agreed with this supplier for the item, or for every item listed when assessed as a whole; it is `not_tracked` when Specifications is off. Audits are `not_tracked` (attach the reports); certificates of analysis and receipt testing apply `at_goods_in`. None of those is ever reported as met. Issues: no assessment, items bought without one, assessments due or overdue for review, controls missing or expiring, the approval review overdue, and questionnaires not back after 30 days. `GET /suppliers/compliance` lists every supplier with its highest risk level and issues; `GET /suppliers/:id/controls` gives one supplier's current assessments with control states, history and questionnaires.
- **Sync**: `supplier_risk_assessment` and `supplier_questionnaire` rows are Items reference data in the sync log.
- **MCP**: `list_supplier_compliance`, `get_supplier_controls`, `log_supplier_questionnaire`. There are no tools for assessments or questionnaire reviews.
- **Enforcement** (`supplier_control_settings`, `GET`/`PUT /suppliers/control-settings`; changing it needs Items `manage` and a person, "Change supplier control enforcement"): `report` (the default) only shows open issues. `block` makes issues needing action (`blockingSupplierIssues`; an overdue approval review doesn't block the approval that renews it) refuse approving a supplier and sending it a purchase order (`409 supplier_controls_open`, with the issues), unless the request carries `controlsOverride` with a reason. The override and the issues it overrode are recorded on the supplier (`approvalControlsOverride`, cleared by the next decision) or the order (`sendControlsOverride`). Only a signed-in person with Items `manage` can override (a food-safety judgement, not a buyer's): a buyer with only Purchasing `manage`, or an API token sending an order where the workspace allows `purchasing.send`, gets `403 override_not_allowed`. Suspending or rejecting is never blocked. The setting syncs as `supplier_control_settings`. Web: the setting on the suppliers page; the approval and send dialogs list the issues and ask for the reason; supplier and order pages show any override.
- **Not built yet**: goods-in prompts for certificates of analysis; audit records beyond attachments; notifications for due reviews and expiring certificates; questionnaire templates.

## Recipes module

Versioned bills of materials on top of items. Code: `packages/core/src/recipes.ts`, `packages/db/src/schema/recipes.ts`, `apps/server/src/{services,routes}/recipes.ts`, `apps/mcp/src/tools/recipes.ts`, and the web pages under `/<workspace>/recipes`. Needs `items`.

- **Recipes** have a unique code (case-insensitive among live recipes), a name and the work-in-progress or finished item one batch makes. Retire one by archiving it; a recipe that has ever had an approved version can't be deleted.
- **Versions** are document-controlled: `draft`, `approved`, `superseded`. A recipe has at most one draft (the only editable version) and at most one approved version, which the database enforces. Saving the draft replaces its yield, method, change note and whole ingredient list in one transaction, or starts the next version number if there is no draft (the client sends the full content; copying from the current version is the client's job). Approved and superseded versions never change and are kept.
- **Lines** name an item, a quantity (4 decimal places) and a unit that converts to the item's own unit with `convertQuantity` (same kind, or the item has a density or unit weight). A line can't be the recipe's own output item. Items must be live; archived items are allowed on existing versions but refused on a new draft save and at approval. The yield must be in the same kind of unit as the output item.
- **Approval** (`POST /recipes/:id/draft/approve`) makes the draft `approved`, supersedes the previously approved version and records who and when. It needs `manage` and a signed-in person (API tokens are refused, and there is no MCP tool). A draft with no lines or a zero yield can't be approved.
- **Roll-ups** are computed on read (the one stored value is the allergen roll-up recorded at approval, below): allergens (contains is the union of the ingredients'; may contain excludes what is contained), `allergenMismatch` against the output item's declaration (`undeclared`: brought in but not declared; `unexplained`: declared but in no ingredient), cost per batch and per output unit (partial with `costComplete: false` and the item codes lacking a cost), and scaling by batches or by a target output (unrounded; the UI rounds to 4 places for display).
- **Reading**: `GET /recipes` (filters `q`, `status`, `outputItemId`, `itemId` = recipes whose approved or draft version uses that ingredient; `limit`/`offset`, with `total`), `GET /recipes/:id` (header, versions, current version in full, and the draft beside an approved one) and `GET /recipes/:id/versions/:versionId` (optionally `?batches=` or `?outputQuantity=&outputUnit=`). Recipes are not in the bootstrap; writes go to the sync log (`recipe`, `recipe_version`, `recipe_line`), filtered by module access.
- **Writing**: `POST /recipes`, `PATCH /recipes/:id` (code, name, description, status), `PUT /recipes/:id/draft`, `DELETE /recipes/:id/draft`, `DELETE /recipes/:id` (soft delete, refused once approved). Archived recipes can't be drafted or approved until made active again.

- **Starting from an older version**: the API already takes the full content, so copying is the client's job. On the recipe page, a superseded version (when there is no draft) has "New draft from this", which opens the editor with that version's yield, method and lines; saving makes it the next version number. Archived items in the copy must be replaced before saving.
- **Comparing versions**: `diffRecipeVersions` in `@knotworking/core` compares two versions (lines added, removed and changed by item; yield; method; allergen roll-up) and the "Compare versions" card on the recipe page uses it. Quantities are compared as entered, without unit conversion.
- **Allergen re-check**: approval stores the ingredients' allergen roll-up on the version (`recipe_versions.approved_allergens`; the migration backfilled existing released versions from today's declarations). On read, an approved version whose roll-up from the ingredients' current declarations no longer matches that record carries `allergenDrift` (`contains` / `mayContain`, each with `added` and `removed`), and list rows carry `allergenDrift: true`. The recipe page shows a warning and the list an "Allergens changed" badge; MCP `get_recipe` reports it as `allergenRecheck`. Approving a new version (a fresh draft, or a copy of the current one, saved and approved) records the new roll-up and clears the flag. Drafts and superseded versions are not flagged. This is separate from `allergenMismatch`, which compares the roll-up with the output item.

- **Flagged recipes**: `GET /recipes?allergenDrift=true` lists only the recipes whose approved version is flagged (`false`: the others). The flagged set is worked out before paging and applied in the query (`id in (…)`), so `total`, `limit` and `offset` count only matching recipes; it is the same computation as the badge (`recipesWithAllergenDrift`). The list page has an "Allergens changed" toggle (`?drift=1`) and MCP `list_recipes` takes `allergen_drift`.
- **Notification**: the sweep sends `recipe_allergen_drift` to members with `manage` on recipes for each active recipe whose approved version is flagged, deduped per version and drift (`recipe_drift:<versionId>:<what changed>`): the same drift never notifies twice, a further change to the ingredients' declarations is a new drift and does, and approving a new version clears the flag. The Inbox entry links to the recipe and says what moved (`contains +soybeans; may contain +nuts`). Archived recipes are not swept. See [notifications](notifications.md).

Which production batches used which version is recorded by the production module (`GET /production/batches?recipeVersionId=`).

## SOPs module

Standard operating procedures under document control (BRCGS). Code: `packages/core/src/sops.ts`, `packages/db/src/schema/sops.ts`, `apps/server/src/{services,routes}/sops.ts`, `apps/mcp/src/tools/sops.ts`, and the web pages `/<workspace>/sops` and `/<workspace>/sops/<id>`. They reuse the rich-text editor and the `DocContent` format of the wiki, but an SOP is its own record, not a row in `documents`: there is no free editing and no throttled autosave history.

- **Shape**: `sops` (code such as `SOP-001`, unique among live SOPs case-insensitively; title; category; owner; review interval in months, default 12; the working draft; active or archived), `sop_versions` (immutable snapshots numbered 1, 2, … per SOP, each with a required change summary) and `sop_acknowledgements` (one per person per version) and `sop_copies` (the controlled-copy register, below).
- **Draft**: anyone with `write` edits the draft (`PUT /sops/:id/draft`). Send `expectedDraftUpdatedAt`; if someone saved in between the answer is `409 draft_conflict` with the current draft. Images are uploaded to the SOP (see Images).
- **Submit** (`write`): snapshots the draft as the next version, `in_review`, with a change summary. Refused while another version is in review, when the draft is empty, or when it is identical to the current approved version. The draft is kept.
- **Decide** (`manage`, signed-in person, API tokens refused, no MCP tool): _approve_ records who and when, the effective date (default today, may be in the future) and the review date (default effective date plus the review interval), marks the previous approved version `superseded`, takes the version's title as the SOP's title and clears the draft. _Reject_ needs a note and leaves the draft for rework. Four-eyes: whoever submitted a version may not approve it unless they are the only member with `manage` (`canApproveSopVersion`); withdrawing your own submission by rejecting it is allowed.
- **Current version**: the one with status `approved` (a future effective date is shown but does not delay it). Superseded and rejected versions are kept and readable; nothing edits a version after it is created. An SOP that has ever had an approved version can only be archived, not deleted.
- **Who sees what**: `read` sees the current version and the history. The draft and the content of a version in review need `write`; read-only members only learn that a version is in review.
- **Acknowledgement**: any member with at least `read` can record "read and understood" once for the current approved version (`POST /sops/:id/acknowledge`; a person's attestation, so API tokens are refused; this is the one write that needs only `read`, see `READ_LEVEL_WRITES` in `middleware/modules.ts`). A new approved version needs fresh acknowledgements. `manage` lists who has and has not acknowledged a version.
- **Review due**: `GET /sops?due=overdue|soon` filters by the current version's review date (soon: within 30 days). Rows also say whether the caller has acknowledged the current version.
- **Sync**: writes go to the sync log as `sop`, `sop_version`, `sop_acknowledgement`, `sop_target` and `sop_copy` with metadata only; draft text and version content never appear there, and SOPs are not in the bootstrap.
- **MCP**: `list_sops`, `get_sop`, `create_sop`, `update_sop_draft` (plain text), `submit_sop_for_review`. Agents draft and propose; approval and acknowledgement are people's acts.

- **Notifications** (see [notifications](notifications.md)): submitting a version tells everyone who can approve it (`manage`, minus the submitter, who may not approve their own version unless sole manager) with `sop_review_requested`; approving one tells the people who must acknowledge it (the targeted readers when targets are set, otherwise every member with `read`), except the approver, with `sop_version_approved`. Both are created in the write transaction, emailed after commit and subject to per-type preferences and module access.
- **Training**: acknowledging counts as training for a linked SOP course, see [Training module](#training-module).

- **Images**: the editor's image button, paste and drop work in an SOP draft. An image is an attachment on the SOP (`entityType` `sop`, kind `photo`; see Attachments), served under `read` on SOPs, never the wiki's. A draft may only use images uploaded to that SOP (`400` otherwise: pasting from a page won't work). A file the draft or any version (approved, superseded, rejected or in review) uses can't be deleted (`409 attachment_locked`), so an approved version's images stay as they were approved; an unused upload can be removed by its uploader or a manager. Bytes are never replaced.
- **Targeted acknowledgement**: `PUT /sops/:id/targets` (`manage`) sets job roles and/or named members (`GET` reads them); the `sop_target` rows are workspace data in the sync log. A job role reaches the members whose active staff record (linked to their login) holds it, so roles need `people`; people without a login can't acknowledge in the app. With no targets everyone with `read` must acknowledge, as before. Anyone with `read` may still acknowledge a non-targeted SOP; rows and the SOP page say whether it is `required` of the viewer. A job role an SOP targets can't be deleted (archive it). `GET /sops/outstanding?sopId&userId&minDays` (`manage`) lists each person still to acknowledge each current version, with `daysOutstanding` counted from the later of the version's effective date and approval date, in the workspace's time zone; the web page is `/<workspace>/sops/outstanding` (by person) and the SOP's Acknowledgements tab (per SOP, with the targets editor). Changing targets notifies the people it newly includes (see below).
- **Retraining reminders**: each SOP has `ackReminderDays` (default 14, "Remind after" in Details). The notification sweep sends `sop_ack_reminder` to each person still outstanding that many days after the version took effect, and one `sop_ack_overdue` per version, with the count, to everyone with `manage` on SOPs (the people module has no reporting lines, so "their manager" is the SOP managers). Deduped per version (`sop_ack:<versionId>`, `sop_ack_overdue:<versionId>`), within the usual look-back.
- **Reminder repeats**: an SOP may also set `ackReminderRepeatDays` ("Then remind every", default empty: no repeat). A person still outstanding is then reminded again every that many days after the first reminder. Reminders are worked out in rounds (`sopReminderRound`: round 0 at `ackReminderDays`, round `n` at `ackReminderDays + n × repeat`), and the sweep only ever sends the **latest** round: one reminder per round, keyed `sop_ack:<versionId>` for round 0 and `sop_ack:<versionId>:r<n>` for repeats, so a sweep that runs often creates nothing twice and one that runs late, or after the setting was changed, sends one reminder rather than a catch-up burst. A round older than the look-back is skipped, like any other due date. The notification carries `reminderRound` and the Inbox says "reminder 2". The managers' `sop_ack_overdue` stays once per version. Acknowledging, a new version or archiving the SOP ends the repeats (the outstanding list is what is swept).
- **New readers**: saving targets that newly include someone (`PUT /sops/:id/targets`) sends them `sop_targeted` ("SOP added to your reading") in the same transaction, once per person and version (`sop_targeted:<versionId>`; removing and re-adding someone for the same version does not notify again). Decisions: it applies only to an active SOP that has a current approved version (otherwise they hear on approval, `sop_version_approved`); it skips people who have already acknowledged that version, the person making the change, and people without `read` on SOPs; going from "everyone" to named people includes nobody new, and clearing the targets includes everyone who was not yet targeted. A person who gains a targeted job role through the people module (not a targets change) is not notified; the reminder sweep still reaches them.
- **Controlled copies** (`sop_copies`, `GET /sops/copies`, `GET /sops/copies/:copyId`, `POST /sops/:id/copies`, `POST /sops/copies/:copyId/close`): the register of printed copies, for BRCGS document control. Issuing a copy (`manage`, a person: API tokens are refused, an attestation like acknowledging) takes the current approved version of an active SOP (or `versionId` of that version; a superseded one is `409 version_not_current`), is numbered per version (copy 1, 2, … of v3, taken under the workspace lock) and records who it was issued to (a member and/or a name or place), where it is kept, and who issued it and when. A copy is then `issued` until a manager records it `returned` or `destroyed` (with a note), once (`409 copy_closed`); nothing is edited or deleted. A copy still `issued` whose version is no longer the current approved one is `outOfDate`: it is to be called in, and the SOP's tab and the register flag it. Copies are workspace data in the sync log (`sop_copy`, metadata only). Reading the register needs `read`. Web: "Issue a copy" (Controlled copies tab of the SOP, `manage`) records the copy and opens `/<workspace>/sops/<id>/print?copy=<id>`, the print with a boxed "Controlled copy no. N, issued to …" notice in place of "Uncontrolled when printed"; `/<workspace>/sops/copies?status=issued|returned|destroyed|all` is the print-friendly register ("Controlled copies" on the SOP list). A copy made by simply printing the SOP without issuing it stays "uncontrolled when printed".
- **PDF export**: `/<workspace>/sops/<id>/print` (`PrintPage`, no PDF library; "Save as PDF" in the print dialog) prints the current approved version, or `?version=<id>` for another (boxed as not current): title, code, version, owner, effective and review dates, change summary, content with images, submitter and approver, and "Uncontrolled when printed".
- **MCP**: `list_outstanding_sop_reading` (read-only, needs `manage`) and `list_sop_copies` (read-only register; there is no tool to issue or call in a copy).

Not built yet: nothing from the first list. Left out on purpose: notifying people who gain a targeted job role through the people module, and an automatic call-in notice when a new version makes copies out of date (the register and SOP page flag them).

## Stock module

Lots, locations and the stock ledger, for traceability (BRCGS) and first-expired-first-out use. Code: `packages/core/src/stock.ts`, `packages/db/src/schema/stock.ts`, `apps/server/src/{services,routes}/stock.ts`, `apps/mcp/src/tools/stock.ts`, and the web pages `/<workspace>/stock`, `/<workspace>/stock/lots/<id>` and `/<workspace>/stock/locations`.

- **Locations** have a name, an optional unique code and a storage condition (ambient, chilled, frozen). A location is archived, not deleted, and only once it holds no stock; nothing can be put into an archived location.
- **Lots** belong to one item and have a lot code (unique per item; generated as `L` + yymmdd + a sequence when not given), the supplier and the supplier's own lot code (one step back), received date, expiry date with its type (use-by is safety, best-before is quality) and a QA status: available, quarantine or rejected. Without an expiry, an item with a shelf life gets a best-before date of received date + shelf life.
- **The ledger** (`stock_movements`) is append-only: no route updates or deletes a movement. Each row names a lot and a location and holds a signed quantity in the item's base unit (g, ml or each), rounded to 4 decimal places where it enters the API. Types: receipt, transfer out / in (a pair sharing a `transferId`), adjustment, waste, stocktake, and consumption / production (posted only by production batch records, which also correct them; the stock reversal route refuses them). A correction is a reversal: a new row with the opposite quantity and `reversesMovementId`; a movement is reversed once, and both rows of a transfer are reversed together.
- **Balances** are the sum of a lot's movements at a location. `stock_balances` holds that sum, kept by the `stock_movements_balance` trigger in the statement that inserts a movement, so every write path stays in step and reads (levels, lots, locations, valuation, pick suggestions, demand and scheduling stock) follow the stock held, not the length of the ledger. The ledger is the truth: `reconcileStockBalances` (`@knotworking/db`) compares the two. Never write `stock_balances` from application code. Every write that checks a balance takes the workspace lock and re-reads it inside the transaction; a lot's balance at a location can't go negative (`409 insufficient_stock`, with what is available).
- **Quantities** are entered as a number and a unit and converted with the item's units (`convertQuantity`; density or weight each to cross kinds, `400 unit_not_convertible` otherwise). Responses give `{ base, baseUnit, display, displayUnit }`.
- **Goods-in** (`POST /stock/receipts`, `write`) creates the lot and its receipt in one transaction. Ingredients and packaging only; other items need `manage` and a reason (opening balances). Supplier approval applies: suspended or rejected suppliers are refused (`409 supplier_not_approved`); a pending supplier can deliver into quarantine only.
- **Who may do what**: reading, goods-in and transfers need `write` (API tokens allowed, so agents can record deliveries). Waste (`write`), adjustments (`manage`) and stocktakes (`POST /stock/stocktakes`, `manage`: counts per lot and location; a `stocktake` movement for each difference, sharing a reference) are a signed-in person's, and API tokens are refused unless an owner or admin allows `stock.waste`, `stock.adjust` or `stock.stocktake` under Settings → API access; then the MCP tools `record_stock_waste`, `adjust_stock` and `post_stocktake` appear and the token's owner still needs the level shown. Lot status changes (a QA release is a sign-off; records who and when) and reversals need `manage` and a signed-in person, and tokens are never allowed. Transfers are allowed whatever a lot's status.
- **FEFO**: `pickFefo` in core, exposed as `GET /stock/items/:itemId/pick?quantity=&unit=`, suggests lots in order of earliest expiry (no expiry last, then received date): available lots only, never past a use-by date; past best-before stays pickable but is flagged. It reserves nothing: stock set aside for batches is the production module's [reservations](#production-module), and the stock page shows it (Reserved and Free columns) to people who can read production.
- **Reading**: `GET /stock/levels` (one row per item: on hand split by status, lots, earliest expiry; filters `q`, `itemType`, `locationId`, `expiringWithinDays`, `includeZero`; paged), `GET /stock/lots` (FEFO order with balances per location, or latest received first with `order=newest` for pickers of past lots; filters `itemId`, `status`, `locationId`, `expiringWithinDays`, `q`, `includeEmpty`), `GET /stock/lots/:id` (the traceability view: lot, supplier, balances, every movement with who posted it), `GET /stock/movements` (filters `itemId`, `lotId`, `type`, `from`, `to`; newest first; paged), `GET /stock/locations`. Stock is not in the bootstrap; writes go to the sync log (`stock_location`, `stock_lot`, `stock_movement`), filtered by module access.

Not covered: reservations are the production module's (a stock pick is only a suggestion), purchasing and purchase orders (goods-in is the only way in), printing lot barcodes lives under [Printing and exports](#printing-and-exports), scales, a stocktake screen in the web app (stocktakes are API-only for now), and stock valuation (the `costing` module values the lots a batch consumed, and a single lot at `GET /costing/lots/:id`, but there is no valuation report of stock on hand). Consumption and production movements come from the production module's batch records.

### Evidence-based QA release

Releasing a lot (stock `POST /stock/lots/:id/status` to `available` from quarantine or rejected) is checked against the evidence other modules hold (Phase 3, milestone 3.2.3). Code: `packages/core/src/lot-release.ts` (`evaluateLotRelease`), `apps/server/src/services/lot-release.ts` (gathering the facts, rules, release records), and the lot page and `/<workspace>/stock/release-rules` on the web.

- **Always** (when Testing is active): no sample on the lot awaiting results; no failed sample with an open investigation; no failed sample left unresolved. A failure is resolved once its investigation is closed and a later complete, passing sample covers the failed analytes (a retest).
- **By rule**: `lot_release_rules` holds one rule per item, versioned (every change bumps `version`). It can require a complete passing sample covering named analytes; an approved specification for the item (the lot supplier's, else the internal one, else any); or, for a lot made in production, every current CCP log of the batches whose output it is approved, with no corrective action open on those batches or logs. A rule needing a switched-off module blocks rather than passing silently.
- **For the record, never blocking**: CCP readings that were out of limits during the batch, and packing checks (not recorded until 3.5). Harmless deviations don't block.
- **Enforcement**: the check runs inside the status change, under the workspace lock, whatever the person's own module access; a hidden blocker is still a blocker. A blocked release is refused (`409 release_blocked` with the checks). Every release is appended to `lot_releases`: who, when, the status it left, the note, the rule and its version, the checks as they stood, and any exception.
- **Release exceptions (two people)**: a blocked lot is released only when two people with stock `manage` agree (`apps/server/src/services/lot-release-exceptions.ts`). One requests it (`POST /stock/lots/:id/release-exceptions`: release note, reason, evidence), which keeps the checks and rule version it was asked against in `lot_release_exception_requests` (one pending per lot; refused with `409 exception_not_needed` when nothing blocks). Another approves it (`POST …/:requestId/approve`), which re-runs the check and releases the lot; the requester can approve only when nobody else has stock `manage` (`canApproveReleaseException`, `403 four_eyes` otherwise), and the release records `exceptionSelfApproved`. An approval is refused and the request closed as `superseded` (`409 exception_stale`) if anything blocks that the request wasn't shown, down to the samples and records each blocker names (a second failed test is a new blocker even though the kind is the same), the rule version changed, the lot's status changed since the request (the request keeps the lot's status and when it last changed), or nothing blocks any more (`exceptionCovers`). Any lot status change (`POST /stock/lots/:id/status`) also closes a pending request as `superseded`. `POST …/:requestId/reject` with a reason rejects it (another manager) or withdraws it (the requester). The release keeps the request id, who asked and who approved. All are person-only (tokens refused) and listed under "Release, quarantine or reject lots"; `GET /stock/lots/:id/release-exceptions` lists requests with the checks filtered for the reader. Requests aren't synced; each change records the lot so clients refresh. The lot page shows a pending request with Approve and Reject.
- **Reading**: `GET /stock/lots/:id/release-check` gives the checks as they stand; `GET /stock/lots/:id/releases` gives the history. References (sample references, the specification) are left out of checks whose module the reader can't read, marked `hidden`. `GET /stock/release-rules` lists rules; `PUT`/`DELETE /stock/release-rules/:itemId` need `manage` and a person ("Change lot release rules"). Rules sync as `lot_release_rule`; releases are read through endpoints. MCP: `get_lot_qa_evidence` (read only; releasing has no tool).
- **Not built yet**: per-product rule templates beyond these requirements, packing-check evidence (milestone 3.5, with packing checks themselves), and automatic release when every check passes (release stays a person's decision).

## Production module

Batch planning and batch records on top of recipes and stock. Code: `packages/core/src/production.ts`, `packages/db/src/schema/production.ts`, `apps/server/src/{services,routes}/production.ts`, `apps/mcp/src/tools/production.ts`, and the web pages `/<workspace>/production` (the week's schedule), `/<workspace>/production/batches/<id>`, `/<workspace>/production/requirements` and `/<workspace>/production/plan` (planning from demand). Needs `recipes`, `stock` and `demand` (and through them `items`).

- **Batches** have a code (unique among live batches, case-insensitively; generated as `B` + yymmdd of the planned date + a sequence), a recipe, the recipe's **approved version at planning time** (`recipe_version_id`, so `GET /production/batches?recipeVersionId=` answers "which batches used which version"), a planned date, an optional output location, notes, and a size. The size is entered as a number of batches or a target output (any unit that converts for the output item) and stored once, as the planned output in the output item's base unit; the number of batches is derived from the version's yield with the recipes module's `scaleFactor`. A recipe without an approved version can't be planned (`409 no_approved_version`).
- **Status**: `planned` → `in_progress` → `completed`, and `planned` or `in_progress` → `cancelled` (`canTransitionBatch`). While planned, a batch can be rescheduled, resized, recoded or moved to the recipe's current approved version (`PATCH`, `recipeVersionId`); it can't start on a version that has since been superseded (`409 version_superseded`), so a person consciously adopts the new version. Output location and notes can change until the batch closes.
- **Batch records** (`production_records`) are append-only. Recording consumption (`POST /production/batches/:id/consumption`, several lot and location lines at once) posts a `consumption` movement out of each lot; recording output (`POST …/output`) posts a `production` movement into the produced lot. Each record names the stock movement it posted. Consumption is limited to ingredients of the batch's version (`409 not_in_recipe`), released lots (`409 lot_not_available`) not past a use-by date (`409 lot_expired`), and what is at the location. Output creates a lot of the recipe's output item in **quarantine** (QA releases it from the batch page or the stock lot page: both use the stock module's status route, so the same sign-off rules apply), with the batch code as lot code (or `-2`, `-3` … when taken, or a code given), today as its date and, without an expiry, the output item's shelf life as a best-before date; more output can be added to a lot the batch produced (`lotId`) while it is still in quarantine: once QA has released or rejected it, further output goes into a new lot (`409 lot_released` / `lot_rejected`), so QA's decision covers everything in the lot.
- **The stock ledger**: production posts through the stock service's own primitives (`createLot`, `toBase`, `assertAvailable`, `insertMovements`, `pickCandidates`), so the ledger rules are the same: base units rounded to 4 places, the workspace lock, balances re-read inside the transaction, no negative balance, append-only rows. The stock module's reversal route refuses consumption and production movements (`409 production_movement`): they are corrected from the batch.
- **Corrections** (`POST …/records/:recordId/reverse`, with a reason) append a reversal record and a reversing stock movement (`reversesMovementId`); a record is reversed once, and reversing output can't take back more than is left of the produced lot. A signed-in person only; `write` on an in-progress batch, `manage` once it is completed or cancelled.
- **Sign-off**: completing a batch (`POST …/complete`, optional note) needs `manage` and a signed-in person (API tokens are refused, whatever the API access setting, and there is no MCP tool), needs output on record, and records who and when. The record is then closed: no more consumption or output. Cancelling (`POST …/cancel`, with a reason) is a person's act unless an owner or admin allows `production.cancel` under Settings → API access, which gives tokens the `cancel_production_batch` MCP tool: `write` for a planned batch, `manage` for a started one, which must have no output left on record; ingredients already consumed stay consumed.
- **FEFO**: `GET /production/batches/:id/pick` suggests lots for what each ingredient still needs (`pickFefo`); the web app prefills the consumption form with it. It reserves nothing itself. It offers the lots the batch has reserved first (up to what is outstanding), then first expired first out from stock nobody has reserved, so another batch's reservation is never suggested.
- **Material requirements** (`GET /production/requirements?from=&to=`, at most a year): planned and in-progress batches in the range, each version's lines scaled to the batch in base units (`batchMaterials`), minus what in-progress batches have consumed, rolled up per ingredient against stock usable today (available, not past use-by) with quarantined stock shown beside it, and the shortfall (`materialRequirements`). Computed on read, never stored; a line whose unit can't be converted is flagged `incomplete`, not guessed.
- **Traceability** (`GET /production/lots/:lotId/trace`, optional `depth`, default and maximum 10): `backward` is one-up repeated (the batch that made the lot, the lots it consumed, and so on back to goods-in lots with their supplier lot codes); `forward` is one-down repeated (the batches that consumed the lot, the lots they made, onwards), each with quantities and what is still on hand: a mock recall is this query. `massBalance` accounts for the lot: received or produced against used in batches, wasted, adjusted (with the share of input) and on hand (`lotMassBalance`). Fully reversed records drop out. The stock lot page shows this trace to people who can read production.
- **Releasing the produced lot**: the batch page's "Produced lots" card has a **Release** button on each lot still in quarantine, with a required note. It posts to the stock module's `POST /stock/lots/:id/status` (`available`), nothing new: `manage` on `stock`, a signed-in person, API tokens never. The button shows only to people with that level.
- **Who may do what**: reading needs `read`; planning, editing, starting and recording consumption and output need `write` (API tokens allowed, so agents can plan and a future scale or tablet can record). Corrections are a person's; cancellations are a person's unless `production.cancel` is allowed for tokens; completion is `manage` and a person, never a token. Recording posts stock movements with production access alone: the module depends on stock, so it can't be used without at least reading it.
- **Reading**: `GET /production/batches` (filters `from`, `to`, `status` including `open`, `recipeId`, `recipeVersionId`, `outputItemId`, `q`; paged) and `GET /production/batches/:id` (materials required, consumed and outstanding; produced lots; every record with who and when). Batches and records are not in the bootstrap; writes go to the sync log (`production_batch`, `production_record`, plus the stock module's `stock_lot` and `stock_movement`), filtered by module access.
- **Planning from demand** (`GET /production/plan-from-demand?from=&to=`, `read`): per finished or WIP item with demand and per ISO week (dates or weeks like `2026-W46`; default 12 weeks from the current one, at most 104), the demand from the demand module's `getDemandSummary`, what the batches planned in that week already cover (by planned date, any recipe for the item: a planned batch its planned output, an in-progress one the larger of planned and produced, a completed one what it produced; cancelled ones don't count), the gap (never below zero) and the whole batches of the item's recipe that cover it, rounded up, with what they make (`planFromDemand` and `batchesToCover` in core, sized with `plannedOutputBase` like a batch). The recipe is picked deterministically: the only active recipe with an approved version, or the one named in `recipeIds` (comma-separated) when there are several. Items that can't be planned are flagged: `no_recipe`, `no_approved_version`, `choose_recipe` (the candidates are listed with what a batch makes) or `no_yield`. Computed on read, never stored. Demand is a soft dependency of production: this view needs `read` on demand too (`404 module_inactive` when Demand is off, `403 module_forbidden` without access), so the figures don't leak; the web app hides **Plan** without it. Creating the proposed batches (`POST /production/plan-from-demand`) is plain batch planning and needs production `write` only.
- **Creating from the plan** (`POST /production/plan-from-demand`, `write`; API tokens allowed: planning is not a sign-off): up to 200 `{ recipeId, plannedOn, batches | outputQuantity + outputUnit, … }`, each exactly what `POST /production/batches` takes, created in one transaction through the same insert as a single batch (approved version pinned, codes generated per day, sync log). All or nothing: a refusal names the batch (`Batch 2 (…): …`, `details.index`) and nothing is planned. The web page `/<workspace>/production/plan` (linked from the schedule) shows the grid for 4 to 26 weeks with editable batch counts, a recipe picker where several recipes make an item, and the batches to create with their planned dates (default: the week's Monday), each of which can be left out; the MCP tool `propose_batches_from_demand` returns the proposal and a `plan` list that `plan_production_batch` takes as `plan`.

- **Reserving stock** (`production_reservations`, sync entity `production_reservation`). A reservation sets aside a quantity of one lot (base unit) for a planned or in-progress batch; **available = on hand − reserved**, worked out per lot and never below zero, so a lot that has been reserved twice over, or has gone into quarantine or past its use-by date, can't make other stock look smaller. `POST /production/batches/:id/reservations` (`write`; API tokens allowed: it is planning, not a sign-off) takes `lines` and/or `allOutstanding`. A line is a lot (`lotId`, `quantity`, `unit`; strict: the lot must be released and not past use-by, the item an ingredient of the batch's version, and enough of it unreserved, else `409 insufficient_unreserved_stock`) or an item (`itemId`, with a quantity and unit or, without them, everything the batch still needs), which is picked first expired first out from unreserved, usable stock; if stock is short it reserves what there is and lists the `shortfalls` in the response. A batch can't hold more of an ingredient than it still needs (`409 over_reserved`). Reserving a lot the batch already holds adds to its reservation. Taking the workspace lock and re-reading balances inside the transaction makes two people reserving the last kilo safe.
  - **Released** when the batch consumes the lot (consumption fulfils the batch's reservation of that lot, oldest first, and releases it once used up; consuming more than was reserved just uses it up), when the batch is **completed** or **cancelled** (what is left is released), or **by hand** (`POST /production/reservations/:id/release`, optional note). A released row stays as history with its reason (`consumed`, `completed`, `cancelled`, `manual`). Reversing a consumption does not re-reserve.
  - **Consumption never needs a reservation and is never blocked by one**: recording consumption works as before whether or not the lot is reserved, even by another batch (the stock service only checks what is physically at the location). Reservations are a promise between planners, not a lock on the shelf.
  - **Shown on**: the batch page (a Reserved column in Materials, a "Reserved stock" table with Release, and a Reserve stock dialog; a reserved lot that can no longer be used is flagged), `GET /production/batches/:id` (`reservations`, `materials[].reserved`), the stock lot page (a Reservations card: reserved, free, which batches), the stock levels page (Reserved and Free columns), `GET /production/availability?itemIds=` (usable on hand, reserved and available per item) and `GET /production/reservations` (filters `batchId`, `lotId`, `itemId`, `status`). Material requirements count stock reserved for batches outside the date range as unavailable (so the shortfall, and with it purchasing's suggestions, is honest) and show what the range's own batches hold (`reserved`). All of these are production-module data: people who can't read production see none of it, and the stock module's own lot and level endpoints are unchanged.
  - **By location** (`production_reservations.location_id`, nullable). A line (or `locationId` with `allOutstanding`) may name a stock location: a lot line reserves the lot's balance there (refused with `409 insufficient_unreserved_stock` beyond what is at that location unreserved, and beyond what the lot as a whole has free); an item line picks first expired first out from that location only. Without it the reservation is of the lot as a whole, as before. A location reservation comes off its own location's balance first in FEFO picks, availability and requirements (`netOfReservations` with `reservedByLotLocation`); if the stock was moved away so there is less there than is held, the excess comes off the lot's other balances. A reserve for the same lot and place adds to the existing hold. Consumption fulfils the hold at the location it takes stock from first, then whole-lot holds, then another location's. The batch's own pick offers its location holds from that location only.
  - **Expiry** (`expires_on`, a date). A reservation holds **through** its `expiresOn` (the last day, in the workspace's time zone) and is expired from the next day; the date can't be in the past. Set it per line, per request (`expiresOn`, the default for lines without one), on re-reserving the same hold, or with `PATCH /production/reservations/:id` `{ expiresOn: date | null }` (`write`, tokens allowed). **Both on read and by write**: every read ignores an expired hold the moment its day passes (`reservedByLot` filters it), the batch shows it as `expired` with nothing held, and the notification sweep releases it (reason `expired`, no `releasedBy`, in the sync log); a reservation write also releases expired holds first so stock never stays tied up. Consumption ignores expired holds.
  - **Reserved stock at risk** (notification `reserved_lot_problem`, see [notifications](notifications.md)): when a reserved lot goes into **quarantine** or is **rejected** (the lot status change, in its transaction), or **runs out** (there is less of the lot, or of the lot at the reservation's location, than active reservations hold: found by the sweep, as waste, adjustments, stocktakes and other batches' consumption are many paths). Told once per reservation and problem: the person who reserved it (if they can read production) and everyone with `manage` on production, except whoever caused it. The batch detail flags such a reservation (`lotUsable`, `short`).
  - **MCP**: `reserve_batch_ingredients` (also `location` and `expires_on`) and `unreserve_batch_ingredients`; `get_production_batch` lists reservations.
- **Lines and capacity** (`production_lines`, `production_line_recipes`, `production_batches.line_id`; sync entities `production_line`, `production_line_recipe`). A line has a name (unique), hours per day (the capacity, default 8, at most 24) and an active flag (deactivated, never deleted). `PUT /production/lines/:id/recipes/:recipeId` says a line can make a recipe and at what **rate**: output per hour, entered in any unit that converts for the recipe's output item and stored in its base unit. Configuring lines and rates needs `manage`; reading needs `read`. A batch is put on a line with `lineId` when planned or later (`PATCH`, until it closes); an inactive line takes no new batches (`409 line_inactive`).
  - **Hours** for a batch are its planned output ÷ the line's rate for its recipe. `GET /production/schedule?from=&to=` (at most 62 days) returns per line per day the batches with their hours, `hours` against `capacityHours`, `loadPercent`, `overloaded` and `unrated` (batches whose recipe the line has no rate for: they add no hours, and are flagged rather than guessed), plus the open batches in range on no line. Cancelled batches don't count; completed ones do. **Overloading warns and never blocks**: the batch detail carries a `lineCheck` (its hours, the day's load, and warnings for overload, no rate, inactive line) which the batch page shows as an alert. The web pages are `/<workspace>/production/schedule` (the week as a line-by-day grid with load bars and over-capacity badges) and `/<workspace>/production/lines` (setting up lines and rates), linked from the batch schedule.
  - **Working-day calendar** (`production_non_working_days`, sync entity `production_non_working_day`). Every day is a working day unless listed, so weekends are marked like bank holidays. `POST /production/calendar` (`write`, tokens allowed) takes `from`, optional `to`, optional `weekdays` (1 = Monday … 7 = Sunday, `[6, 7]` for weekends), optional `lineId` (omit for the whole workspace) and `reason`, up to 366 days; dates already marked for that scope are left alone (`created` and `existing` are returned). `GET /production/calendar?from=&to=&lineId=` (at most two years) lists them; `DELETE /production/calendar/:id` puts a day back. **Advisory only**: a day off has `capacityHours` 0 and a `dayOff` on the line's day in `GET /production/schedule` (a batch on it is `overloaded`; the workspace's days off are in `daysOff`), a batch's detail carries `calendarWarning` (the planned date is a day off for the workspace or its line) and its `lineCheck` warns, and the cover view's capacity table lists days off and batches on them. Nothing is moved, refused or sequenced. **Not in the web app**: days off are set through the API and MCP (`list_production_days_off`, `set_non_working_days`); the schedule and batch pages show them.
  - **MCP**: `list_production_lines`, `get_production_schedule` and `assign_batch_line` (and `line` on `plan_production_batch`); lines and rates themselves are set up in the app. A batch is on one line for its whole size, and rates are per recipe not per pack size.
- **Detailed scheduling (3.5.1)** (`production_line_shifts`, `production_changeovers`, `production_lines.changeover_minutes` / `allergen_clean_minutes` / `crew_available`, `production_line_recipes.crew_size`, `production_batches.sequence`; sync entities `production_line_shift`, `production_changeover`). Rules in `@knotworking/core` `production-scheduling.ts`. **Advisory throughout**: nothing is reordered, moved or refused except by applying an order on purpose.
  - **Shifts**: `PUT /production/lines/:id/shifts` (`manage`) replaces a line's shift windows (name, ISO weekdays, `start`/`end` as `HH:MM`; an end at or before the start crosses midnight and belongs to the day it starts). Overlapping windows on the same day merge. An overnight shift counts fully on the day it starts, even when the next day is a day off or has an overlapping early shift. With any shifts, a day's capacity (the line schedule, a batch's `lineCheck` and `GET /demand/capacity`) is the length of that weekday's windows, and a weekday with none has no capacity (warned "no shift"); without shifts it stays the hours per day.
  - **Changeovers and allergens**: `PUT /production/lines/:id/changeovers` (`manage`) replaces the line's rules (from recipe, to recipe, either may be null for "any", minutes, note; one rule per pair, recipes must be the workspace's). The same recipe again takes none unless a rule names that exact pair (A→A); wildcard rules never apply to it. Otherwise the most specific rule wins (from→to, from→any, any→to), else the line's `changeoverMinutes`. An **allergen clean-down** is needed when the batch before contains an allergen this one doesn't (the pinned version's approved ingredient roll-up together with the output item's own declaration); a "may contain" label doesn't replace cleaning. It takes the longer of the changeover and the line's `allergenCleanMinutes`.
  - **Crew**: `crewSize` on a line recipe (the rate's `PUT`) and `crewAvailable` on the line (`PATCH`). A batch needing more people than the line has is warned; labour hours are run hours × crew.
  - **Day plan**: `GET /production/schedule/day?lineId=&date=` times the line's uncancelled batches in their applied order (`sequence`, then batch code) inside the shift windows (or the hours per day from 06:00 without shifts, warned): each step has its changeover with source and reason, allergen clean-down, start and end (minutes from midnight and clock times, "+1" past midnight), crew and warnings. Work pauses between windows and continues past the last one as an **overrun**. Batches without a rate take no time and are flagged. `proposal` is an explained greedy order: started and finished batches stay first, then the batch that avoids a clean-down, then the shortest changeover (a clean-down counts its full time), then the fewest allergens (so allergen-free runs first), then the current order. It compares current and proposed changeover minutes, clean-downs and end time.
  - **Applying an order**: `PUT /production/schedule/sequence` (`write`, tokens allowed) with `lineId`, `date` and `batchIds`, which must be exactly the day's uncancelled batches, and optionally `expectedBatchIds`, the order the plan was read in (`409 sequence_stale` if the set changed or someone else reordered it meanwhile; the web page sends it). At most 500 batches a day. Moving a batch to another day or line clears its `sequence`.
  - **Daily demand**: `GET /production/daily-demand?days=&itemId=` (1–31 days from today, default 14): per product per day, outstanding **confirmed sales commitments** (Sales readers only; overdue ones count on today), **planned output** of planned and in-progress batches (late ones count on today) and the **projection** from today's free stock: released, unexpired lots less what active reservations hold (Stock readers only) with `short` days and `firstShortDate`. Weekly demand (Demand readers) is listed beside it and **never added in**, so an order and its forecast are not counted twice; `basis` says what was readable.
  - The line schedule lists a day's batches in the applied order too. **Web**: `/<workspace>/production/schedule/day` (opened from a line schedule day: times, changeovers, clean-downs, reordering and the proposed order with its reasons) and `/<workspace>/production/daily-demand`; shifts, changeovers and crew are set on `/<workspace>/production/lines`. **MCP**: `get_production_day_plan`, `set_production_sequence`, `get_production_daily_demand`.
  - **Not yet**: automatic moving of batches between days or lines, splitting a batch across lines, labour drawn from the workforce rota (crew is a number per line), and changeover times per pack size.
- **Factory performance (3.5.2)** (`production_downtime`, `production_rejects`, `production_labour`; sync entities `production_downtime`, `production_reject`, `production_labour`). Rules and definitions in `@knotworking/core` `production-performance.ts`. Records are **append-only**: a mistake is voided with a reason (`POST /production/{downtime,rejects,labour}/:id/void`, a person's act: tokens refused) and stays as history without counting. Recording takes a `commandId` (or `Idempotency-Key`), so a retry records once.
  - **Downtime**: `POST /production/downtime` (`write`, tokens allowed so a line device can post) with line, production day, minutes, a reason and optionally the batch running (it must be on that line). Reasons are planned (planned maintenance, scheduled cleaning, changeover, break, no orders, trial) or unplanned (breakdown, minor stops, material shortage, staff shortage, quality hold, utilities failure, other). `GET /production/downtime?from=&to=&lineId=` lists them, voided ones marked.
  - **Rejects and labour**: `POST /production/batches/:id/rejects` (quantity in a unit of the output item, reason) and `POST /production/batches/:id/labour` (people × minutes) on a started batch; on a signed-off batch adding or voiding needs `manage` (`403 batch_completed`); recording on a planned or cancelled batch is refused (`409 batch_not_running`), and voiding on a cancelled one needs `manage`. Downtime against a signed-off or cancelled batch follows the same `manage` rule. Rejects are never put into stock.
  - **OEE**: `GET /production/performance?from=&to=&lineId=` (at most 93 days) returns per line per day and over the range, with the definitions it used (`definitions`, version `oee-v1`): planned production time = capacity (shifts, else hours per day; none on a day off) less planned downtime, where a day with nothing planned on the line is not scheduled and counts none (downtime logged on it is listed as `unscheduledDowntimeMinutes`, not counted); run time = that less unplanned downtime; availability = run ÷ planned; performance = ideal time for good output plus rejects at the line's rate ÷ run time; quality = ideal good time ÷ ideal total time; OEE = their product = ideal good time ÷ planned production time. Good output is the net output recorded by batches planned that day, and downtime recorded against a batch counts on that day too. Batches without a rate are left out of performance and quality and flagged; performance over 100% warns that a rate may be too low. Also downtime by reason (minutes, stops), rejects by reason (records and the run time they cost, comparable across products), and labour: standard person-hours (planned ÷ rate × the recipe's crew size) against actual. `GET /production/batches/:id/performance` gives one batch's good output, rejects, first-pass yield, run and standard hours, actual speed, labour and records.
  - **Standard and actual cost stay apart**: performance never changes costing. For people who can read costing it adds line hours at the costing module's per-hour labour and overhead absorption rates: standard hours at the rates in force on the planned date against actual run hours (started to completed) at those in force on the completion date, as costing absorbs them, with the variance (positive is adverse) and the batches it couldn't cost. Per-kg rates are left out. Others get `cost: null`.
  - **Web**: `/<workspace>/production/performance` (the week per line: OEE and its parts, the day table, downtime and rejects by reason, labour, line cost, the downtime log with void, and the definitions) and a Performance card on each started batch (record and void rejects, labour and downtime). **MCP**: `get_production_performance`, `record_production_downtime`, `record_batch_rejects`, `record_batch_labour`, each taking an optional `command_id` so a retry records once (voiding stays in the app).
  - **Not yet**: changeover time from the day plan isn't recorded as downtime automatically; downtime has no start and end times (minutes only); labour isn't drawn from attendance; device feeds (counters, checkweighers) come with the adapters milestone.
- **Packing checks (3.5.3)** (`production_packing_runs`, `production_packing_checks`; sync entities `production_packing_run`, `production_packing_check`). Rules in `@knotworking/core` `production-packing.ts`.
  - **Packing run**: `POST /production/batches/:id/packing` (`write`, tokens allowed) on a started or completed batch pins the **approved product label** the packs carry, which must be of the batch's recipe and drafted from the batch's own recipe version (`409 label_not_for_batch`, `label_not_approved`). It is required when the labels module is on; with no label given the only approved label is pinned, so a packer without Labels access can start, and a choice among several is refused (`409 label_choice_needed`). It also takes, optionally, an **approved specification version** of an active specification of the output item, the **date code** and **lot code** (default the batch code) the packs must show, a **nominal quantity** in g or ml (turns on weight samples) and whether the line has a metal detector. The label and specification are copied onto the run, so the record reads without those modules. One open run per batch (`409 packing_run_open`).
  - **Checks**: `POST /production/packing/:id/checks`, append-only and voided with a reason (`POST /production/packing/checks/:id/void`, a person's act). Kinds: **line clearance** (four items, all done to pass; one left out counts as not done), **first-off** and **in-run label and code checks** (approved label on the pack, codes as expected ignoring case and spaces, legible), **metal detector challenge** (each of the ferrous, non-ferrous and stainless steel test pieces, with its size, rejected; refused on a run opened without a detector, `409 no_detector`), and **weight sample** (pack weights against the nominal: mean at least nominal, no more than 2.5% of packs below nominal less the tolerable negative error, none below nominal less twice the TNE, using the UK Packaged Goods Regulations table; indicative, as the rules apply to a whole batch; refused on a run without a nominal quantity, `409 no_nominal_quantity`). A failed check needs `failureAction` (`400 failure_action_required`), and stays open until a later check of the same kind passes.
  - **Closing**: `POST /production/packing/:id/close` (a person's sign-off) needs line clearance, first-off, a detector challenge (when the line has one) and a weight sample (with a nominal quantity), the last of each kind passed (`409 packing_checks_incomplete` lists what's missing), the packs produced and **packaging reconciliation** for at least one material (issued = used + wasted + returned), including a printed one when a label is pinned (`400 printed_packaging_required`); any difference needs `varianceReason`. A batch with an open packing run can't be cancelled (`409 packing_run_open`). A closed run never changes (`409 packing_run_closed`).
  - **Tracing**: `GET /production/lots/:lotId/packing` gives, for a lot, the batches that produced it and the label (recipe, variant, version, product name, barcode) and specification version each batch's packs carried, or `unrecorded` when a batch has no packing run. `GET /production/batches/:id/packing` lists a batch's runs with checks and `gaps`, and the labels and specification versions a new run may pin, these only to people who can read the labels and specifications modules (others get `labelsVisible: false` and `labelCount`).
  - **HACCP**: where metal detection is a CCP, its monitoring stays in HACCP CCP logs against the batch (shown on the batch page to HACCP readers); the challenge here is the packing line's verification record.
  - **Web**: a Packing card on the batch page (start packing, each check, void, close with reconciliation). **MCP**: `get_batch_packing`, `get_lot_packing`, `open_packing_run`, `record_packing_check` (closing and voiding stay in the app).
  - **Not yet**: metal detector and coder event feeds (see Device adapters below; checkweigher and scale readings can make weight samples); completing a batch doesn't yet require its packing to be closed (packing can start after completion); label artwork isn't compared, only which approved label version was used.
- **Device adapters (3.5.4)** (`production_devices`, `production_device_readings`; sync entities `production_device`, `production_device_reading`). Rules in `@knotworking/core` `production-devices.ts`; added on top of the manual workflows, which keep working without any device.
  - **Identity**: `POST /production/devices` and `PATCH /production/devices/:id` (`manage`) register a device: a code (unique; how a site gateway names it), name, kind (scale, checkweigher, metal detector, temperature probe, scanner, label printer), the unit a measuring device reads in (g, kg, ml or l for a scale or checkweigher, °C for a temperature probe), an optional line and optional HACCP equipment. Linking (or unlinking) equipment needs HACCP read access (`403 haccp_access_required`); an area, or retired or deleted equipment, reads as not found. Devices are retired, never deleted. Readings arrive through a person's or integration's API token (a gateway), never anonymously.
  - **Calibration state**: from the linked equipment's latest calibration record at or before a given time that no later record corrects, due again on its next-due date or, without one, its date plus the equipment's calibration frequency (as the HACCP schedule works it out): `in_date`, `overdue`, `failed`, `unknown` (none on record, or the equipment is retired or deleted) or `not_required` (not linked). `GET /production/devices` shows the state now with when each device last sent a reading; the equipment link and calibration dates only to HACCP readers. Every reading is stamped with the state **when it was taken**, so a reading buffered through a failed calibration stays failed after a later pass.
  - **Readings, offline and duplicates**: `POST /production/devices/:id/readings` (`write`, tokens allowed) takes up to 500 readings, each with the device's own reading id, the time taken (with offset), value and unit. Each is **accepted** (converted to the device's unit when the unit converts, the sent value kept), a **duplicate** (same id, value, unit and time, values compared to the 6 decimal places they are stored to: stored once, so a gateway can resend after a lost response), a **conflict** (same id, different value, unit or time: refused) or **rejected** with its reason (a unit that doesn't convert, out of range, more than 2 minutes in the future, more than 30 days old). One bad reading never fails the batch. Readings buffered while offline are accepted and marked `late` when received more than 10 minutes after they were taken. A retired device's readings are refused (`409 device_retired`), and so are readings from a device that doesn't measure (`409 device_not_measuring`). `GET /production/devices/:id/readings?from=&to=&unused=` lists them.
  - **Into records**: `POST /production/packing/:id/checks/weight-from-device` (`write`, tokens allowed) turns a scale's or checkweigher's readings into a weight sample on an open packing run, converted to g or ml and judged like any weight sample, with the device and reading ids kept on the check. Refused unless the device is linked to calibrated equipment (`409 device_not_calibrated`) that is in date now and was when each reading was taken (`device_calibration`), each reading is a pack weight between 0 and 100 kg (`reading_out_of_range`), was taken after the run started (`reading_before_run`) and isn't already part of a check (`reading_used`). A retired device's earlier readings may still be used.
  - **Web**: `/<workspace>/production/devices` (register, retire, calibration state, last reading), linked from Lines; a "Weight from scale" dialog on the packing card. **MCP**: `list_production_devices`, `list_device_readings`, `send_device_readings`, `record_weight_from_device`. Readings are in the sync log like other production records; a high-rate checkweigher may later warrant syncing only the readings used.
  - **Not yet**: per-device credentials (a gateway uses an API token); detector, coder and printer events (their checks stay manual); temperature readings into HACCP CCP logs; external laboratory feeds (the testing module's result API remains the route); scanners beyond the existing lot-code scan.
- **Consumption by barcode**: the Record consumption dialog has a scan field. A keyboard-wedge scanner types the lot code and presses Enter; the form looks the code up (`GET /production/batches/:id/scan?code=`: exact, case-insensitive lot code, with where the lot is, what the batch still needs of it and why it can't be used: `not_in_recipe`, `lot_not_available`, `lot_expired` or `no_stock`), picks that lot, fills in the quantity still needed (capped at what is at the location) and clears other suggestions for the same ingredient. The person then edits the quantity if they used less. The code is the lot code as printed; this system does not print lot barcodes yet, so use whatever the supplier's or your own label encodes as the lot code. **Scales are left out**: reading a weight from a scale is an external device and an integration of its own; people type the weight, or an agent or tablet can post it to `POST …/consumption`.

Not built yet: a web page to edit the working-day calendar (API and MCP only), automatic scheduling (proposals are advisory and applied by hand), a GTIN on GS1-128 lot labels (items have none), and reading weights from a scale. CCP checks against a batch are recorded by the `haccp` module and shown on the batch page to people who can read it, with a warning (never a block) when the batch has open deviations, also in the completion response; what the batch cost, its yield and variances come from the `costing` module, shown there to people who can read costing.

## Demand module

Demand per product per week: what customers want, entered by hand, imported from CSV or posted by the sales system, and summed for production planning. Code: `packages/core/src/demand.ts`, `packages/db/src/schema/demand.ts`, `apps/server/src/{services,routes}/demand.ts`, `apps/mcp/src/tools/demand.ts`, and the web pages `/<workspace>/demand` (the plan grid) and `/<workspace>/demand/lines` (lines, history and imports). Needs `items`.

- **Period**: an ISO week, Monday to Sunday, stored as the date of its Monday (`periodStart`) and shown as `2026-W46`. Weeks match how UK food manufacturers plan production and how retailers send forecasts; any date given (ISO `2026-11-12`, UK `12/11/2026`) is read as the week it falls in, and `2026-W46` works too (`parsePeriod`).
- **Lines** (`demand_lines`): one live line per item, week and customer. The customer (or channel: a retailer, wholesale, online) is optional free text, compared without case or extra spaces; no customer is its own key. Only active finished goods and work in progress can have demand (ingredients and packaging are bought in). The quantity is entered as a number and a unit (default: the item's own), converted with `convertQuantity` and held in the item's base unit (g, ml or each) as `numeric(18,4)`, like the stock ledger; responses give `{ base, baseUnit, display, displayUnit }`. Each line has an optional note, the sales system's reference (`externalRef`), its `source` and who created and last changed it.
- **Source**: where the current quantity came from: `manual` (a person in the app), `import` (a CSV import, with the import's id), `api` (an API token: the sales system's feed or an agent) or `forecast` (copied from the forecast, see below). It follows the credential (or the copy action), so it can't be claimed. A write that only changes a note keeps the source.
- **Writing** (`write`; API tokens allowed): `PUT /demand/lines` with up to 1000 `{ itemId | itemCode, period, quantity, unit?, customer?, note?, externalRef? }`. Writing a key that exists replaces its quantity; sending the same values again changes nothing (idempotent, so a feed can resend a whole horizon); `quantity: null` removes the line. Every batch is all or nothing: lines are checked against the items first and any problem answers `400 invalid_rows` with each problem by line number (`details.errors`). `DELETE /demand/lines/:id` removes one line. Removed lines are soft-deleted.
- **CSV import** (`POST /demand/imports`, `write`): CSV text (comma, semicolon or tab separated; parsed in core by `parseCsv` / `parseDemandCsv`, so the web app and agents share the rules). A header row names the columns in any order: `item` (stock code), `period`, `quantity` (0 or more; `1,200` is read as 1200) are required; `unit`, `customer`, `note` and `reference` are optional; other columns are ignored and reported. Up to 5000 rows. `dryRun: true` checks every row and returns the problems by spreadsheet row and column, and a preview of what would be created, updated or left unchanged; nothing is written. Without it the import writes every row in one transaction or answers `400 invalid_rows` and writes nothing. Two rows for the same item, week and customer are an error, not a sum. Each import is recorded (`demand_imports`: file name, rows, counts, who) and the lines it sets point at it. A file removes nothing unless **`replaceHorizon: true`** is sent (see below); otherwise send 0, or remove lines in the app or with `quantity: null`.
- **Replace this horizon** (`replaceHorizon: true`, opt-in; `write`, API tokens allowed): after checking the file, every live line in the weeks from the file's first to its last, for any item but **only the customers the file names** (case-insensitively; no customer counts as one), that no row of the file names is removed. Other customers' demand is left alone, so one retailer's forecast never wipes another's. Each removal is soft-deleted (with a `remove` history row with source `import` and the import's id, a `delete` in the sync log, counted in the import's `removedCount`). Rows in the file are created, changed or left as usual. The dry run lists the removals as `remove` changes (`row: 0`, with the line's quantity as `previous`) and returns `removed` and the `horizon` (`from`, `to`, `fromWeek`, `toWeek`); `removed` is 0 and `horizon` `null` without the flag. A file with problems removes nothing. The web import dialog has a "Replace this horizon" checkbox (off by default, the preview lists removals first, and ticking it needs a new check); the MCP `import_demand_csv` takes `replace_horizon`.
- **History**: `demand_history` is append-only: every create, change and removal with the quantity before and after, the source, the import and who. `GET /demand/lines/:id/history` returns the history of that line's item, week and customer, including earlier lines with the same key that were removed and set again.
- **Reading** (`read`): `GET /demand/lines` (filters `itemId` or `itemCode`, `from`, `to` (dates or weeks), `customer`, `source`, `importId`, `q`; by week, item code and customer; `limit`/`offset`, with `total`) and `GET /demand/imports` (newest first, paged).
- **Summary for production**: `GET /demand/summary?from=&to=` (default: 12 weeks from the current week; at most 104; filters `itemId` or `itemCode`, `itemType`; `byCustomer=true` splits each item by customer). It returns the `DemandSummary` type from core: every week of the range, and one row per item with demand (archived items included, check `item.status`), each with its total and the weeks that have demand (`quantity` in base and display units, `lineCount`). Server code calls `getDemandSummary(db, workspaceId, { from, to, itemIds, itemType, byCustomer })` in `apps/server/src/services/demand.ts` directly; the production module plans batches from it.
- **Sync**: lines and imports are written to the sync log (`demand_line`, `demand_import`), filtered by module access; history is not. Demand is not in the bootstrap.
- **Who may do what**: nothing needs `manage`: demand is planning data, not a sign-off, and every change is in the history. API tokens may read and write, so agents and the sales system can post demand.
- **Web**: the plan grid shows products (and customers) against weeks, 4 to 26 at a time; people type quantities in the item's own unit, changed cells are saved together, clearing a cell removes the line, and rows can be added for any active finished good or WIP item. The CSV import dialog checks the file first and lists every problem or the changes it will make. The lines page filters, pages, shows each line's history and lists recent imports.
- **MCP**: `get_demand_summary`, `list_demand_lines`, `set_demand`, `import_demand_csv` (plus the tools below).

- **Units of sale** (`demand_pack_units`): per item (active finished goods and WIP), named packs with a size in the item's base unit: `case = 12 each`, `tray = 2.5 kg` (sizes may be given in another unit of the item's dimension and are converted: `size: 2.5, unit: "kg"` is 2500 g). Names are unique per item without case; a name can't be a plain unit (`kg`, `each`, `litres`…), and an item has at most 20. `PUT /demand/pack-units` (`write`, API tokens allowed) sets many (`{ itemId | itemCode, name, size, unit? }`, `size: null` removes; all or nothing, `invalid_rows` like lines), `GET /demand/pack-units` lists (`itemId`/`itemCode`), `DELETE /demand/pack-units/:id`. Writes go to the sync log (`demand_pack_unit`). A line's `unit` (in `PUT /demand/lines`, `unit` column of a CSV import, MCP `set_demand`) may name a pack unit (checked first, case-insensitive; the old CSV alias `case` = each is gone) as well as a plain unit. The quantity is stored in base units (5 cases of 12 is 60 each), and what was typed is kept on the line (`enteredQuantity`, `enteredUnit`: `5`, `case`) and in each history row; it is cleared when the quantity is next set in a plain unit and kept when a write leaves the base quantity unchanged. Resizing or removing a pack never changes demand already entered (it is in base units); the note on the line keeps the pack name as it was. The plan grid has a unit picker per product that has packs (cells then show and take that unit), a "Units of sale" dialog, and the lines page and history show "entered as 5 case".
- **Forecast** (`GET /demand/forecast`, `read`; rules in core `demand-planning.ts`): per item, or per item and customer with `byCustomer=true`, for `from`..`to` (default: 8 weeks from this week, at most 52; only active finished goods and WIP). No model: the **baseline** is the average weekly demand over the last `window` weeks (default 8, 2 to 26) before the current week, counting weeks without a line as 0 but not weeks before the first demand on record; with `seasonal` (default on), when there is history from a year ago the baseline is multiplied by a **seasonal index**: the average of the same ISO week last year and the weeks either side, divided by that year's own baseline for the weeks before it, held between 0.25 and 4. With less than a year of history, or no demand then, it is the plain average. Each week returns `method` (`average` or `seasonal`), the baseline, the index and an `explanation` sentence. History is the demand lines of weeks before the current week, **except** lines whose source is `forecast` (copied forecasts are not demand, so the forecast never feeds on itself). Whole base units; weeks at 0 are not shown. **Copying** (`POST /demand/forecast/apply`, `write`, same options plus `overwrite`) writes the forecast with `setDemandLines` and source `forecast` (an ordinary write: history, sync log, all or nothing). It fills only keys (item, week, customer) that have no line, never writes an item total beside customer lines or a customer's forecast beside a no-customer line (it would be counted twice), and returns what it `skipped` and why; `overwrite` replaces the line with the same key. The plan grid's "Show forecast" shows the figures in grey in empty cells (hover for the explanation, the grid forecasts by customer) and "Copy forecast into empty weeks" applies it. Source badge: "Forecast".
- **Cover** (`GET /demand/coverage`, `read`; web `/<workspace>/demand/cover`): per item with demand, per week from the current week (earlier weeks are clamped: stock is what is on the shelf now): demand, the output of **planned and in-progress** production batches due that week (batches due earlier count in the first week; completed and cancelled ones don't), and what is left at the end of the week = available stock now + planned batches so far - demand so far, with `shortfall` (how much more is needed by then) and `status` (`ok`, `short`, `unknown`). Stock is the balance of released (`available`) lots that have not expired. Demand doesn't depend on stock or production, so the route reads those tables only for callers who can read the module (active and `read`): `basis` says what is counted, `stock`/`planned` are `null` otherwise, and with neither the status is `unknown` and no shortfall is shown. With only one of them, "short" means short against that source alone. It uses planned batches as they are today; line capacity is compared in the next bullet.
- **Capacity** (`GET /demand/capacity`, `read`; same filters as cover; shown under the cover table on `/<workspace>/demand/cover`; core `demand-capacity.ts`). There is no separate capacity field: a production line's capacity is its **hours per day** (production module) on each of its working days, so a week's capacity is that summed over the active lines, with the working-day calendar's days off (workspace-wide or the line's own) counting nothing. Per week it returns `capacityHours`; `bookedHours` (batches already on lines, cancelled ones left out, open ones dated before the range in its first week; a batch on a line with no rate for its recipe adds nothing and is counted as `unratedBatches`, one on no line as `unassignedBatches`); `neededHours` (the cover view's _new_ shortfall of the week, this week's cumulative shortfall less last week's, never below 0, divided by the **fastest** active line's rate for any active recipe of the product, and products no line can make are `unratedItems`); `loadPercent` = (booked + needed) ÷ capacity; `status` `ok`, `tight` (90% or more) or `over`; `daysOff` and `batchesOnDaysOff`; and per-line capacity and booked hours. Decisions: it does not pick a line for the shortfall (that is planning), so the comparison is in total hours; with no active line every week is `ok` with no load. Production data: callers who can't read production get `available: false` (demand doesn't depend on production). Advisory; nothing is scheduled.
- **Daily periods: assessed, not built.** Weeks are the key of a demand line, the unique index, history, imports (`period` is always read as its Monday), the summary, the forecast and the cover view, and the production plan-from-demand proposal plans per ISO week; the grid, CSV and MCP all show weeks. Days would need a second period kind through each of these (a `period_kind`/`period_end`, a day-level key that can overlap a week line, rules for summing days into weeks for production, and a day-level grid) and a decision about how a weekly forecast from a retailer and a daily order for the same week combine. That is a large cross-cutting change with an unclear rule for overlaps, so it stays in "Not built yet". Until then, enter a week's demand as the week's total, with the daily split in the note.
- **MCP** adds `list_pack_units`, `set_pack_units`, `get_demand_forecast`, `copy_demand_forecast`, `get_demand_coverage` and `get_demand_capacity`.

Not built yet: a pull from the sales system (the API takes a push today) and daily periods (see above).

## Food safety records (HACCP) module

The HACCP plan and the records that show it is followed (BRCGS, Codex HACCP principles). Code: `packages/core/src/haccp.ts`, `packages/db/src/schema/haccp.ts`, `apps/server/src/{services,routes}/haccp.ts`, `apps/mcp/src/tools/haccp.ts`, and the web pages `/<workspace>/haccp` (today's checks), `/<workspace>/haccp/review` (readings awaiting review; `manage` only), `/<workspace>/haccp/plans` and `/<workspace>/haccp/plans/<id>`, `/<workspace>/haccp/deviations` and `/<workspace>/haccp/records` (cleaning and calibration). Needs `assets` (its areas and equipment are assets of the register); Items, Recipes, Stock and Production only enhance it. Production does not depend on it.

- **Plans** (`haccp_plans`, `haccp_plan_versions`, `haccp_ccps`) are controlled documents with the recipes module's rules: a code unique among live plans, at most one draft (the only editable version) and one approved version per plan (partial unique indexes), superseded versions kept and readable. Saving the draft (`PUT /haccp/plans/:id/draft`) replaces its scope, notes, change note and whole CCP list, or starts the next version (the client sends the full content). A plan that has ever been approved can be archived, not deleted (`DELETE /haccp/plans/:id` only removes a plan that was never approved, with its draft). Editing the header (`PATCH /haccp/plans/:id`: code, name, description, review interval) is `write`, API tokens allowed; the status is not accepted there. Archiving or restoring (`POST /haccp/plans/:id/status` with `{ status: "archived" | "active" }`) takes the plan out of force or back into it, so like approval it needs `manage` and a signed-in person. Plans are reviewed every `reviewIntervalMonths` (default 12).
- **Scope**: the products (items) and recipes a version covers; both empty means the whole site. A production batch is covered when its recipe or output item is in scope (`scopeCoversBatch`).
- **Soft dependencies**: HACCP needs only the asset register (`assets`, below). Items, Recipes, Stock and Production enhance it (`enhancedBy`): naming products or recipes in a scope needs read access to Items or Recipes (a draft may keep what earlier versions already name), a batch on a reading or corrective action needs Production, a lot needs Stock, and `GET /haccp/batches/:batchId` needs Production. Responses leave out (`null` / `[]`) the batch, lot, items and recipes the caller can't read, and without Production per-batch CCPs are never due (`batchesInProgress` is empty), as when nothing is in production.
- **CCPs**: a code (`CCP1`, unique in the version; checks follow a CCP across versions by its code), the process step, the hazard and its type (biological, chemical, physical, allergen), the critical limit (a measured `range`: a minimum, a maximum or both, inclusive, with a unit such as °C; or a `pass_fail` criterion), the monitoring method and frequency (once per `batch`, or every N hours, days, weeks or months; optionally only `duringProduction`), the responsible role, the corrective action and the verification. Values are `numeric(14,4)`.
- **Approval** (`POST /haccp/plans/:id/draft/approve`, `manage`, a signed-in person: API tokens are refused and there is no MCP tool) needs at least one CCP. It records who and when, the effective date (default today, may be in the future) and the review date (default effective date plus the review interval, with the SOPs module's date helpers), and labels the previous approved version `superseded`.
- **The version in force** follows the effective dates, not the status labels (`versionInForce`, core): on a date it is the signed-off version (`approved` or `superseded`, with an approval stamp) that has taken effect by then (`effectiveOn` <= the date) and was approved last, which is the highest version number (versions are approved one at a time in number order, so a shared effective date goes to the higher number). Approving with a future effective date therefore schedules the new version: the previous one stays in force, is monitored against and is what due checks and the batch panel use until that date, and the new version's CCPs take over on it. Because the last approval wins, a scheduled version that a later approval replaces with an earlier date never takes effect. `planVersionSchedule` (core) also gives each version its `state` (`in_force`, `scheduled`, `replaced`, `draft`) and the version in force its `inForceUntil` (the date the next version takes effect). Plan reads return `currentVersion` (in force, with `inForceUntil`), `upcomingVersion` (the next scheduled one) and `state` on every version; the web shows a scheduled version as "Effective from <date>" and the one it replaces as "in force until <date>".
- **CCP monitoring logs** (`haccp_ccp_logs`) are append-only: no route deletes one, and the only update is the one-time review stamp (below), the same pattern as a cleaning record's verification. A check (`POST /haccp/logs`) names a CCP of the version of its plan **in force on the day of the check** (in the workspace's time zone; `409 ccp_not_current` for any other version, a scheduled one included) and is pinned to that row and version. It holds the reading (a value, or pass/fail), the batch (optional; in progress or completed, and covered by the plan), when it was made (default now, never in the future), who recorded it, the `source` and the review status. `evaluateReading` (core) works out `withinLimits` and the `deviationReason` (below minimum, above maximum, failed) when it is written. **An out-of-limit reading opens a corrective action in the same transaction**, titled with the CCP, the reading and the limit, and filled with what was done straight away if the request says (`correctiveAction`). This happens whoever recorded it: **a deviation never waits for review**.
- **Corrections** (`POST /haccp/logs/:id/correct`, with a reason) append a new log with the right reading, pinned to the same CCP, batch and time, pointing at the one it corrects (`corrects_log_id`; a log is corrected once). A signed-in person only; correcting an out-of-limit reading also needs `manage`, so a deviation can't be quietly written away, and its corrective action stays open until a manager closes it. A correction that is still out of limits reuses the corrective action of the deviation it replaces, found anywhere in the chain of corrections back to the original reading (a correction of a correction doesn't open a second one); only an _open_ action carries over: if the one in the chain is closed (a manager dealt with that excursion), a reading that is still out of limits opens a new one, as site storage corrections do. Lists show corrected readings struck through (`correctedByLogId`); `includeCorrected=false` leaves them out. Any reading can be corrected whatever its review status, a pending one included (the agent or sensor got it wrong and the person on the line knows the right value): the correction is the person's own entry, so it is `approved` as written. The pending reading it replaces keeps its `pending` stamp but no longer counts, leaves the review queue and the awaiting-review count, and can't be reviewed (`409 already_corrected`).
- **Who records readings**: people with `write`, in the app, and **any API token with `write`** on the module (an agent through the MCP server's `record_ccp_readings`, or a sensor or line-system feed). Agents make data entry quicker; a person still signs the record off. A person's reading is `approved` as they enter it, with `reviewed_by` and `reviewed_at` set to them and that moment: their entry is the attestation. A token's reading is marked `source: api` and starts `pending`.
- **Review** (`POST /haccp/logs/:id/review` with `{ decision: "approve" | "reject", note? }`, and the bulk `POST /haccp/logs/review` with `{ ids, decision, note? }`, up to 200, all or nothing) is a sign-off: `manage` and a signed-in person (tokens are refused; there is no MCP tool). A note is required to reject. Every reading must be `pending` (`409 already_reviewed`, with the `ids` that weren't) and not corrected (`409 already_corrected`); a missing one is `404`, and then nothing is reviewed. It stamps `review_status`, `reviewed_by`, `reviewed_at` and `review_note` once and writes the log to the sync log. A reviewer may approve readings recorded through their own token: the point is that a person looked at them. **Rejecting a reading doesn't close the corrective action it opened**: the deviation may still be real (the product may have been under-cooked even if the probe was misplaced), so it stays open until a person with `manage` closes it with what was found. Review statuses and labels, `countsAsDone` and `awaitsReview` are in core.
- **Corrective actions** (`haccp_corrective_actions`) link to the deviation (a log), a failed calibration, a batch and a stock lot, all optional (putting a lot into quarantine is a stock action: do it on the lot page and link the lot). They hold what was done, the root cause and the preventive action, and are open while being worked on (`PATCH`, `write`; API tokens allowed, so agents can propose them: the ones they raise are `source: api`). Closing (`POST …/close`) is the sign-off: `manage`, a signed-in person, and all three fields recorded (`409 corrective_action_incomplete` with what is `missing`). A closed one never changes. `GET /haccp/deviations` lists out-of-limit readings with their corrective actions (`status`: open while one of them is open).
- **Areas and equipment** are assets of the register ([Assets](#assets-module), kinds `area` and `equipment`): added, renamed and archived there (archiving needs `manage` on Assets). HACCP keeps its part of each one in `haccp_assets`, an extension table keyed by the asset's id: an optional cleaning frequency, and for equipment a calibration frequency, tolerance and measurement unit. `GET /haccp/assets` lists the register's areas and equipment with their schedules (the shape it always had); `PATCH /haccp/assets/:id` sets the schedule (`haccpAssetScheduleSchema`; `null` takes an asset out of a schedule; only equipment is calibrated). There is no `POST /haccp/assets` any more: add the asset with `POST /assets`, then set its schedule. Cleaning and calibration records point at `assets`. Vehicles, IT and tools are not in HACCP's schedule.
- **Cleaning records** (`haccp_cleaning_records`) are append-only: the area or equipment, when, who did it (default the person recording; any member) and who recorded it. Verification (`POST /haccp/cleaning/:id/verify`) is a one-time stamp by someone other than whoever cleaned (`403 four_eyes`). Recording and verifying are a person's attestations (tokens refused).
- **Calibration records** (`haccp_calibration_records`) are append-only: the equipment, the reference and actual readings, the tolerance and unit copied from the equipment, pass or fail (`calibrationPasses`: within the tolerance, inclusive; without one, the person says), the method and the next due date (default: the check plus the equipment's frequency). A failed calibration opens a corrective action (check what the instrument measured since its last good calibration). A person's attestation (tokens refused).
- **What's due** (`GET /haccp/due`, `include=due|all`, `kind`) is computed on read. Only the CCPs of the version in force today are scheduled. Per-batch CCPs are due on every batch in progress that the plan covers until checked on it. Other CCPs and cleaning use `checkDueState` from the last record: hourly checks run on the clock; daily, weekly and monthly ones on the calendar (the workspace's time zone); never done is due now; `duringProduction` CCPs are `idle` while nothing the plan covers is in progress. Pending and approved readings count as done (`countsAsDone`), so nothing is entered twice while a reading waits for review; a rejected one doesn't, so the check is due again. `counts.awaitingReview` is how many readings wait for review (pending and not corrected). Calibrations use the next due date of the last record, and are overdue after a failure. States: overdue, due, soon (within 7 days), ok, idle.
- **On the production batch**: `GET /haccp/batches/:batchId` returns the checks recorded against a batch, the CCPs of the plan versions in force that cover it (with how many checks, how many of those await review, and deviations each has), how many of its readings await review, and its open corrective actions. Rejected readings don't count as checks, so a per-batch check rejected on review is missing again. The batch page shows it to people who can read haccp, with a warning while a corrective action is open or a per-batch check is missing. It does not block completing the batch: production doesn't know about haccp.
- **Reading**: `GET /haccp/plans` (filters `q`, `status`; paged), `GET /haccp/plans/:id`, `GET /haccp/plans/:id/versions/:versionId`, `GET /haccp/logs` (filters `planId`, `ccpCode`, `batchId`, `from`, `to`, `outOfLimits`, `source`, `review` (`pending`, `approved`, `rejected`), `includeCorrected`; newest first; paged; the review queue is `review=pending&includeCorrected=false`), `GET /haccp/logs/:id`, `GET /haccp/deviations`, `GET /haccp/corrective-actions` and `/:id`, `GET /haccp/assets`, `GET /haccp/cleaning`, `GET /haccp/calibration` (filters `assetId`, `from`, `to`; paged). Nothing is in the bootstrap; every write goes to the sync log (`haccp_plan`, `haccp_plan_version`, `haccp_process_step`, `haccp_ccp`, `haccp_ccp_log`, `haccp_corrective_action`, `haccp_asset`, `haccp_cleaning_record`, `haccp_calibration_record`, `haccp_day_signoff`), filtered by module access. The module adds nothing to search; its notifications are below. Corrective actions take attachments (photos, evidence): see [Attachments on module records](#attachments-on-module-records).
- **Who may do what**: reading needs `read`. Drafting plans, the register of areas and equipment, recording readings in the app and working on corrective actions need `write` (API tokens allowed; their readings wait for review). Correcting a reading, a cleaning record or a calibration record and recording or verifying cleaning and calibration are a signed-in person's acts (`write`; correcting an out-of-limit reading needs `manage`). Approving (not the last editor of the draft, unless the only manager), archiving or restoring a plan, reviewing a plan's hazard analysis, reviewing readings, signing off a day (one plan or several) and closing a corrective action are sign-offs: `manage` and a signed-in person. Keeping the hazard library and plan templates is `write` (tokens allowed).
- **MCP**: `get_haccp_plan` (with the hazard review), `draft_haccp_plan`, `get_haccp_library` (hazards or templates), `save_haccp_library_entry`, `start_haccp_plan_from_template`, `list_haccp_records` (with a `review` filter), `list_haccp_deviations`, `get_haccp_due`, `propose_corrective_action`, `record_ccp_readings`. Agents read, draft plan changes, keep the hazard library, start plans from templates, propose or fill in corrective actions and record CCP readings, which await a person's review. `draft_haccp_plan` takes the hazard analysis (`steps`, and `step` on a CCP). They don't review readings or a plan's hazard analysis, record, correct or sign off cleaning, calibration or a day (one plan or several), approve plans or close corrective actions.
- **Web**: the food safety navigation shows managers a Review tab with the number of readings awaiting review; Today's checks says how many there are. Readings show an "Awaiting review" or "Rejected" badge beside the API badge wherever they are listed (latest checks, deviations, the batch panel).

- **Check sheet** (`/<workspace>/haccp`, "Check sheet", `write`): one dialog with a line for every CCP check that is overdue or due (per-batch CCPs get a line per batch in progress). Fill in the readings taken and leave the rest blank; each filled line is sent to the same `POST /haccp/logs` as a single check, one after the other, so every line is judged on the server, an out-of-limit one opens its corrective action (with the "what was done straight away" typed on the line) and nothing new was added to the API. A refused line stays on the sheet with the reason and the others still go in; lines already recorded are locked, so submitting again never records one twice.
- **Notifications** (see [notifications](notifications.md)): `ccp_checks_overdue` (the daily digest above, to `manage`), `reading_out_of_limit` (an out-of-limit reading, also from a correction that opens an action), `deviation_recorded` (a corrective action raised by hand that answers no reading, by a person or an agent's proposal) and `readings_awaiting_review` (a token's reading waits for a person) go to people with `manage` on haccp, never the person who did it. `hazard_review_due` (below) is a sweep notification to the same managers. Review notifications coalesce: a manager with an unread one is not told again until they have read it.
- **Open deviations and production.** Completing a production batch is never blocked by an open corrective action on it. For people who can read haccp, `POST /production/batches/:id/complete` returns `haccpWarning` (`openDeviations` and the first five `actions`; `null` for everyone else, so haccp data does not leave the API for people without access), the batch page shows an alert while the batch runs and in the completion dialog (from the batch's food safety record, same access), and a toast follows the completion.

- **Hazard analysis** (`haccp_process_steps`, on the plan version): the ordered process steps, each with its hazards (type: biological, chemical, physical, allergen; likelihood and severity 1 to 5, risk = product, up to 4 low, up to 12 medium, above that high; control measure; justification) and the Codex decision tree answers Q1 to Q4 (`decisionTreeOutcome`, core: Q1 no is "no control measure", Q2 yes is a CCP, Q2 no then Q3 no is not a CCP, Q3 yes then Q4 yes is not a CCP and Q4 no is a CCP; answers the path doesn't reach are refused). Hazards are stored on the step row (jsonb), so the steps sync as `haccp_process_step`. It is versioned with the plan under the same document control: `PUT /haccp/plans/:id/draft` replaces the draft's `steps` with the CCPs (a step has a client `ref`; a CCP points at it with `stepRef`, stored as `step_id`, and keeps its own `processStep` text), approved and superseded versions never change, and plan reads return `steps` (with each hazard's `risk` and `outcome`), `flow` and `analysisNotes`. `analysisNotes` are warnings for the reviewer and never block approval: a hazard the tree makes a CCP at a step with no CCP, a CCP at a step where no hazard is a CCP, a high-risk hazard undecided or without a control measure. Plans with no steps work as before. Leaving `steps` out of a draft **clears** them (it is the full content, like `ccps`); the MCP tool keeps the current analysis when `steps` is omitted.
- **Process flow diagram**: generated from the ordered steps (`processFlow`, core) and drawn by `ProcessFlow` (`components/process-flow.tsx`) as numbered boxes joined by arrows, CCP steps outlined and tagged with their CCP codes. It is in the plan view and, with the hazard analysis table, in the audit print. There is no separate plan print.
- **Four-eyes on approval**: `approvePlanDraft` refuses (`403 four_eyes`) whoever last edited the draft (`updated_by`), as for SOPs (`canApproveSopVersion`), unless they are the only member with `manage` on haccp. The plan page says so on a draft you last edited.
- **Corrections of cleaning and calibration records** (`POST /haccp/cleaning/:id/correct`, `POST /haccp/calibration/:id/correct`, with a `reason`; a signed-in person, `write`): a new row pointing at the one it corrects (`corrects_record_id`, `correction_reason`; a record is corrected once, `409 already_corrected`), copying what the request leaves out. The original stays, listed as `correctedByRecordId` and struck through on prints and in the web tables, and no longer counts for what's due. A cleaning correction starts unverified (the verifier must differ from whoever is now `doneBy`; a replaced record can't be verified). A calibration correction is judged against the tolerance copied onto the original (not the equipment's current one); correcting a failed calibration needs `manage`, its corrective action stays open for a manager, and a correction that still fails reuses an open action of the chain or opens one. Web: a pencil on each row of the Cleaning and calibration page.
- **Supervisor's sign-off of the day** (`haccp_day_signoffs`; `GET /haccp/signoffs`, `POST /haccp/signoffs` `{ planId, day, note? }`): **per plan per day** (a calendar day in the workspace's time zone), because the monitoring record is a print per plan. `manage` and a signed-in person; one per plan and day (`409 already_signed_off`), never changed, not for a future day, and `409 readings_pending` while that day still has readings awaiting review. It stores how many checks and deviations it covered; checks entered for that day afterwards are shown as "added after". The audit print lists the sign-offs in the range and the days with checks that have none. Web: the "Daily sign-off" card on the plan page.
- **Storage temperature logging and pre-start and allergen changeover checklists are in the `site` module** (storage units and readings, checklist templates and checks, with review and sign-off); they are not duplicated here. See [Site programmes](#site-programmes-module).
- **Hazard analysis review due**: the sweep creates one `hazard_review_due` notification per HACCP manager for each active plan whose `hazard_review_due_on` is today or past (within the sweep's 14-day look-back), dedupe key `hazard_review:<planId>:<dueOn>` (so a review that moves the date makes a new one when that date comes). Overdue and due-today only: it does not warn ahead of the date (the plan shows `soon` from 30 days). It links to the plan page and is hidden once the plan is deleted. See [notifications](notifications.md).
- **CCP checks overdue digest**: the notification sweep creates one `ccp_checks_overdue` notification per HACCP manager per day (dedupe key `ccp_overdue:<date>`) naming the scheduled CCP checks that are overdue by more than one interval (`overdueByMoreThanInterval`, core: an hourly check more than its interval past due, a daily, weekly or monthly one more than its interval past its due date). Per-batch checks and checks never done are left out (the batch page and the check sheet show them), and the digest of a day is created once, by the first sweep that finds something. See [notifications](notifications.md).

- **Hazard library and plan templates** (`haccp_hazard_library`, `haccp_plan_templates`; sync entities `haccp_hazard_library`, `haccp_plan_template`; web `/<workspace>/haccp/library`, "Hazard library" in the navigation). The library holds reusable hazards (title, usual step, type, description, likelihood, severity, control measure, justification and the decision tree answers, validated like a hazard of the analysis: `hazardLibraryEntrySchema`). A template holds a hazard analysis and CCPs (no scope). Both are **copied by value**: adding a library hazard to a step (the "Add from the hazard library" select in the draft editor; `hazardFromLibraryEntry`, core) or starting a plan from a template copies the content into the draft, so editing or deleting an entry or template never changes a plan, an approved version included. Routes: `GET/POST /haccp/library`, `GET/PUT/DELETE /haccp/library/:id` (`PUT` replaces the entry), `GET/POST /haccp/templates`, `GET/PUT/DELETE /haccp/templates/:id`. A template is created from content or, with `fromPlanId`, from a plan's draft (else its version in force; `409 no_content` when it has neither); a plan is started from one with `templateId` on `POST /haccp/plans` (not together with `draft`; its `changeNote` says which template). Deleting is a soft delete. They are reference material, not sign-offs: `write`, API tokens allowed (agents may keep the library; a plan still needs a person's approval before it counts). There is no built-in starter library: the workspace builds its own.
- **Hazard analysis review dates** (`haccp_plans.hazard_review_interval_months`, `hazard_review_due_on`, `hazard_reviewed_on`, `hazard_reviewed_by`, `hazard_review_note`). The hazard analysis is reviewed on its own cycle, apart from the plan's review date (which belongs to each approved version and defaults from `reviewIntervalMonths`). The interval (`hazardReviewIntervalMonths`, set on the plan header, `write`; `null` means the plan's review interval) only decides the default for the next date. **Approving a version that has a hazard analysis counts as a review of it**: it sets the due date to `hazardReviewDueOn` from the request, else the effective date plus the interval (a version with no steps leaves the dates alone; giving `hazardReviewDueOn` for one is `400`). Between approvals a manager records a review with `POST /haccp/plans/:id/hazard-review` `{ nextDueOn?, note? }` (`manage` and a signed-in person; tokens refused, no MCP tool): it needs a hazard analysis in force (`409 no_hazard_analysis`), stamps today, who and the note, and moves the due date to `nextDueOn` (after today) or today plus the interval. Only the latest review is kept on the plan; the change log (sync) keeps each one. Plan reads return `hazardReviewState` (`overdue`, `due`, `soon` within 30 days, `ok`; `null` with no date), `hazardReviewIntervalEffective`, `hazardReviewedByName`; the plans list has a "Hazard review due" column and the plan page a card with "Record a review". The sweep notifies managers (`hazard_review_due`, see below).
- **Four-eyes on approval, and sign-off over several plans.** The approver must not be whoever last edited the draft (above), unless they are the only member with `manage`; the error is `403 four_eyes` ("You last edited this draft, so someone else with manage access must approve it") and the plan page says so. Only the last editor is checked, not everyone who ever touched the draft: otherwise two managers working on one draft could never approve it. A **sign-off of a day over several plans or the whole site** is `POST /haccp/signoffs/bulk` `{ planIds | wholeSite: true, day, note? }` (`manage` and a signed-in person; tokens refused, no MCP tool; "Sign off a day" on the plans page). It makes the same per-plan, per-day sign-offs as `POST /haccp/signoffs` (each stores its own check and deviation counts and is synced and printed per plan), in **one transaction: all or nothing**. It is refused as a whole, writing nothing, for a day in the future, an unknown plan (`404`), a named plan already signed off for that day (`409 already_signed_off`, `details.planIds`) and any plan whose day still has readings awaiting review (`409 readings_pending`, `details.planIds` and `details.pending`). `wholeSite` covers every active plan with a version in force on that day; plans already signed off for the day are left as they are and listed in `skipped` (so a site sign-off works after one plan was signed on its own), `409 already_signed_off` when nothing is left to sign and `409 no_plans_in_force` when no plan applied. The response is `{ signoffs, signedOff, skipped }`. Both new person-only routes are in `personOnlyActions` (core) and `route-classification.test.ts`.

Not built yet: a built-in starter hazard library, a history of hazard analysis reviews (only the latest is on the plan), a four-eyes rule for the hazard analysis review itself, and notifications ahead of a hazard review falling due (it notifies once it is due). The audit record is a print per plan (see [Printing and exports](#printing-and-exports)).

## People module

The staff register that training and attendance build on. Code: `packages/core/src/people.ts`, `packages/db/src/schema/people.ts`, `apps/server/src/{services,routes}/people.ts`, `apps/mcp/src/tools/people.ts`, and the web page `/<workspace>/people`.

- **Staff** are people, not users: most never sign in.
  - **Fields**: first and last name, job title, department, start and end dates, status (`active` or `leaver`), a payroll reference (when the payroll package uses a different employee number), and an optional link to one workspace member. Changing that link needs `manage` and a signed-in person (it decides whose hours `/attendance/me` returns and whose training counts as your own at sign-off).
  - **No sensitive HR data**: no NI number, date of birth, address or pay. The payroll package keeps those.
- **Staff numbers** are exactly six digits, stored as text so leading zeros survive.
  - **Never reused**: they are unique per workspace across every row ever created, including leavers and deleted rows, because payroll history refers to them.
  - **Default**: without one, a new person gets one above the highest in use, starting at `100001`.
  - **Fixed once used**: a person with training or attendance records can't be renumbered (`409 staff_number_fixed`).
- **Leavers**: becoming a leaver sets the end date (today unless given), and coming back clears it. Leavers can't use the kiosk. Deleting is for mistakes only; once training or attendance exist, those modules will refuse to delete someone with records.
- **Job roles**: a name (unique case-insensitively) and a description, active or archived. A person can hold several. Training requirements will hang on them.
  - **Deleting**: a role some course requires can't be deleted (`409 job_role_in_use`), because the matrix would stop requiring that course. Archive it instead; archived roles still count in the matrix.
- **Kiosk PINs** are six digits.
  - **Storage**: HMAC-SHA-256 under a key derived from `BETTER_AUTH_SECRET`, salted with the workspace and staff ids, so a database dump alone can't be brute-forced. Changing the secret invalidates every PIN.
  - **Rejected PINs**: one digit repeated, straight runs, repeated pairs or triples, and the person's own staff number (`isWeakPin`).
  - **Who sets them**: a manager in person (`manage`, signed in; API tokens refused, no MCP tool). `PUT /people/staff/:id/pin` with `{ "pin": "…" }` sets one, and without a body it generates one. The PIN is returned once, in that response only. `DELETE …/pin` removes it.
  - **Lock-out**: five wrong PINs in a row lock the number for 15 minutes. A locked number is refused even with the right PIN. `POST …/unlock` lifts the lock, and a right PIN resets the count.
  - **Never leaves the database**: the hash and the counters are never in a response or the sync log. Responses carry `hasPin`, `pinSetAt`, `pinSetBy` and `pinLockedUntil`, the last only while the lock is in force.
  - **Kiosk side**: `verifyStaffPin` and `changeOwnStaffPin` in the service are what the kiosk will call.
- **Access**:
  - `read`: the register and job roles.
  - `write`: add and edit staff and roles. API tokens are allowed, so an agent can load a staff list.
  - `manage`: PINs.
- **Reading**:
  - `GET /people/staff`: filters `q` (name, staff number, payroll reference, job title, department), `status` (default `active`; `all`), `jobRoleId` and `department`; paged with `total`.
  - `GET /people/staff/:id`.
  - `GET /people/staff-number`: the next free number.
  - `GET /people/job-roles`: with active staff counts.

  Staff are not in the bootstrap. Writes go to the sync log (`staff`, `job_role`, `staff_job_role`), filtered by module access.

- **CSV import** (`POST /people/staff/import`, `write`; API tokens allowed): CSV text parsed in core by `parseStaffCsv`, with the same dry run, preview and all-or-nothing commit as the items import. People are matched by **staff number** (exactly six digits; keep the column as text so spreadsheets don't drop leading zeros). `first name` and `last name` are needed for a new person; optional `job title`, `department`, `job roles` (existing, active role names separated by `;`, which replace the person's roles; an empty cell means none; an unknown or archived role is an error, roles are never created), `start date`, `end date` (`2026-01-05` or `05/01/2026`), `payroll reference` and `status` (`leaver` without an end date records today, as in the app). A missing column leaves people alone; an empty cell clears the value except names and status. Because the number is the key, an import never changes one: the rule that a number is fixed once a person has training or attendance records holds by construction (a different number is a new person), and a number that belonged to a removed person is an error (numbers are never reused). **Never imported**: kiosk PINs (a `PIN` column is ignored and reported, and an existing PIN is untouched) and the link to a login (`manage`, a person only).
- **MCP**: `list_staff`, `create_staff`, `update_staff`, `list_job_roles`, `create_job_role`, `import_staff_csv`. Agents never see or set a PIN.

A person's training is linked from the People page.

## Training module

Courses, which job roles need them, and who has been trained. Needs `people`; uses `sops` when it is active. Code: `packages/core/src/training.ts`, `packages/db/src/schema/training.ts`, `apps/server/src/{services,routes}/training.ts`, `apps/mcp/src/tools/training.ts`, and the web pages under `/<workspace>/training` (Matrix, Due, Sign-off, Courses, and `/training/staff/<id>`).

- **Courses**:
  - **Fields**: a code (unique case-insensitively), title, kind (`sop`, `induction`, `on_the_job`, `external`, `certificate`), validity in months (none: never expires), whether a competency sign-off is needed (default yes), active or archived.
  - **SOP courses**: an `sop` course names an SOP, and only `sop` courses do. Once anyone has been trained on a course, its SOP can't change.
  - **Deleting**: only a course nobody has been trained on can be deleted. Archive the others.
- **Requirements** (`PUT /training/courses/:id/requirements`): which job roles need a course, each with an optional window in days from the person's start date. Together they make the matrix. Several roles needing the same course take the tightest window.
- **Records** are append-only:
  - **Recording**: `POST /training/records` records one session (a course, a date, an outcome, a trainer member or a free-text trainer, notes) for up to 200 people at once. Dates can't be in the future, people must be active, and the course must be active.
  - **SOP version and expiry**: an SOP course's record names the SOP's current approved version (`409 sop_not_approved` if there is none). The expiry date is set when recorded: completed date plus the course's validity at the time.
  - **Sign-off** (`POST /training/records/sign-off` with `recordIds`): needs `manage` and a signed-in person (API tokens refused, no MCP tool). It is all or nothing. Every record must be live, `competent`, not yet signed, and on a course that needs a sign-off, and nobody signs off their own training (a staff record linked to their login: `403 own_training`). It records who and when.
  - **Void** (`POST /training/records/:id/void` with a reason, `manage`, signed in): for a record made in error. It records who, when and why. The record is kept and shown, and never counts again.
  - **Nothing else changes a record**, and people with records can't be deleted (`409 staff_has_records`; mark them as leavers instead).
- **Status per person and course** (`trainingStatus` in core) is derived on every read, never stored:
  - **How it's worked out**: from the latest record that counts (not voided, not `not_yet_competent`, and for sign-off courses `competent` and signed off).
  - **The statuses**: `current`; `expiring` within 30 days; `expired`; `outdated` (an SOP course trained on a version that is no longer the approved one); `pending_sign_off`; `missing` (required, never trained; `overdue` past its due date); `not_required`.
  - **Takes effect at once**: approving a new SOP version, or changing a requirement, changes standings immediately with no extra write.
  - **"Today"**: today in the workspace's time zone.
- **Reading**:
  - `GET /training/matrix`: rows are active staff; columns are courses anyone shown needs or has trained on, or every active course with `allCourses=true`. Filters `jobRoleId`, `department`, `courseId` and `status` (people with at least one course in that status), with a count per status.
  - `GET /training/due?withinDays=`: missing, expired, outdated, awaiting sign-off and expiring within the window. Overdue first.
  - `GET /training/staff/:staffId`: standings and full history, voided records included.
  - `GET /training/records`: filters `staffId`, `courseId`, `signOff=pending`, `includeVoided`; paged.
  - `GET /training/courses`: SOP codes and titles only for callers who can read SOPs.
- **Access**:
  - `read`: everything above.
  - `write`: record training. API tokens are allowed, so an agent can log who attended.
  - `manage`: courses and requirements, plus sign-off and void (those two in person only).
- **Sync**: `training_course`, `training_requirement` and `training_record` go to the sync log, filtered by module access. Not in the bootstrap.
- **MCP**: `list_training_courses`, `get_training_matrix`, `list_training_due`, `get_staff_training`, `record_training`. There are no sign-off or void tools.

- **SOP read-and-acknowledge counts as training** (both `sops` and `training` active; otherwise nothing happens). A course is linked to its SOP by `sopId`. When a member acknowledges the SOP's current approved version (in the app or at the kiosk), `acknowledgeSop` appends an ordinary training record in the same transaction for each active `sop` course on that SOP, for the active staff record linked to that login (`recordSopAcknowledgementTraining`): completed today, naming the version, expiry from the course's validity, trainer "Read and acknowledged". It keeps records append-only and sign-off rules intact: a course that needs a competency sign-off gets a `competent` record that still waits for a manager (never the person themselves), any other course gets an `attended` record that counts at once. One record per person, course and version (a voided one is not recreated). A new approved version makes it `outdated` until acknowledged again. People without a staff record or a login are not covered.
- **Back-fill**: acknowledgements of the SOP's current approved version made before the course was linked (or reactivated) are turned into training records by `backfillCourseFromAcknowledgements`, in the same transaction, when a `sop` course is created or updated to a new SOP (sops active), and on demand with `POST /training/courses/:id/backfill` (`manage`; returns `{ created, skipped }`). Same rules as live: active staff record linked to the acknowledging login, dated the acknowledgement's day in the workspace's time zone, expiry from validity, trainer "Read and acknowledged", `competent` awaiting sign-off or `attended`, recorded by the acknowledging person, one per person, course and version (a voided one is not recreated, so re-running adds nothing). Older versions are not back-filled. On the same day the record on the current SOP version wins in `trainingStatus`.

(Training falling due now notifies; see [notifications](notifications.md).)

## Attendance module

Clocking in and out on a tablet kiosk, shifts and payroll, the on-site register for evacuations, and visitors. Needs `people`. Code: `packages/core/src/attendance.ts`, `packages/db/src/schema/attendance.ts`, `apps/server/src/{services,routes}/attendance.ts`, `services/attendance-kiosk.ts` (badges, the offline queue, prompts, visitor hosts) and `routes/kiosk.ts`, `apps/mcp/src/tools/attendance.ts`, the pages under `/<workspace>/attendance` (On site, Shifts, Payroll, Settings & kiosks) and the tablet page `/kiosk`.

- **Privacy**: clock times and visitor names are personal data. Like private calendar events, nothing in this module is in the sync log or the bootstrap (`syncEntities: []`); pages read it through the endpoints below.
- **Clock events** (`attendance_events`) are append-only:
  - **Fields**: person, `in` or `out`, the time, the source (`kiosk` with its device, or `manager` with who added it and a required reason).
  - **Corrections**: a wrong event is voided (who, when, why: kept, shown, never counted). A missed one is added by someone with `write`, in person; API tokens are refused unless an owner or admin allows `attendance.events` under Settings → API access (for example, events from another clocking system). There is no MCP tool.
  - **Your own record**: nobody adds or voids clock events on their own record (`403 own_attendance`), unless they are the only member with `manage` on attendance. This holds for an API token too: the token acts as its owner.
  - **Deleting people**: anyone with events can't be deleted (`409 staff_has_records`).
- **Shifts** (`buildShifts` in core) are derived on read, in the workspace's time zone:
  - **Pairing**: each `in` pairs with the next `out`, and a shift's day is the local date of its clock-in, so night shifts are fine.
  - **Problems**: an `in` followed by another `in` is a missed clock-out; an `out` with no `in` is a missed clock-in; a pair longer than the maximum shift is flagged; an `in` still open within the maximum shift means the person is on site. Shifts with a problem pay nothing until fixed.
  - **Paid minutes**: clock times rounded to the nearest 0, 5, 10 or 15 minutes (payroll only; raw times are kept), less the unpaid break when the shift is long enough. Overtime is the minutes past a weekly threshold, per ISO week (Monday start). Weeks are counted whole: hours worked earlier in a week that started before the period fill up that week's threshold first, so a week split across two monthly pay periods loses no overtime.
- **Settings** (one row per workspace; defaults until saved): rounding (shift days and pay periods use the workspace's time zone, shown read-only here), break after N hours and its length, maximum shift (default 16 hours), weekly overtime threshold, default payroll format, the pay period (below), the Sage payment references, visitor retention in months (default 24; older visits, and refused offline visitor entries, which name the visitor, are deleted when visitors are next written or listed), whether a badge still needs the PIN (default yes), how old an offline kiosk entry may be (default 72 hours, at most 168), and the site rules visitors accept (none by default).
- **Payroll export**:
  - **Pay period** (settings `payPeriodKind` and `payPeriodAnchor`, `payPeriod*` in core `pay-periods.ts`): `weekly`, `fortnightly` (every 2 weeks), `four_weekly` or `monthly`, default monthly. The weekly kinds repeat from the anchor, the first day of any one period (a Monday, say; required). A monthly period starts on the anchor's day of the month and ends the day before the next (anchor day 26: 26th to 25th; none, or day 1: calendar months); a day past the end of a short month starts that month's period on its last day. Settings & kiosks offers "Month starts on day" for monthly and a date for the others. Periods are local dates in the workspace's time zone and never longer than 31 days, so they fit the 93-day limit; an unusable setting (a weekly kind with no anchor) is refused with `400`.
  - **Default period**: the **last finished** pay period (`lastFinishedPayPeriod`). The payroll page opens on it and has Previous, Next and "Last finished" buttons while the dates are a whole period (typing other dates still works and marks them "custom"). `GET /attendance/payroll` and `payroll.csv` take `from` and `to` together or neither: with neither they use that period (the response and the file name say which); one without the other is `400`.
  - **Preview**: `GET /attendance/payroll?from=&to=&format=` shows per-person shifts, basic and overtime minutes, the shifts to fix first, and warnings and problems.
  - **File**: `GET /attendance/payroll.csv` downloads it (API tokens are refused unless `attendance.payroll_export` is allowed under Settings → API access, as it holds personal data; needs `manage`), refused with `409 payroll_export_blocked` while there are problems (missing Sage references).
  - **Formats**: the `payrollFormats` registry in core builds the format drop-down:
    - `generic`: one row per person with staff number, payroll reference, names, period, shifts, and basic, overtime and total hours.
    - `sage50`: Sage 50 Payroll hours by payment type, one row per person and payment, with `Employee Reference` (the payroll reference, else the staff number), `Payment Reference` (from settings) and `Hours` (2 decimal places).
  - **`sage50` is marked unverified**: its column headers come from Sage's import conventions but haven't been checked against the template exported from a real Sage 50 install. The page warns until that check and a test import have been done. To add a package, add an entry to the registry with a test.
- **On-site register** (`GET /attendance/on-site`):
  - **On site**: staff whose latest event is `in` within the maximum shift, and visitors signed in within 24 hours.
  - **May have left**: older open ones are listed apart as `possiblyLeft`.
  - **Page**: refreshes every 30 seconds (every 10 during a roll call, so marshals see each other's ticks) and prints.
- **Roll calls**:
  - **Starting**: `POST /attendance/roll-calls` with `drill` or `real` freezes everyone on site plus the "may have left" list. One runs at a time (`409 roll_call_open`; starts are serialised per workspace).
  - **During**: each entry is marked `safe`, `missing` or left `unaccounted`, with who and when.
  - **Ending**: `…/end` closes it. Ended roll calls are read-only and kept as evidence.
  - **Who can run them**: members with `read` (fire marshals; see `READ_LEVEL_WRITES`), in person.
- **Visitors**: sign in at the kiosk with first and last name, and optionally their company and who they are visiting (the host: an active member of staff or a workspace member who isn't a guest, picked by name, kept with the name as it was; see Host search). When the workspace has site rules, the visitor must tick that they accept them (`400 site_rules_required`), and the visit keeps the rules as accepted (and their SHA-256) and when. A sign-in queued offline is recorded against the rules and host the kiosk showed (see Offline queue). They sign out with their name: the most recent open visit with that name, case-insensitively (`404 visitor_not_found` otherwise). The on-site register, the Shifts page and roll calls show company and host (a roll call entry reads "Visitor · Engines plc · visiting Grace Hopper"). Someone with `write` can sign out a visitor who left without doing it; API tokens are refused unless `attendance.visit_sign_out` is allowed under Settings → API access.
  - **Host told on arrival** (`visitor_arrived`): when a visitor names a host with a login (a workspace member, or a staff host linked to one who is still an active member), the host gets an Inbox entry and an email (unless they turned the type off): "Linus Visitor (Engines plc) has arrived to see you". It is created in the sign-in's transaction, once per visit (`visitor:<visitId>`), so a retry or a replay with the same client id adds nothing. **Decisions**: the host is told whether or not they can read attendance (hosts rarely have it, and the entry holds only the visitor's name and company), so the type isn't tied to the module and shows in their email preferences while the module is on. A sign-in queued offline notifies only if it is replayed within 30 minutes of when the visitor signed in (`VISITOR_NOTIFY_MAX_AGE_MINUTES`): the visit is always recorded, but "your visitor has arrived" hours later would mislead. Hosts without a login (staff with no linked member), and visitors naming nobody, notify no one. The notifications go on the visits' retention schedule (deleted with the visits' period, see Settings).
  - **Visitor pass** (`/<workspace>/attendance/visits/<id>/print`, `GET /attendance/visits/:id`, needs `write`): a print-ready page in the style of the other print pages (`PrintPage`, the browser's print dialog): a cut-out card with VISITOR, the name and company, who they visit, the date and sign-in time, a line about signing out and the fire assembly point, and below it whether and when they accepted the site rules. A visit already signed out prints "no longer valid". The Shifts page lists a "Pass" button for each visitor. There is no pass printing at the kiosk itself (the tablet has no printer); a receptionist prints from the app.
- **Your own times**: `GET /attendance/me?from=&to=` returns the shifts of the staff record linked to the caller (needs `read`). A period is at most 93 days, like shifts and payroll.
- **Missed clock-outs notify** (`missed_clock_out`, the sweep, see [notifications](notifications.md)): once a clock-in is older than the maximum shift with no clock-out (the `missing_out` shifts the payroll preview says pay nothing), the sweep tells everyone with `write` on attendance, who can add the missing event, and the person's own login if it can read attendance (their copy says to ask a manager: nobody fixes their own record). One each per clock-in (`clock_out:<clock-in event id>`); adding the clock-out or voiding the clock-in stops it, and later misses are new. Only clock-ins from the last 14 days (`NOTIFY_LOOKBACK_DAYS`) are looked at, and the module's first sweep is seeded without email. A clock-in followed at once by another clock-in waits for the maximum shift too. Overlong shifts that did get a clock-out (`too_long`) are not notified: they are on the Shifts page.
- **Access**:
  - `read`: on-site register, roll calls, your own times.
  - `write`: everyone's shifts and visitors, adding missed events.
  - `manage`: voids, payroll, settings, kiosks and badges.
  - **API tokens**: they can read shifts, the on-site register, settings, kiosks, badges, refused offline entries and the payroll preview. Clocking, roll calls, voids, settings changes, kiosk pairing, issuing or revoking badges and dismissing refused offline entries are always refused. Manual clock events, visitor sign-outs and the payroll file are refused unless an owner or admin allows `attendance.events`, `attendance.visit_sign_out` or `attendance.payroll_export` under Settings → API access (none has an MCP tool).

### The kiosk

- **Pairing**: someone with `manage` adds a kiosk (Settings & kiosks) and gets an 8-character pairing code, valid once for 15 minutes. The tablet opens `/kiosk` and enters it (`POST /v1/public/kiosk/pair`, rate-limited with the other public routes). It gets a device credential (`knk_…`) kept in that browser's local storage. Codes and credentials are stored as SHA-256 hashes.
  - **Replacing or removing**: a new pairing code replaces a lost tablet (the old credential stops working). Removing a kiosk revokes it. The list shows when each kiosk was last seen.
- **What the credential can do**: it is accepted only on `/v1/kiosk/*`, and sessions and personal tokens are refused there (`401 device_required`; `route-classification.test.ts` checks every kiosk route). It can identify a person by staff number and PIN (or badge), clock them in or out, let them change their PIN, sign visitors in and out (and look up hosts by name; see Host search), replay what it queued offline, and show a person who has just clocked in what is due for them. It can't read anyone's times or who is on site.
- **Staff flow**:
  1. Staff number, then PIN, on a large keypad. A physical keyboard works too. Or **Scan badge**, then the PIN (see Badges).
  2. The kiosk then shows "Good morning, Grace" (first name only) with the expected action as the big button (out while clocked in within the maximum shift, otherwise in) and the other as a smaller one.
  3. A confirmation, then back to the start after a few seconds. After a clock-in, what is due for them comes first (see Prompts at clock-in). Abandoned screens reset after 30 seconds (two minutes on the SOP prompts, matching the prompt token).
- **Repeated taps**: a second clock of the same kind within two minutes returns the first event (double taps and retries). The kiosk also sends a client id with each clock and visitor sign-in or sign-out: a retry with the same id returns what the first one did, however late.
- **PIN mistakes**: unknown numbers, people without a PIN and wrong PINs all get the same answer (`401 pin_invalid`). Five wrong PINs lock a number for 15 minutes (`423 pin_locked`). A kiosk with 30 wrong PINs in 5 minutes is throttled (`429`). Leavers are refused after a correct PIN (`403 staff_inactive`).
- **PIN handling**: PINs are held in the tablet's memory only for the seconds a clock-in takes.
- **Time**: the event time is the server's; the tablet's clock is only used for entries queued offline (below).
- **Reserved slug**: `/kiosk` is a top-level route, so `kiosk` is reserved as a workspace slug. Migration `0022_reserve_kiosk_slug` renames any workspace using it, like `0007`. The slug test now checks the latest reserved-slug migration against `RESERVED_WORKSPACE_SLUGS`.

- **Host search** (`GET /v1/kiosk/hosts?q=`): any paired tablet can ask, so it is built not to list the directory. It needs at least three characters (`KIOSK_HOST_QUERY_MIN`) and matches the **start** of a first or last name (`%` and `_` match literally), returns at most eight names and nothing else (no department), never offers guests or deleted accounts, and is limited to 30 searches a minute per kiosk (`429 rate_limited`, the shared in-process limiter keyed on the device). A member linked to an active member of staff appears once, as that person; one linked to a leaver is found as a member.

- **Starting without the network** (`apps/web/src/app/kiosk/sw-source.ts`, served at `/kiosk/sw.js`): the kiosk registers a service worker so a tablet that restarts or reloads while the network is down still opens the page and can queue entries (the queue below does the rest; the pairing and the server's key are already in the tablet's local storage).
  - **Scope, strictly**: registered for `/kiosk` (`Service-Worker-Allowed: /kiosk` on the script, as the default scope of `/kiosk/sw.js` would exclude the page itself). The worker answers only a navigation to exactly `/kiosk`, and the content-hashed `/_next/static/*` files when a page at `/kiosk` asks (any other page under the same URL prefix gets the network). It never calls `respondWith` for anything else, in particular not for `/api/*`: the kiosk's ten-second timeout, its `isOfflineError` detection and the offline queue see real network failures, exactly as without it.
  - **Page**: network-first, stored copy after four seconds or on any failure or error status. Only a successful, non-redirected HTML response is stored. The page is static and holds nothing about anyone. The files named in its HTML and data (scripts, styles, fonts, lazy chunks such as the badge scanner) are stored with it, and a new build's set replaces the old one only after all of it is stored.
  - **Where it works**: browsers allow service workers on HTTPS or localhost only, which is also what sealing offline entries needs; over plain HTTP on a LAN address the kiosk works online as before. Production builds only (development would hide code changes). A webview without service workers just loads from the network. It adds no Tauri IPC and no capability: the desktop app's rules are untouched. Tablets need one online visit to the page after this release to have it stored; updating the worker needs no action (the script is never cached).

#### Offline queue

- **What is queued**: when the kiosk can't reach the server (no network, the API down, or a request taking over ten seconds), clock-ins, clock-outs and visitor sign-ins and sign-outs are kept in the tablet's local storage, each with the time it happened and a random client id. The header shows "Offline", how many entries wait to be sent, when they were last sent, and how many the server refused. The queue is sent every 20 seconds while anything waits, and as soon as the browser is back online (`POST /v1/kiosk/replay`, up to 50 at a time).
- **Offline PINs (the decision)**: the tablet can't check a PIN without the PIN hashes, and handing it those would let anyone with the tablet brute-force them (six digits). So the kiosk **queues unverified entries and the server checks the PIN when it replays them**. To keep the PIN off the tablet's storage, the credential (staff number or badge, and PIN) and visitors' details are **sealed to the server's public key** before they are stored: ECDH P-256 with a fresh key per entry, HKDF-SHA-256 and AES-256-GCM, bound to the entry's client id, type and time. The key pair is derived from `BETTER_AUTH_SECRET`, so every API instance has it and nothing is stored; the public key comes with `GET /v1/kiosk/session`. Only the server can open an entry; a stolen tablet holds no PIN or name anyone could read. Sealing needs WebCrypto, so it needs HTTPS (or localhost): over plain HTTP the kiosk says it can't keep entries offline.
- **What the person sees offline**: after their number and PIN (or badge), "The kiosk is offline" with Clock in and Clock out side by side (the kiosk can't know which is expected, nor their name). A mistyped PIN is only found on replay: the entry is then refused and a manager sees it.
- **Idempotent**: every entry is keyed on its client id (unique on clock events, visit sign-ins and sign-outs, and refusals). A retry returns the same answer (`duplicate` or the same refusal) and never clocks twice or counts a wrong PIN twice.
- **Original times, bounded**: the event keeps the time it happened, corrected for the tablet's clock.
  - **An authenticated correction**: `GET /v1/kiosk/session` returns the server's `now`; the kiosk keeps the difference from its own clock (`clockOffsetMs`, measured at the middle of the round trip) and **seals it into every entry** it queues, so the correction is as tamper-proof as the entry. The batch's `sentAt` is sent in the clear and is no longer trusted: changing it can't move a sealed entry. A kiosk that never reached `/session` seals 0 (no correction).
  - **Capped**: the correction is at most five minutes (`KIOSK_MAX_CLOCK_CORRECTION_MINUTES`). An entry from a tablet further out is **refused** (`clock_skew`, with how far out the clock was), not flagged: a manager checks the tablet's time and adds the entry by hand if it's right. Refusing keeps the ledger free of times nobody can vouch for.
  - **Entries queued before this change** (no sealed offset) fall back to the old `sentAt` difference, but only within the same five-minute cap; beyond it they are refused as `clock_skew` too.
  - **Window**: an entry older than the workspace's replay window (default 72 hours, `offlineReplayHours`) or more than five minutes in the future is refused. Events are marked `offline`, and visits `offline` / `signedOutOffline`; the Shifts page says "saved offline".
- **Checked against the ledger**: entries in a batch are applied in time order. A clock of the same kind within two minutes of a recorded one is a duplicate (the person also clocked on another kiosk). A clock-in while already clocked in, or one that a later clock of the same kind within the maximum shift already covers (someone fixed the shift by hand), contradicts the ledger and is refused (`replay_conflict`, with what it contradicts). Wrong PINs, locked numbers, unknown or revoked badges and leavers are refused as at the keypad, and count towards the lock-out and the kiosk's throttle (when it is hit, the remaining clock entries are `deferred` with `kiosk_throttled` and sent at the next try; visitor entries still go through). Replays of the same entry are serialised: each entry is replayed in a transaction holding an advisory lock on the workspace and client id, taken before the PIN is checked, so one replayed twice at once (two tabs) counts a wrong PIN once.
- **Two offline kiosks, out of order**: someone clocks in on kiosk A and out on kiosk B while both are offline, and B gets through first. A clock-out with no open clock-in before it is therefore **not** refused: it is `deferred` with `awaiting_clock_in`, nothing is stored, and the kiosk keeps it, waits two minutes (`retryAt` on the queued entry, `DEFERRED_RETRY_MS`) and sends it again, while the rest of the queue carries on. Once A's clock-in has arrived it fits and is accepted. A clock-out whose clock-in never comes is retried until it is older than the replay window, and is then refused as `replay_too_old` for a manager to deal with. A visitor sign-out needs an open visit with that name that began before it.
- **Offline visitor sign-ins are recorded, whatever changed since**: a person physically on site must never be missing from the on-site register or a roll call, so a replayed sign-in is judged against what the kiosk showed, not the settings at replay time. The kiosk seals the site rules it showed (`siteRules`, null when there were none) and the host's name as shown. On replay:
  - **Rules added or changed while offline**: the visit is still recorded. It keeps the text of the rules shown (sealed in full, up to 4,000 characters, so the sealed field allows 24,000) and their SHA-256 (`attendance_visits.site_rules_hash`), and the acceptance time when the visitor ticked them; a visitor who didn't is recorded without it rather than dropped. A kiosk that showed no rules records none. Entries queued before kiosks sealed the hash are recorded too, without the rules text.
  - **Host left since**: the visit keeps only the host's name as the kiosk showed it, with no link, flagged `hostMissing` (the on-site register returns it, and the Shifts page says to check who they visited).
  - Live sign-ins are unchanged: they still need today's rules accepted (`site_rules_required`) and a current host (`host_not_found`).
- **Refusals**: each one is kept (`attendance_kiosk_rejections`: type, time, code, message, the person when known, or the unknown staff number or visitor name) and listed under Settings & kiosks for anyone with `write`. Refused visitor entries name the visitor, so they are purged with visits after the retention period (`GET /attendance/kiosk-rejections`). A manager adds the missed event by hand if it was real, then dismisses it (`POST …/:id/dismiss`). Dismissing is a person's act: refused wrong PINs are a security record, so API tokens are refused (`forbidTokens`, listed as "Dismiss refused kiosk events" under Settings → API access).

#### Badges

- **What a badge is**: a printable card with a QR code holding `knb:` and 16 random characters (80 bits) from the pairing alphabet, and the same code in groups of four for typing. It holds nothing about the person: not the staff number, never the PIN. Only its SHA-256 hash is stored, with the last four characters to match a printed card to its row.
- **Issuing** (`POST /attendance/badges` with `staffId`, `manage`, signed in; API tokens refused): the code is returned once, for printing (the browser's print dialog, credit-card size). One live badge per person: issuing a new one (a lost card, a reprint) revokes the old. **Revoking** (`DELETE /attendance/badges/:id`) stops it at once, also for entries queued offline before the revocation. `GET /attendance/badges` lists active staff with their badge (issued when and by whom, last used: the last scan that let the person in, not refused or abandoned ones).
- **At the kiosk**: **Scan badge** uses the camera through the browser's `BarcodeDetector` (Chrome and Edge on Android and ChromeOS, Safari 17+); where there is none, or no camera, the code is typed (a handheld scanner that types works too).
- **Badge plus PIN (the decision)**: by default a badge only replaces typing the staff number, and the PIN is still asked (`401 pin_required` without it). A card can be lost or lent, and the PIN is what makes a clock-in the person's own. A workspace can switch `badgeRequiresPin` off for speed; then the badge alone clocks in and out, but SOPs can't be acknowledged at the kiosk without the PIN.

#### Prompts at clock-in

- **What**: a clock-in (`POST /v1/kiosk/clock` with `kind: in`) returns `prompts`: training that has expired, is outdated, is missing and overdue or due within 30 days, or expires within 30 days (from the training matrix, `training` module on); and SOPs to read (`sops` module on). The kiosk shows them before going back to the start.
- **SOPs follow the SOP module's model**: acknowledgements belong to members, so SOP prompts are for staff linked to a workspace member who can read SOPs, and list the current approved versions that member hasn't acknowledged (the newest ten, with the total). Staff without a login get training prompts only.
- **Acknowledging at the kiosk**: when the PIN was checked (not a badge alone), the response carries a prompt token (HMAC-signed, for that person and kiosk). With it the kiosk reads the SOP's current version (`POST /v1/kiosk/sops/:id/read`) and records "read and understood" for the linked member (`POST /v1/kiosk/sops/:id/acknowledge`), with `source: kiosk` on the acknowledgement.
- **Short-lived token (the decision)**: the token lasts **two minutes** (`PROMPT_TOKEN_MINUTES`), and every read or acknowledgement answers with a renewed one for another two minutes, which the kiosk uses next. So someone reading and acknowledging SOPs keeps going, but the next person at the kiosk can't use the last person's token once they have walked off: the prompts screen also returns to the start after two minutes without a tap. (A single-use token per acknowledgement would still leave the rest of the list open to the next person, and needs state shared between API instances.) The same rules apply as in the app: once per version, the current version only.
- **Training only informs**: sign-off stays a manager's act with `manage`; the kiosk asks the person to speak to their manager.
- **Modules**: no training prompts with `training` off, no SOP prompts with `sops` off.

- `get_attendance_shifts`, `who_is_on_site` (read-only; visitors with their company and who they are visiting).

## Quality module

Complaints, non-conformances, internal audits and mock recall tests: the quality records a BRCGS audit asks for beyond HACCP. Needs no other module; `items` and `stock` enhance it (`enhancedBy`). With them, complaints and non-conformances can name a product, lot or supplier, and mock recall tests, complaint trends, sales volumes, supplier standing and authenticity assessments are available; without them (off, or not readable by the member) those links are refused and left out of responses, those parts answer `404 module_inactive` / `403 module_forbidden`, the summary's `recallTest` is `null`, and the web app hides their tabs and pickers. Code: `packages/core/src/quality.ts`, `packages/db/src/schema/quality.ts`, `apps/server/src/{services,routes}/quality.ts`, `apps/mcp/src/tools/quality.ts`, and the web pages under `/<workspace>/quality` (Overview, Complaints, Non-conformances, Audits, Recall tests).

- **References**: every record gets one, `CMP-` (complaint), `NC-` (non-conformance), `IA-` (internal audit) or `MR-` (mock recall), then the year of its date and a sequence that restarts each year (`CMP-2026-007`). Unique in the workspace and never reused; it keeps the year it was given if the date is later edited. Every write takes the workspace's sync-log lock before reading the record's status, so a close and an edit, or a completion and a cancellation, can't interleave. Nothing is deleted except a planned audit with no findings: complaints and non-conformances are closed and reopened, audits completed or cancelled, recall tests voided.
- **Complaints** (`quality_complaints`): received date (not in the future), source (consumer, customer, retailer, regulator, internal), category (foreign body, allergen, illness, microbiological, quality, labelling, packaging, quantity, other), severity (minor, major, critical), the product and lot when known (a lot must be a lot of that item, and a lot given alone sets the item; the web offers empty lots too, latest first, with a search for older ones), the lot code as reported, the customer account, the complainant's name and contact details, the description, then the investigation, root cause, response and whether it was justified. **A critical complaint, or a major one about food safety (foreign body, allergen, illness, microbiological), opens a non-conformance in the same transaction** (`complaintOpensNonconformance`), whoever logs it; and so does raising a complaint's severity or category to that level later, unless one was already raised from it. Status: `open` ↔ `investigating` is `write`; **closing** (`POST …/close`) needs `manage` and a signed-in person, and the response to the complainant on record; **reopening** (`POST …/reopen`, with a reason) the same. A closed complaint can't be edited. The complainant's name and contact details are left out of the sync log, and the contact details are never returned to an API token (they may record them), so the MCP tools never see them.
- **Non-conformances** (`quality_nonconformances`): raised date, source (complaint, audit, supplier, process, product, other; `complaint` and `audit` exactly when linked to one), severity, title, description, the item, lot or supplier concerned, containment, root cause, corrective and preventive action, an owner (a member) and a due date (default by severity: critical 7 days, major 28, minor 56; never before the raised date, and it can be moved but not removed). Links are checked when they change, so a non-conformance whose owner has left can still be edited. `open` → `action_taken` needs the corrective action (`write`, API tokens allowed); **closing is verifying that the action worked**: `manage`, a signed-in person, the status `action_taken`, the root cause on record and a verification note. Moving back to `open` clears when the action was taken. Reopening is the same person's act, with a reason, and clears the earlier verification. Overdue (derived): not closed and past the due date.
- **Internal audits** (`quality_audits`): title, scope (an area, a process, the clauses covered), planned date, an auditor (a member, or a name). While planned it can be edited, cancelled (with a reason) or, with no findings, deleted (`write`). **Completing** (`POST …/complete`) is the auditor's record in person (API tokens refused): the member auditor or whoever heads quality, meaning anyone with `manage` on the module, signs it off (`canCompleteAudit`, else `403 not_auditor`). An outside auditor can't sign in, so someone with `manage` records their audit. It takes the date (not in the future), a summary and up to 100 findings (due no earlier than the completion date), **each of which becomes a non-conformance** with source `audit` in the same transaction. State (derived): overdue once the planned date has passed, `soon` within 14 days.
- **Mock recall tests** (`quality_recall_tests`): a lot traced (forward, backward or both) and reconciled against the clock. Recorded in person (`write`, API tokens refused) with the date, the scenario, start and finish times (the date within a day of the start) and the quantity of the lot and the quantity accounted for, in the lot item's unit (at most 10^11, and traced at most 1000 times the lot: more is a unit mistake). The result is worked out once (`recallTestResult`): the duration in started minutes, the percentage accounted for (one decimal, for display), and **passed** when the quantities are equal to the stored 6 decimals (over-accounting fails like under-accounting; a rounded 100.0% is not enough) and the test took no longer than 4 hours, to the second. Append-only: a test recorded in error is voided (`manage`, a person, with a reason), never edited. The yearly test is due 12 months after the last passing test that isn't voided (`recallTestSchedule`).
- **Summary** (`GET /quality/summary`): open and investigating complaints, complaints in the last 30 days and by category over 12 months, open, awaiting-verification and overdue non-conformances, overdue and upcoming audits and the next one, and the last recall test, the last passing one and when the next is due.
- **Who may do what**: reading needs `read`. Agents (`write` tokens) may log and update complaints, raise and update non-conformances, plan, edit and cancel audits. Closing, verifying and reopening are `manage` and a person; completing an audit is the member auditor or a person with `manage`; recording a recall test is a person with `write`; voiding a test is `manage` and a person.
- **MCP**: `get_quality_summary`, `list_complaints`, `list_nonconformances`, `list_quality_audits`, `get_quality_record` (by reference), `log_complaint`, `update_complaint`, `raise_nonconformance`, `update_nonconformance`, `plan_quality_audit`, `get_complaint_trends`, `get_supplier_quality`, `list_audit_programmes`. No tools for the sign-offs.

- **Due notifications** (`nonconformance_due`): the sweep notifies the owner (if they can read quality) and everyone with `manage` on quality once per due date when a non-conformance that is not closed falls due today or is overdue (within the usual 14-day look-back). Dedupe key `nc:<id>:<dueOn>`; moving the due date makes a new occurrence.
- **Four-eyes on verification**: the person who marked the corrective action `action_taken` is stored (`action_taken_by`) and may not close (verify) the non-conformance, unless they are the only member with `manage` on quality (`canVerifyNonconformance`, else `403 four_eyes`). It is cleared when the record goes back to open or is reopened; a record with nobody stored can be verified by any manager. An action taken through an API token counts as its owner's.
- **Complaint trends** (`GET /quality/trends?months=`, the Trends page): complaints per million units, per month for the last 1 to 36 months, overall, per product and per complaint category. **Units are entered, not derived**: quality does not depend on production, and units produced is not units sold, so a person (or agent, `write`) enters the units sold of a finished product per month (`PUT /quality/sales-volumes`, table `quality_sales_volumes`, saving again replaces it). A product's rate uses its own units; the overall and category rates use all products' units in the month; a month with no units has a null rate (shown as a dash) and the counts are shown beside every rate. Complaints are counted by received date.
- **Supplier non-conformances and approval**: `GET /quality/suppliers/:id` lists a supplier's open non-conformances beside its approval status with a suggestion to look at suspension (`supplierSuspensionAdvice`: any open critical or major one, or three or more open of any severity; nothing when it is already suspended or rejected). It is only advice: approval stays a person's `manage` decision in the items module (`POST /suppliers/:id/approval`), whose dialog shows the list. To record the link, the dialog then calls `POST /quality/suppliers/:id/review` (`manage` on quality, a person) which stamps each open supplier non-conformance with when the approval was decided, by whom and the status it was left at (`supplier_reviewed_*`); it changes no approval and needs an approval decision on record.
- **Recall test prefill** (`GET /quality/recall-tests/prefill?lotId=&direction=`, the "Fill from the lot's trace" button): the lot's mass balance from the stock ledger in the item's unit (expected = received + produced; traced = on hand + used + despatched + wasted, so adjustments and stocktake differences show as a shortfall to explain) and, when production is on and readable, the forward and backward batch trace as text for the notes. The person checks and edits everything before recording; the test itself is unchanged and still a person's attestation. Without production the lot's use in batches is not known, so the notes say the batches were not traced.
- **Recurring audit programme** (`quality_audit_programmes`, `/quality/audit-programmes`, `write`): an area or standard, a scope, an interval in months, the date of the next audit not yet planned, an optional weekday, an auditor (member or name) and an active flag. Saving a programme and the notification sweep plan its audits ahead as ordinary planned audits (`programme_id` on the audit) for every date within 60 days (`AUDIT_PROGRAMME_AHEAD_DAYS`), then move the next date on by the interval (months are added to the anchor date, so month ends hold). A date more than one interval in the past is skipped, so a programme left alone doesn't plan a backlog of overdue audits. Re-reading under the workspace lock makes two sweeping instances plan each audit once. **Weekday**: a programme may pin its audits to an ISO `weekday` (1 Monday … 7 Sunday, or none). Each audit is planned on the first such day on or after its date (`onOrAfterWeekday`: never earlier than the date, at most 6 days later), and the programme's `nextPlannedOn` stays the unpinned date, so pinning never makes the schedule creep later; the programme view adds `nextAuditOn`, the date the next audit will actually fall on. Changing the weekday only affects audits not yet planned. Removing a programme leaves the audits it planned; one-off planning is unchanged, and the planned audits notify their auditor as before (`audit_upcoming`).

Not built yet: nothing from the first list. The non-conformance page shows who took the action and, for a supplier non-conformance, the supplier review (when, by whom and the status the supplier was left at, or that it is not yet reviewed). Left out on purpose: units sold are not read from sales or production data.

## Purchasing module

Purchase orders to suppliers, goods-in against them and how well suppliers deliver. Needs `items` (suppliers live there, and stock lines name items). Enhanced by `stock`: **stock lines** (ingredients and packaging received into stock as lots) need it, and **non-stock lines** (services, subscriptions, goods not kept in stock) don't, so a service business, office or trade can buy with Items and Purchasing alone. Enhanced by `production` (suggestions) and `quality` (supplier warnings). Code: `packages/core/src/purchasing.ts`, `packages/db/src/schema/purchasing.ts`, `apps/server/src/{services,routes}/purchasing.ts`, `apps/mcp/src/tools/purchasing.ts`, and the web pages under `/<workspace>/purchasing` (Orders, an order, Invoices, an invoice, Suggestions, Supplier performance). Supplier invoices: `packages/core/src/purchase-invoices.ts`, `apps/server/src/{services,routes}/purchase-invoices.ts`, `apps/mcp/src/tools/purchase-invoices.ts`. `purchasing` is not a reserved workspace slug: the pages live under the workspace.

- **Orders and references** (`purchase_orders`): an order has one supplier and a reference, `PO-` then the year it was created and a sequence that restarts each year (`PO-2026-001`). Drafts get one too, so people can talk about them. Unique in the workspace and never reused, deleted drafts included. Also a requested delivery date, a default delivery location, the supplier's own order number, notes, and who sent, closed or cancelled it, when and why. Every write takes the workspace's sync-log lock before it reads the order.
- **States**: the stored status is `draft`, `sent`, `closed` or `cancelled`. The state shown is derived from the receipts (`purchaseOrderState`): a sent order is `sent` until something is accepted, `part_received` while any line is short and `received` once every line has its ordered quantity or more. Each line is `none`, `partial`, `full` or `over` (`lineReceiptState`, compared at the stored 4 decimals, so float noise never turns a full delivery into an over-delivery).
- **Line kinds** (`kind`, default `stock`): a **stock** line is as described next. A **non-stock** line (`kind: "non_stock"`) has a `description` (required), a quantity stored as entered (no base unit, no conversion; at most 1,000,000,000), an optional free-text `unit` (`hour`, `month`, `visit`; stored as `unit_label`), an optional price per that unit (no price list or standard cost default), an optional `nominalCode` (the expense account or nominal code, as text) and optionally an `itemId` it refers to (any active item; a reference only: nothing is stocked or converted). Stock lines need the stock module active and readable by the caller: creating one, moving a line to another stock item, setting a delivery location or receiving a stock line otherwise answers `404 module_inactive` (or `403 module_forbidden`); non-stock lines never touch stock. The database enforces the split (`purchase_order_lines_stock_check`: a stock line has an item and a unit; `purchase_order_lines_non_stock_check`: a non-stock line has a description and no stock unit); existing lines were all stock lines. A line can change kind only while it has no receipts and is on no invoice (`409 line_has_receipts`, `409 line_invoiced`); a non-stock line's unit can't change once received or invoiced, nor its item or price once invoiced.
- **Lines** (`purchase_order_lines`, stock lines): an ingredient or packaging item (`409 item_not_purchasable`), active (`409 item_archived`), a quantity and the unit it was entered in, which must convert for the item (`400 unit_not_convertible`). The quantity is stored in the item's base unit (g, ml or each), like stock, and may be at most 1,000,000,000 base units (the `numeric(14,4)` column's room; 400 otherwise, on lines and receipts). The price is per line unit, with **8 decimal places** (a line in grams of an item costed at 3.45 a kilo is priced 0.00345 a gram; 4 places would round it to 0.0035, 1.4% off the value), optional, and defaults to the supplier's **price list** entry in force today, converted to the line unit, else the item's standard cost converted to the line unit without rounding to fewer places; there is no currency setting, so prices are plain numbers like `standardCost`. A line may carry its own expected date, which overrides the order's. The same item may be on several lines. An order's value is the sum of quantity times price over priced lines, with `valueComplete` false when any line has no price.
- **Price lists** (`purchase_supplier_prices`; `GET/POST /purchasing/prices`, `PATCH/DELETE …/:id`, `write`, tokens allowed): per supplier and item, a price per unit (which must convert for the item) effective from a date to an optional inclusive end date; unique per supplier, item and start date (`409 duplicate_price`). The entry in force on a date is the one that took effect last among those covering it (`priceInForce`). It is the default price of a new line (and of a line whose item or unit changes) when none is typed, ahead of the standard cost; lines already on an order keep their price. Listing takes `supplierId`, `itemId` and `on`. The suggestions page still suggests the item's standard cost.
- **Editing** (`PATCH`, `write`): a draft can change in every way, and a draft (only) can be deleted (`409 not_draft` otherwise). A sent order keeps its supplier (`409 not_draft`) but its dates, delivery location, supplier reference and notes can change, and lines can be added, re-priced, re-dated and resized; a line with any receipt on it can't be removed (`409 line_has_receipts`), a line on a supplier invoice can't change item and one on an approved invoice can't be removed (`409 line_invoiced`), and its quantity can't go below what has been accepted (`409 below_received`). After any edit a sent order must still be sendable: at least one line and an expected date for every line (`400`). While a sent order's supplier is suspended or rejected, nothing more may be ordered from them: no new lines, no other item on a line, no larger quantities (`409 supplier_not_approved`); lowering quantities, re-dating and notes still work. An active invoice reservation also fixes the line’s item, unit, quantity and unit price and prevents removal. A line whose item has since been archived or deleted stays editable (kept by its `id`); new lines need a live, active item. Lines are sent as a full replacement, each carrying its `id` to keep an existing line. A closed or cancelled order can't be edited (`409 order_closed`). The supplier must be live: suspended or rejected suppliers are refused (`409 supplier_not_approved`); a pending supplier may be ordered from.
- **Sending** (`POST …/send`) turns a draft into a commitment to buy. It needs at least one line, an expected date on the order or on every line, and a supplier that is not suspended or rejected. It records who and when, and the order date (default today, not in the future). Each line is **promised** its effective expected date (`promised_on`); a line added to a sent order is promised its effective date when added. Later edits never move a promise: re-dating changes the expected date (and so what is overdue and the receive form), not what supplier performance measures against. The order page shows the promised date where it differs. It needs `manage` and a signed-in person: API tokens are refused unless an owner or admin allows `purchasing.send` under Settings → API access, which also gives tokens the `send_purchase_order` MCP tool.
  - **Approval above a threshold** (`purchasing_settings.send_approval_threshold`, `GET/PUT /purchasing/settings`; the PUT is `manage` and a person, "Change purchase order approval and over-delivery settings"; **off by default**): an order worth more than the threshold (priced lines only; unpriced ones count for nothing) can't be sent until a manager **other than its creator** approves it (`POST …/approve`, `manage`, `forbidTokens`, "Approve purchase orders for sending", optional note; `403 four_eyes` for the creator, strict even for a sole manager: clear the threshold instead). The approval stores the value it covers: a draft that has grown past it is `stale` and needs approving again, one that shrank stays approved, and changing the supplier clears it. Sending without it answers `409 approval_required` (tokens too, where `purchasing.send` is allowed). The order shows `sendApproval` (`not_required`, `needed`, `approved`, `stale`). Creating or editing a draft above the threshold notifies the other purchasing managers once (`purchase_order_approval_needed`, see [notifications.md](notifications.md)).
  - **Emailing the order** (`POST …/email`, `{ to?, message? }`; same switch (`purchasing.send`) and level as sending): a sent order is emailed to `to` or the supplier's email in the register (`400 no_supplier_email`; `409 order_not_sent` otherwise) through the existing mailer, as a plain text and an HTML body with the same print-style summary (reference, dates, delivery location, lines with quantity, price and value, total, notes; no links, no PDF). Each attempt is logged on the order (`purchase_order_emails`, synced as `purchase_order_email`, shown as `emails`), failed ones too. Like invitations a broken mail server never gives a 500: the answer is `{ emailed: false, mailError, … }` with the failure logged; with no SMTP configured nothing is sent or logged and `emailed` is false.
- **Receiving** (`POST …/receipts`, `write`; API tokens allowed, so agents can record deliveries, as with stock goods-in): the order must be sent (`409 order_not_open`). One request is one delivery: a received date (not in the future), an optional delivery note number and, per line, either `accepted` or `rejected`. Everything happens in one transaction under the workspace lock, and every row of a delivery shares a `deliveryId`. On a stock line `unit` defaults to the line's and `locationId` to the order's delivery location (400 when it has none).
  - **Non-stock lines** are received by quantity alone, in the line's own unit: the receipt records the quantity, date, who and the price per that unit, with no lot and no stock movement (`unit`, `locationId` and the lot fields are refused with 400). Accepted ones from suspended or rejected suppliers are refused (`409 supplier_not_approved`); there is no quarantine. Part, over and under delivery and the over-delivery tolerance work as for stock lines. An accepted non-stock receipt recorded in error is voided (see Corrections).
  - **Accepted lines** create a stock lot through stock's `createLot` (the order's supplier, the supplier's own lot code, expiry date and type with the same rules as goods-in, lot code generated when not given, status available or quarantine) and a `receipt` movement into an active location. The movement's `reference` is the order's reference, followed by `/` and the delivery note number when there is one (`PO-2026-001 / DN4471`), so the lot page shows which order it came in on. The quantity is entered in any unit that converts and stored in the base unit.
  - **Supplier approval** is stock's rule, shared with goods-in (`assertSupplierCanDeliver`): accepted goods from suspended and rejected suppliers are refused (`409 supplier_not_approved`), and a pending supplier's goods must go into quarantine. A delivery made only of rejected lines is recorded whatever the supplier's approval (refusing goods at the door is what you do with a suspended supplier).
  - **The item** must pass goods-in's item rule (`assertItemReceivable`, shared with stock): an item archived (or deleted) since it was ordered is refused (`409 item_archived`), and one that is no longer an ingredient or packaging is refused (`403 item_not_receivable`; there is no opening-balance path against an order). Rejected lines of such items are still recorded.
  - **Over and under delivery**: a part delivery leaves the line `partial` and the order `part_received`, and more can be received later. **Over-delivery tolerance** (`purchasing_settings.over_delivery_tolerance_percent`, workspace-wide, **off by default**: null means any amount is accepted, as before; 0 means any excess needs a reason). With one set, an accepted entry that takes a line's accepted total (net of reversals, in base units, compared at 4 decimals) past ordered plus that percentage needs an `overDeliveryReason` (`409 over_delivery_reason_required`); with a reason it is accepted and **flagged**, never blocked (the reason is stored on the receipt and shown on the order). Decision: a per-item tolerance was not built (it would need an items schema change); the workspace percentage covers it. Whether the rest of a short order is still coming is decided by closing it.
  - **Rejected deliveries** are goods refused at the door: a `rejected` line needs a quantity and a reason, adds a receipt row and moves no stock. It does not count towards what was received, so the line stays outstanding.
  - **Receipts** (`purchase_receipts`) are append-only, one row per line per delivery, naming the lot and the stock movement for accepted rows. Each accepted row keeps the **price per base unit at 8 decimal places** (a price per gram is small): the price given at receipt per unit (an invoice price) or else the line's price, converted, and null when neither is known. Costing reads it until an approved supplier invoice prices the order line (see Supplier invoices below); the receipt itself is never changed.
- **Supplier invoices** (`purchase_invoices`, `purchase_invoice_lines`): an invoice belongs to one supplier and has the supplier's invoice number (unique per supplier, case-insensitive; `409 duplicate_invoice_number`), an invoice date, an optional due date (not before the invoice date), VAT as a plain amount and notes; there is no currency, so amounts are plain numbers. Net is the sum of the lines (quantity × price, 4 places) and gross is net plus VAT. A line is either an **order line** (a purchase order line of the same supplier, `409 wrong_supplier` otherwise, on a sent or closed order, `409 order_not_invoiceable` for drafts and cancelled orders; once per invoice) with the quantity and unit price **per the order line's unit**, which the invoice line keeps, or a **charge** (carriage, a pallet charge) with a description, a quantity (default 1) and a price, which is not matched. Drafts are entered and edited by people and drafted by agents (`write`, API tokens allowed): `POST /purchasing/invoices`, `PATCH …/:id` (the lines are a full replacement), `DELETE …/:id` (drafts only).
- **Invoiced order lines are fixed.** An order line on a live invoice (a draft, approved or superseded version) can't be removed from its order, nor change item, unit or price (`409 line_invoiced`), like a received line; its quantity, dates and notes can. The invoice was matched (and is costed) against those; to change them, revise the invoice (or delete the draft) first. The order page disables those fields and says why; order lines carry `invoiced` and `invoicedQuantity` (on approved invoices in force, per the line's unit).
- **Three-way match** (`matchInvoiceLine` in core), computed on read for a draft and frozen at approval: each order line on an invoice against the **order** (price) and against what was **received** (accepted receipts whose stock movement still stands and that are not voided) and not yet invoiced on other approved invoices (quantity). A non-stock order line is matched the same way in its own unit: nothing converts (`basePerUnit` 1), its invoice lines have no stock unit, and an invoice line that gives no description takes the order line's.
  - **Price**: the invoice price against the order line's, either direction (a much lower price is as likely a wrong unit). Equal at 8 places is `matched`; within tolerance is `within_tolerance`; beyond it, or an order line with no price, is a `mismatch`.
  - **Units**: received and already-invoiced quantities are converted to the line's unit with the item's conversion (unit weight, density). When they can't be (a unit weight was cleared), the line is a `mismatch` with the issue `unit_not_convertible` and `received` is null: grams are never compared with sacks.
  - **Quantity**: invoiced against received-and-not-yet-invoiced. Invoicing no more than that is `matched`: a part invoice (a part delivery invoiced, or one delivery of several), the rest can come on a later invoice. Over-invoicing is `within_tolerance` or a `mismatch`; invoicing when nothing is left to invoice (nothing received, or the same delivery invoiced twice) is a `mismatch`.
  - **What counts as already invoiced**: for a draft, the approved invoices in force (not the version it would replace); for an approved or superseded invoice, those approved before it and not yet replaced then, so a later invoice never turns an earlier one into over-invoicing.
  - **Frozen at approval.** Approval stores, on each order line, what it was matched against (`approved_match`: the order price per the line's unit, the order line's unit, the conversion as base units per line unit, received, invoiced elsewhere, and the match) and, on the invoice, the tolerances (`approved_tolerances`). An approved or superseded invoice **shows and uses the match it was approved at** (`matchStatus` is `approvedMatchStatus`; each line's `match`, `received` and order price are as approved), so a later change to the tolerances, an item's unit weight or the order never re-grades it. `matchStatusNow` and each line's `now` are the same comparison today, against the stored price, conversion and tolerances with what has been received (and reversed) since; the page shows "now" only where it differs. Drafts match live.
  - **Tolerances** are per workspace (`GET`/`PUT /purchasing/invoice-settings`, `purchasing_settings`): a percentage and an amount for price and for quantity. **Defaults: 2% and 10.00 for each**, small enough that a wrong price or a short delivery always shows, large enough that a supplier's rounding (a price per kg quoted to 2 places, a sack weighing 24.8 kg) doesn't. Percentages are of the order price and of the quantity received and not yet invoiced; amounts are money (the price difference times the quantity invoiced; the excess quantity times the invoice price). A difference is within tolerance only when it is within **both**, so small lines are held by the percentage and large ones by the amount; boundaries are inclusive; 0 and 0 accept exact matches only. Changing them needs `manage` and a signed-in person (`forbidTokens`, "Change invoice match tolerances"): a token loosening them would let mismatches through without a reason.
  - Each line has a status, its price and quantity variances (per unit and as a percentage; positive is over the order price or over-invoiced) and the issues behind it; the **invoice's status** is its worst order line (`matched` with none).
- **Approval** (`POST …/approve`) for payment is a sign-off: `manage` and a signed-in person, never an API token (`forbidTokens`, in `personOnlyActions` as "Approve supplier invoices for payment"; it can't be opened under Settings → API access) and there is no MCP tool. It needs a line, refuses a line whose order line has since been taken off its order (`409 order_line_removed`) or whose order is no longer sent or closed (cancelled since it was entered: `409 order_not_invoiceable`), refuses a supplier deleted since (`404`), and a `mismatch` needs a reason (`409 reason_required` without one), as does an invoice of charges only (nothing to match it against). It records who, when, the match at approval (see Frozen at approval) and the reason. **An approved invoice is immutable** (`409 invoice_not_draft` on edit, delete or a second approval).
- **Credit notes** (`purchase_credit_notes`, `purchase_credit_note_lines`; `GET/POST /purchasing/credit-notes`, `GET/PATCH/DELETE …/:id`, `POST …/:id/approve`, `POST …/:id/void`): a supplier's credit note is its own document against one **approved** invoice (`409 invoice_not_creditable` for a draft or superseded one), with the supplier's credit note number (unique per supplier among live, non-voided ones), a credit date, VAT credited and positive amounts. A line credits an order line of that invoice with a reason (`price`: it cost too much, or a rebate; `goods_returned`: units sent back) or is a credited charge (carriage). Drafts are entered by people and agents (`write`, tokens allowed); **approval** is `manage` and a person (`forbidTokens`, "Approve supplier credit notes") and makes it immutable; a wrong approved one is **voided** with a reason ("Void supplier credit notes", also a person), so it stops counting and its number is free again. Credits are capped by what was billed: per invoice line the credited value and the returned quantity, and over the invoice its net (`409 over_credited`); only approved credit notes count towards a cap, and the cap is re-checked at approval. **Costing**: the credit lines of approved credit notes net off the invoice price a lot is costed at (`invoicePricePerBase` takes the order line's `ApprovedCreditTotals`): the credited value comes off the invoiced value, and returned units come off the quantity, so a return at the invoice price leaves the cost of what was kept unchanged and a price credit lowers it; charge credits don't touch costing. Returned goods only credit the money: the stock is reversed on the lot page. An invoice shows `creditNotes`, `creditedNet` and `netAfterCredits`. **Discount lines** are charges at a negative price (`unitPrice < 0`); the invoice can't total less than nothing (`400`), and discounts are not spread over order lines in costing (like carriage).
- **Corrections to an invoice are new versions** (a credit note is a separate document, above): `POST …/:id/revise` (`write`, tokens allowed) copies an approved invoice into a draft with the same supplier and number and the next `version` (`supersedesId`), to edit (its supplier and number are locked, `409 revision_locked`) and approve. Until it is approved the original stays approved and in force, in the match and in costing; approving it marks the original `superseded` (who replaced it and when). One draft revision at a time (`409 revision_exists`); superseded versions can't be revised. Every version is kept and linked, so the audit trail is the chain of versions. Chosen over credit notes because it needs no negative lines or netting of several documents in the match or in costing, and the invoice in force is always one document.
- **Feeding costing**: once approved, an invoice's matched prices become the cost of the lots received against those order lines (see the Costing module), without touching the append-only receipts. Costing reads receipts by lot, so non-stock lines (no lot) never reach it. Each lot is priced from its own receipt and what approval stored, never with the item's conversion today.
- **Attachments**: an invoice is an attachment target (`purchase_invoice`, the supplier's paper as a `document`), locked once approved or superseded; the panel is on the invoice page.
- **Reading**: `GET /purchasing/invoices` (filters `status` = `current` (default: drafts and approved), `draft`, `approved`, `superseded` or `all`, `supplierId`, `purchaseOrderId`, `from`/`to` on the invoice date, `q` on the number and supplier; latest invoice date first; paged with `total`; each with its totals and match status now) and `GET /purchasing/invoices/:id` (lines with the order line, ordered, received and already invoiced amounts in the line's unit, the order's price, and the match; the tolerances; the versions). Writes go to the sync log (`purchase_invoice`, `purchase_invoice_line`, `purchasing_settings`), filtered by module access. The web pages are `/<workspace>/purchasing/invoices` (with a tolerances dialog for managers) and `/<workspace>/purchasing/invoices/<id>` (the mismatch summary, the match table, approval, correction); the entry form brings in an order's lines at what was received and not yet invoiced on approved invoices, and the order price, to adjust (lines with nothing left to invoice are left out, and it says so); the order page links to its invoices.
- **Corrections**: purchasing has no route to reverse or edit a receipt. An accepted receipt is corrected by reversing its stock movement on the lot page (the stock module's reversal: `manage` and a signed-in person). Received quantities are the sum of accepted receipts whose movement has not been reversed, so the order, its lines, the suggestions and supplier performance all follow the reversal; the receipt stays on the order, struck through. A **rejected** receipt recorded in error is voided (`POST /purchasing/orders/:id/receipts/:receiptId/void` with a reason: `manage` and a signed-in person, `forbidTokens`, "Void refused deliveries and non-stock receipts"): the row stays, struck through, with who voided it and why (`voided_at`, `voided_by`, `void_reason`), and counts nowhere (the order's rejected totals and supplier performance's rejected deliveries). It is never edited or deleted; record the right refusal as a new delivery. An accepted receipt of a **non-stock** line (no lot, no movement) is voided the same way and then counts nowhere (received quantities, the invoice match, supplier performance). An accepted stock receipt can't be voided (`409 receipt_not_rejected`): reverse its stock movement.
- **Closing and cancelling** are a signed-in person's acts, each with a reason; API tokens are refused unless an owner or admin allows `purchasing.close` or `purchasing.cancel` under Settings → API access, which gives tokens the `close_purchase_order` and `cancel_purchase_order` MCP tools. **Closing** (`POST …/close`) a sent order says nothing more is expected: a short close counts against the supplier's in-full figure. **Cancelling** (`POST …/cancel`) takes a draft, or a sent order with nothing accepted against it (a reversed receipt doesn't count); once goods have arrived it is refused (`409 has_receipts`) and the order is closed instead. Closing a draft is refused (`409 order_not_open`). Closed and cancelled orders are final.
- **Suggestions** (`GET /purchasing/suggestions?from=&to=`, default today to 28 days ahead, at most a year): what to order, worked out from production's material shortfall (`getMaterialRequirements`), for active ingredients and packaging only, minus the outstanding quantity (ordered less received) on **sent** orders' stock lines for the item, whatever their expected date (a non-stock line naming the item doesn't count). Draft orders are shown as `onDraft` and not subtracted, since a draft is not a commitment. Items that sent orders already cover are listed separately. The rest are grouped by the item's preferred supplier (`item_suppliers`), else its only supplier, else no supplier, which the person chooses; suspended and rejected suppliers are never suggested. Each row carries the shortfall, on order, on draft, to order (never below zero, base and display), the item's standard cost as the suggested price and the earliest planned date the shortfall is needed by as the suggested expected date. It needs the production module to be on **and readable by the caller**, because the shortfall is production's data (`409 production_unavailable`, with the reason). Work in progress and finished goods in the shortfall (made in house: plan their production) and archived items can't be bought, so they are listed apart as `notPurchasable` with the shortfall and the reason (`made_in_house` or `archived`, `notPurchasableReason`), and the web page shows them as a short note. Computed on read, never stored. `POST /purchasing/suggestions` (`write`) creates draft orders from a chosen proposal, up to 50, each exactly what `POST /purchasing/orders` takes: all or nothing, and a refusal names the order (`Order 2 (…): …`, `details.index`). The web page `/<workspace>/purchasing/suggestions` has editable quantities, a supplier picker where none is suggested, and rows that can be left out.
- **Supplier performance** (`GET /purchasing/supplier-performance?from=&to=&supplierId=`, default the last 90 days up to today, both included): per supplier, from the lines (stock and non-stock alike, each in its own quantities) of **sent and closed** orders; drafts are left out, and a **cancelled** order's lines are never due but its deliveries and refusals still count (cancelling an order after refusing its delivery must not hide the refusal).
  - **What counts as due**: a line is due when its **promised date** (its effective expected date when the order was sent, never moved by later edits) falls inside the period and is not after today. A line not yet due is left out, so an order placed yesterday for next week doesn't count against anyone, and a line with no expected date is never due. Dates are checked against today in the workspace's time zone.
  - **On time**: the line's first accepted delivery was on or before its promised date. **In full**: the accepted quantity reaches the ordered quantity, whenever it came; over-delivery is in full. **OTIF**: the ordered quantity had been accepted by the promised date. The three are counts of due lines and percentages with one decimal (null when no line was due). Reversed receipts count as never delivered; a line closed short fails in full.
  - **Deliveries** are the distinct `deliveryId`s with any receipt received inside the period, whichever line they were for, and **rejected deliveries** those of them with anything refused. Only suppliers with a line due or a delivery in the period are returned, sorted by name with their approval status.
  - The web page `/<workspace>/purchasing/suppliers` shows the table. Nothing here changes a supplier's approval status. **Quality flag**: where quality is active and the caller can read it, each row carries `qualityFlag` (open, critical, major and overdue non-conformances from the supplier, and quality's `suggest`/`reasons` to look at its approval, `supplierQualityFlags`), and the order read carries `supplierQuality` (`null` when quality is off or unreadable). The order page shows it as a warning. It is information only: the supplier approval dialog (items) already lists them, and nothing is ever blocked automatically.
- **Who may do what**: reading needs `read`. `write` (API tokens allowed, so agents can draft and receive) covers creating, editing and deleting drafts, editing sent orders, recording deliveries and creating drafts from suggestions. Sending needs `manage` and a signed-in person; closing and cancelling need `write` and a signed-in person; a token can do each only when its action (`purchasing.send`, `purchasing.close`, `purchasing.cancel`) is allowed and its owner has that level. Recording a delivery of stock lines posts stock lots and movements with purchasing access plus read access to stock (stock is a soft dependency: without it only non-stock lines can be ordered and received). Responses blank the delivery location and lot codes for a caller who can't read stock.
- **Reading**: `GET /purchasing/orders` (filters `q` on reference, supplier name and the supplier's reference, `status` as `open` (sent or part received), each state or `all`, `supplierId`, `itemId`, `from`, `to` on the earliest effective expected date over the lines, as `nextExpectedOn` shows it; newest first; paged with `total`) and `GET /purchasing/orders/:id` (header, supplier with approval status, lines with ordered, received, rejected and outstanding amounts, state, price and value, the expected and promised dates, whether the item is listed for this supplier, and the receipts grouped by delivery with lot code, who, and a reversed flag). Orders are not in the bootstrap; writes go to the sync log (`purchase_order`, `purchase_order_line`, `purchase_receipt`, plus the stock module's `stock_lot` and `stock_movement` for accepted receipts), filtered by module access: members without purchasing read don't get them. Purchasing adds nothing to search in this release.
- **MCP**: `list_purchase_orders`, `get_purchase_order`, `draft_purchase_order` (creates a draft, or replaces the lines and header of a draft or sent order when given an `orderId`; stock and `non_stock` lines), `receive_purchase_order` (non-stock lines without location or lot fields), `suggest_purchase_orders` (only where production is on, `requiresModules`), `supplier_performance`, and for invoices `list_purchase_invoices`, `get_purchase_invoice` and `draft_purchase_invoice` (creates or edits a draft, or with `correct` drafts a new version of an approved one, or applies the changes to the correction already pending; a correction it drafted is deleted again if the changes are refused; no approval tool). `list_purchase_credit_notes` and `draft_purchase_credit_note` (drafts and edits a credit note; no approval or void tool). `send_purchase_order`, `close_purchase_order` and `cancel_purchase_order` appear only while an owner or admin has allowed `purchasing.send`, `purchasing.close` or `purchasing.cancel` under Settings → API access.

Reserving stock for batches is the production module's [reservations](#production-module): the shortfall that suggestions are built from already leaves out stock reserved for batches outside the planning range. Web: the order page has Approve to send, Email supplier, the approval and supplier-quality warnings and the email log, and each refused delivery line a **Void refusal** button (a manager; asks for the reason, then the line is struck through with who voided it and why). The Orders page has **Order controls** (a manager) for the approval threshold and the over-delivery tolerance, each empty to switch it off. The receive dialog asks for an over-delivery reason on an accepted line only once the quantity goes past ordered plus the tolerance (it uses the same `isOverDeliveryTolerance` rule as the API, in the line's unit; the API stays the authority). **Price lists** (`/<workspace>/purchasing/prices`, a tab) list the entries by supplier and item with an "in force" badge; anyone with write adds a price (item units that convert only), ends one with a last-day date (the history stays) or removes one entered in error. **Credit notes** (`/<workspace>/purchasing/credit-notes`, a tab, and a detail page) list and filter credit notes; a "Credit note" button on an approved invoice, or "Draft credit note" on the list, opens the form (supplier, an approved invoice, the supplier's number and date, lines credited as a price reduction, goods returned or a charge, VAT); the detail page edits or deletes a draft and a manager approves it or voids an approved one with a reason (people only, as on the API). The invoice form has an "Add a discount" button: a charge with a negative price. MCP: `list_supplier_prices` and `email_purchase_order` (shown only while `purchasing.send` is allowed, as emailing is part of sending). Not built yet: a per-item over-delivery tolerance, suggestions priced from the price list, PDF orders, payments and remittances, matching an invoice line to a particular delivery (lines match order lines), and additional accounting providers such as Sage. See [Xero supplier bills](xero.md) for the optional Xero integration.

## Costing module

What production batches actually cost, how much they yielded, and standard against actual by product and period. Needs `recipes`, `production` and `purchasing` (and through them `items`, `stock` and `demand`). Code: `packages/core/src/costing.ts` (the engine and the report, pure), `apps/server/src/{services,routes}/costing.ts`, `apps/mcp/src/tools/costing.ts`, and the web pages under `/<workspace>/costing` (Standard against actual, Batches, a batch's cost). `costing` is not a reserved workspace slug: the pages live under the workspace.

- **Computed on read, never stored.** A batch's cost is a function of records that are already append-only: batch records (production), the price on each purchase receipt (purchasing, frozen at receipt as a price per base unit with 8 places), the prices of approved supplier invoices and item standard costs. A snapshot taken when a batch is completed would go stale, because a completed batch's records can still be corrected (a person with `manage` reverses them) and a lot's cost depends on every batch upstream of it. Reading also means a corrected price (should purchasing ever correct a receipt, or a standard cost change) shows up everywhere at once. The price of that choice: a past period's figures can move when a standard cost is corrected or a record is corrected. Closing a period freezes them (below). Costs themselves have no tables and are not in the sync log or the bootstrap. What people set is stored (and synced as small reference data): period closes, standard cost history and absorption rates; `write` adds nothing to `read`, `manage` is needed to set them (below).
- **A lot's cost per unit** (`createCostingEngine` in core): the **invoice price** when the order line it was received against is on an approved supplier invoice (source `invoice`; priced per receipt by `invoicePricePerBase` in core: the receipt's frozen price per base unit times the invoice-to-order price ratio stored at approval (quantity-weighted over the approved invoices billing the line), or, when the order line had no price, the invoice price over the conversion stored at approval, so a lot's cost never drifts when an item's unit weight or density changes; superseded versions and drafts ignored; an invoice that can't be priced either way is not dropped: the receipt's price (or the standard cost) stands with the reason `invoice_unpriced`, and the lot's receipt says `invoiceUnpriced`; a lot resting on several receipts is `invoice` only when every one has an invoice price, else `purchase` with the prices mixed), else the **purchase price** of the accepted receipt that created it (purchasing goods-in; weighted by quantity if a lot ever had several, ignoring receipts whose stock movement was reversed while others remain); else, when a batch **made** it, that batch's actual cost per unit of output, recursively, so the cost of ingredients flows through work in progress into finished goods; else the item's **standard cost**, flagged as **estimated** with why: `no_purchase_price` (received against an order line without a price), `not_purchased` (opening stock and manual goods-in; stocktake gains and adjustments add to an existing lot, so they carry its cost), `cycle` (made by a batch in a loop with the batch consuming it: batches that, through each other, consumed their own output, such as rework or a starter fed back into its pot; every lot passed around inside a loop takes its standard cost, whichever batch or lot is being costed, so a loop's costs don't feed back into themselves), `too_deep` (made more than `COSTING_MAX_DEPTH` = 20 batch stages back from the batch or lot being costed, counted along each path) or `no_output` (its batch has no output left on record). A lot with neither a price nor a standard cost has no cost (`missing`): the batch's cost is then **incomplete** and says which items.
- **The same figure everywhere.** The engine enforces the depth limit itself (the loader also loads no further), and a batch's or lot's cost depends only on it and the ledger, never on what else is costed in the same request: a batch costs the same on its page, in the batch list and in the report. Because loops are cut the same way from any starting point, every value is memoised (a value the depth limit cut, per remaining depth) and a report stays linear in the batches and lots it reads, however tangled the loops. The walk is bounded by the depth limit, so a long chain (a starter fed from yesterday's for years, rework of rework) can't overflow the stack.
- **A batch's actual cost** is the sum over the lots it consumed, net of reversed records, at their cost per unit. It reports the share resting on standard costs (`estimatedCost`, `estimatedPercent`; a WIP lot carries the estimated share of the batch that made it), the cost per unit of output (per the output item's own unit), and **provisional** while the batch, or any batch upstream of it, is still in progress (a cancelled batch's figures are final; a completed one changes only if a record is corrected). Planned batches have nothing to cost.
- **Yield** is produced against planned output (`yield.percent`, `yield.difference`), and its **cost effect** is the yield variance below.
- **Standard against actual** for a batch compares with its pinned recipe version's lines scaled to the planned output (production's `batchMaterials`) at the **standard costs in force on its costing date** (completion date; planned date while open): see standard cost history below. Variances are actual minus standard, so **positive is adverse**, and they add up: **price** (what was paid for what was used against standard prices), **usage** (inputs used against what the plan needed, at standard prices), **yield** (planned against produced output at the standard cost per unit) and **total** = actual cost − standard cost of what was produced = price + usage + yield; **quantity** = usage + yield. Per ingredient the batch shows required, consumed, actual cost and its own price and usage variances. A variance is null when it can't be worked out cleanly: an item without a standard cost, a recipe line whose unit doesn't convert, or a missing actual cost. Measuring yield against plan means a batch deliberately cut short shows a yield variance offset by a favourable usage variance; the total is unaffected.
- **The report** (`GET /costing/report?period=week|month&from=&to=&outputItemId=&recipeId=`): completed batches grouped by output item and the ISO week or calendar month of their **completion date** (the sign-off, as a date in the workspace's time zone). `from` and `to` take a date, a week (`2026-W46`) or a month (`2026-11`) and widen to whole periods; default the last 6 months or 13 weeks up to the current one, at most two years, at most 2000 batches (`400` beyond, to narrow it). Each row and each period's totals give batches, planned and produced, yield, actual cost and the estimated share, cost per unit and the standard per unit, and the variances. Batches with an incomplete cost or standard count in actual cost and in `incompleteBatches` but are left out of the standard and the variances (`comparedActualCost` is the actual cost they are compared with), so a missing standard cost never turns a partial sum into a false variance.
- **Routes** (all `GET`, all `read`; API tokens allowed): `/costing/batches` (batches with records, newest costing date first, where the costing date is the completion date of a completed batch and the planned date of any other; filters `status` = `completed` (default), `in_progress`, `cancelled` or `all`, `from`, `to`, `outputItemId`, `recipeId`, `q`; paged with `total`), `/costing/batches/:id` (the full cost: each consumed lot with its quantity, unit cost, cost, source, why, and the batch that made it; the materials; the yield; the standard and the variances), `/costing/lots/:id` (one lot's cost per unit and why, with its purchase receipts and order references, each with its receipt price, the approved invoice price that takes over from it, whether an invoice couldn't be priced (`invoiceUnpriced`) and which invoices, or the batch that made it) and `/costing/report`.
- **Who sees costs**: costs and prices are commercially sensitive, so every route needs `read` on costing, which is opt-in like every manufacturing module. Because costing depends on purchasing, production and recipes, anyone who can read costs can already read the purchase prices and recipes behind them. Production readers without costing see no cost: the production batch page shows a compact cost panel (linking to the full page) only to people who can read costing, and production's own API returns no cost.
- **MCP**: `list_batch_costs`, `get_batch_cost` (by batch code or id), `costing_report` (with labour and overhead, waste and adjustments, and which periods are closed), `costing_valuation` and `costing_write_offs`, all read-only. There are no tools for closing, reopening or changing rates and standard costs.

### Period close, history, conversion costs, valuation and exports

- **Closing a period** (`POST /costing/closes` `{ period: week|month, of, note?, acknowledgeGaps? }`): a sign-off by a signed-in person with costing `manage` (tokens refused; no MCP tool; `personOnlyActions`: "Close or reopen costing periods"). The period must have ended (`409 period_not_ended`) and not be closed already (`409 period_closed`). Batches with an incomplete cost or standard, resting on a batch still in progress, or missing the hours or kilograms an absorption rate needs, answer `409 period_has_gaps` until `acknowledgeGaps` is true. It stores a **snapshot** (`costing_period_closes.snapshot`: the period's report totals and rows, each batch's headline figures, waste and adjustments) and a small `summary`; the close is written through `recordChanges` as `costing_period_close` **without** the snapshot (read it from `GET /costing/closes/:id`; `GET /costing/closes` lists closes). **What freezing means**: the report shows the snapshot's totals and rows for a closed period (`periods[].frozen`: who, when, and `drift` = whether today's live figures differ), and the cost journal export carries the frozen figures. It does not block production corrections or other modules' writes: a correction shows as drift instead. A product or recipe filter shows live figures. **Reopening** (`POST /costing/closes/:id/reopen` `{ reason }`, `manage`, person only) marks the close `reopened` with who, when and why and leaves it in place; closing again adds a new row, so a period's rows are its audit trail (a close is never deleted or edited).
- **Standard cost history** (`item_standard_costs`): an entry is an item's standard cost from a date (null: none from then). A change to an item's standard cost (item edit or CSV import) adds an entry from today, with a baseline entry for the cost it had (from its creation day, or from today when back-dating) so nothing is lost; `PUT /costing/standard-costs` `{ itemId, effectiveFrom, cost, note? }` (`manage`, person only) adds or corrects an entry on or before today (no future dates: `items.standard_cost` is always the entry in force today) and `GET /costing/standard-costs/:itemId` lists them. A batch's standard side uses the entry in force on its costing date (a date before the first entry uses the first); an unpriced lot estimated at standard cost uses the one in force the day it was received, so price variance isn't distorted by two different standards. Without history the current cost is used, as before.
- **Mix variance**: `variance.mix` splits the **usage** variance (`usage` = `mix` + `inputQuantity`), separate from yield. Within each unit dimension (mass, volume, count), ingredient i has `(consumed − totalUsed × required ÷ totalRequired) × standard`: the cost of using the ingredients in other proportions than the plan's at the quantity actually used; `inputQuantity` is using more or less in total in the plan's proportions. An ingredient used though the plan didn't need it counts wholly as mix. Null when an input has no standard cost or no plan quantity. Per ingredient: `materials[].mixVariance`; in report rows and totals.
- **Labour and overhead absorption** (`costing_absorption_rates`; `GET/PUT /costing/rates`, `PUT` is `manage`, person only): a simple model chosen over attendance-linked labour (nobody clocks a person onto a batch): per production line, or as the workspace default (`lineId: null`), from a date, an amount **per batch hour** (started to completed) and **per kilogram of output** for labour and for overhead separately. A batch absorbs the rates in force on its completion date: the line's own entry if it has one (it replaces the default, never adds), else the default. A rate that needs hours or kilograms the batch lacks (no start time, an output that can't be weighed) leaves that part out and the batch says so (`conversion.complete`, `missing`). Conversion costs are **separate from materials everywhere**: `GET /costing/batches/:id` has `conversion`; report rows and totals have `conversion` (labour, overhead, total) and `totalCost` beside `actualCost` (materials); variances stay materials only; a lot's cost and the valuation never include them. Not built: per-person labour rates from attendance hours, or packaging-line costs.
- **Stock valuation** (`GET /costing/valuation?itemType&status&q`): stock on hand **now** (no as-of date: the ledger has no cost history to replay) per lot and per item at the lot's cost (the engine's: invoice or purchase price, the making batch, or standard, flagged estimated), with total, estimated value, value by QA status and lots with no cost (counted, adding nothing). CSV: `GET /costing/export.csv?kind=valuation`. MCP: `costing_valuation`.
- **Waste and adjustments** (`GET /costing/adjustments?from&to&itemId`): `waste`, `adjustment` and `stocktake` movements in the window valued at their lot's cost, by type and item (loss positive, a gain negative; reversals net off; movements on lots with no cost are flagged `incomplete`). The report shows them per period beside the batch figures (`periods[].adjustments`, `adjustments`) and they are frozen in a close. MCP: `costing_write_offs`.
- **Accounting export** (`GET /costing/export.csv?kind=journal|batches|valuation|adjustments&period&from&to`, read; tokens allowed): generic CSV, no Sage or Xero connection. `journal` is one line per period, product and cost type (materials, labour absorbed, overhead absorbed) plus waste and adjustment lines per period, with amount, currency and status (open or closed); ISO dates, numbers as numbers, a byte order mark, formulas neutralised. Closed periods export their frozen figures.
- **Web**: `/<workspace>/costing` (period table with labour and overhead, waste, the mix column, close and reopen, export buttons), `/costing/valuation`, `/costing/setup` (rates and standard cost history).

Not built yet: valuation as of a past date, labour from attendance hours per person, packaging-line costs, VAT, and Sage or Xero integrations (out of scope).

## Maintenance module

Enable `assets` and `maintenance` to schedule work, record completion and manage breakdowns on equipment, vehicles, IT and tools. Open **Operations → Maintenance**. Grant readers at least Assets read access and the appropriate Maintenance level. Assets write access lets a person add equipment from the board. A person with Maintenance manage access signs return-to-service clearances. The person who did or recorded the work can sign only if they are the sole Maintenance manager. An active HSE isolation still blocks clearance.

- **Planned maintenance** (`site_maintenance_tasks`, `site_maintenance_records`, on assets of the register). Any asset (equipment, vehicles, tools, IT; its area is the asset's area) can have tasks with a frequency, an optional first due date and `clearance_required` (return-to-service clearance; requires an area). A record holds when, who (default the person recording), a contractor, the work done and the next due date (default completion plus the task's interval). Due: the last record's next due date, else the first due date, else due now. The return-to-service clearance (`POST .../records/:id/clearance`) is a one-time sign-off stamp; records awaiting it are listed and counted. A correction is a patch: what it doesn't change (when and by whom the work was done, contractor, notes, the next due date) is kept from the original, it needs a clearance if the original did (even if the task's flag changed since), and it is allowed on equipment archived since.
- **Breakdowns and work orders** (`site_work_orders`; `GET/POST /maintenance/work-orders`, `PATCH .../:id`, `POST .../:id/complete`, `POST .../:id/cancel`). A `breakdown` or a work `request` on an asset (optionally about one of its planned tasks), with priority (low to urgent), whether it blocks operations, an assignee and a contractor. It goes requested, in progress, then completed or cancelled; open ones can change, closed ones never do (`409 work_order_closed`). **Spares and costs:** free-text `sparesUsed`, `partsCost`, `labourCost`, `contractorCost` (to the penny, no currency stored), `downtimeMinutes`; what is entered while open is kept unless the completion says otherwise. Raising and updating are open to tokens (`source: api`); completing (what was done, by whom) and cancelling are a person's (`forbidTokens`, "Complete work orders", "Cancel work orders"). A completed work order does not replace the planned task's maintenance record. Open ones are counted in `counts.openWorkOrders` of what's due. Shown on the Maintenance page.
- **Calibration instrument link** (needs HACCP: `404 module_inactive` when it is off, `403 module_forbidden` when the caller can't read it). A maintained asset may point at equipment of the HACCP calibration register (`site_assets.calibration_instrument_id`, an extension table keyed by the asset's id; the equipment needs a calibration frequency, and one asset links one instrument: `409 instrument_linked`). Set it with `PATCH /maintenance/assets/:id` `{ calibrationInstrumentId }` (Maintenance's link to a calibration instrument; the asset itself is added and changed in the register, so there is no `POST /maintenance/assets` any more). The asset then shows the instrument's calibration state (the same rule as HACCP's what's-due) and last calibration, read from HACCP; nothing in HACCP is written. Without HACCP access, `GET /maintenance/assets` returns `calibrationInstrument: null`. `GET /maintenance/calibration-instruments` lists what can be linked. Assets that were in both registers before the migration (a maintained asset linked to its HACCP instrument) stay two assets, linked as before.

Maintenance managers receive `maintenance_due` reminders independently of Site. The module keeps the existing table and sync entity names. API tokens can maintain tasks and open work orders. Completion records, corrections, clearances, and closing work orders require a signed-in person.

Migration `0098_maintenance` carries existing Site grants, saved workspace selections, token and assistant allowlists, and reminders forward. Installations with an explicit `KNOTWORKING_MODULES` list must add `maintenance` to keep the extracted workflows available. Include `assets` when listing Maintenance, Site, or HACCP. Existing `/site/maintenance/*` API paths and `/site/calibration-instruments` still work with the corresponding Maintenance access.

## Checklists module

Open **Operations → Checklists** to create a template for an opening, closing, handover or other routine. Add the items and specify a recurring interval or complete it as needed. Templates can be archived and restored. Editing a template leaves the wording of existing results intact.

Record one pass, fail or N/A result per item. Failed items require a note. A failed check opens a follow-up with the failed items and their notes. A person with write access records the resolution. Correcting a check appends a replacement, supersedes its open follow-up and creates a new one if the replacement fails. Previously resolved actions remain in history.

The general routes live under `/checklists`. The workspace needs only the Checklists module for these routes. API tokens can read schedules, results and follow-ups and maintain templates. Recording a result, correcting it and resolving a follow-up require a signed-in person.

Site uses the same checklist engine for its existing pre-start and allergen changeover routes under `/site/checklists`. These routes need access to Site and Checklists. Production links and allergen comparisons also need Production read access. Site continues to control food sign-off and glass holds. General checklists and food checks have separate access scopes.

Migration `0099_checklists` retains existing templates, results and IDs, and carries Site grants, saved module selections and allowlists across. Add `checklists` to an explicit `KNOTWORKING_MODULES` list to keep Site's food checks available.

## Site programmes module

The prerequisite programmes BRCGS treats apart from the HACCP plan. Code: `packages/core/src/site.ts`, `packages/db/src/schema/site.ts`, `apps/server/src/{services,routes}/site.ts`, `apps/mcp/src/tools/site.ts`, and the web pages `/<workspace>/site` (what's due), `/site/pest`, `/site/glass`, `/site/storage` and `/site/checks`.

Site needs Assets for its areas. Pre-start and allergen changeover checks use the Checklists module. Users with Maintenance read access also see maintenance schedules and counts in the due overview, and can include maintenance records in the Site audit export.

- **Records are append-only.** Pest visits, glass checks, storage readings and checklist checks are never updated or deleted, except one-time stamps (a reading's review or a check's sign-off). A correction (`POST .../:id/correct`, with a reason) appends a new row that points at the old one (`corrects_*_id`; a record is corrected once); the replaced row no longer counts for what's due, is marked `correctedBy...`, and leaves review and sign-off queues. Corrections are a person's act; correcting an out-of-limit storage reading also needs `manage`. A corrected check needs its own sign-off.
- **Rules for API tokens.** Agents may read, keep the registers (devices, glass items, storage units, checklists), enter pest control visits, record storage readings, raise and fill in a glass breakage, and raise or fill in corrective actions. They can't sign off, and no sign-off is among the actions an owner can open to tokens: all are `forbidTokens` with `personOnlyActions` entries. Sign-offs (all `manage`): review storage readings, close corrective actions, release an area after a breakage, sign off a pre-start or changeover check. Records that attest a person did something (glass checks and completed checks) and corrections are person-only too (`write`).
- **Pest control** (`site_pest_devices`, `site_pest_visits`, `site_pest_findings`). Devices have a contractor code (unique), kind, location and optional area. A visit holds the contractor, technician, date (not in the future), summary, recommendations, actions taken, the next visit date and findings: a device (or a place with no device), activity (none, low, moderate, high), pest, count, note and action. Activity needs the pest named. What's due is the next visit date of the latest visit that no correction replaced. Pest control never drops off the list for want of a date: when that visit has no next date, the item stays as `due` with `reason: no_next_visit` ("No next visit set"), and when no visit was ever recorded but active devices exist it is `due` with `reason: no_visit_recorded`; both count as needing attention (the web board shows "Not set", MCP `get_site_due` returns `reason`). Moderate or high activity on the last visit is counted for attention. API tokens may record visits (`source: api`).
- **Glass and brittle plastics** (`site_glass_items`, `site_glass_checks`, `site_glass_breakages`). Items belong to an area, with kind, description, quantity and a check frequency (default monthly); items are marked `removed`, never deleted. A check round (`POST /site/glass/checks`, several items at once) records intact, damaged or missing; damaged or missing needs a note. Due checks are grouped per area in what's due; items whose latest check was damaged or missing are counted. A breakage holds the area from the moment it is raised (a token may raise one), records the area, the register item if any, what was quarantined and the clean-down while it is open (`PATCH`), and is released by a person with `manage` once the clean-down is recorded (`409 clean_down_missing`); a released breakage never changes. **An open breakage also blocks pre-start sign-off in its area**: approving a check (pre-start or allergen changeover, whichever template is tied to that area; the check's copied `areaId`) is refused with `409 area_on_hold`, naming the breakage, until it is released. Recording the check is still allowed, and holding (rejecting) one is too; only approval is blocked. Allergen changeover checks are blocked by the same rule, since production can't restart in a held area either way. Checks with no area aren't affected. Check responses carry `areaHold` (the open breakage, for a check awaiting sign-off), and the web checks page shows it in place of the Sign off button.
- **Storage temperatures** (`site_storage_units`, `site_storage_readings`, `site_corrective_actions`). Units (chiller, freezer, ambient, other) have a minimum and/or maximum (inclusive), a unit (default °C) and a reading frequency (default daily). A reading is judged by `evaluateStorageReading` (core, built on HACCP's `evaluateReading`) and keeps a copy of the limits it was judged against, so changing a unit later doesn't rewrite history. **An out-of-limit reading opens a corrective action in the same transaction, whoever recorded it.** A person's reading is `approved` as entered; a token's (an agent, or a future sensor feed) is `source: api` and `pending` until a person with `manage` approves or rejects it (single or bulk; a note is required to reject). Pending readings count as done; rejected ones don't. Rejecting doesn't close the corrective action. Corrective actions are closed by a person with `manage` once what was done, the root cause and the preventive action are recorded (`409 site_action_incomplete`). A reading has at most one open corrective action (`409 site_action_exists`); a correction still out of limits reuses an open action of its chain, or opens a new one when the earlier one was closed.
- **Pre-start and allergen changeover checks** (`site_checklist_templates`, `site_checklist_items`, `site_checklist_checks`, `site_checklist_check_items`). A checklist is per line or area, with items, and an optional frequency (a daily pre-start check; optionally only while something is in production) or none (done when needed). A check holds pass, fail or n/a for every item (the wording is copied, so editing the checklist doesn't rewrite history; a failed item needs a note). An allergen changeover check may reference the production batch before and the one after. It starts `pending`; a person with `manage` signs it off before production starts or holds it. A check with a failed item can only be held (`409 check_failed`). Checks waiting for sign-off count as done for what's due; failed or held ones don't.
- **What's due** (`GET /site/due`, `include=due|all`, `programme`) is computed on read across all programmes using HACCP's `checkDueState` and `dateDueState`, with `counts` of what waits for a person (readings to review, checks to sign off, clearances, open breakages, open corrective actions, damaged glass, pest activity).
- **Reading and sync.** Nothing is in the bootstrap; every write goes to the sync log (`site_pest_device`, `site_pest_visit`, `site_pest_finding`, `site_glass_item`, `site_glass_check`, `site_glass_breakage`, `site_storage_unit`, `site_storage_reading`, `site_corrective_action`, `site_checklist_template`, `site_checklist_item`, `site_checklist_check`, `site_checklist_check_item`), filtered by module access. Notifications are swept for what falls due (`site_check_due`, `pest_visit_due`), and an out-of-limit storage reading notifies as it is recorded (`reading_out_of_limit`): see [notifications](notifications.md). The module adds nothing to search. Glass breakages take photos as attachments (see [Attachments on module records](#attachments-on-module-records)). `/site` sits under the workspace, so no reserved slug or Caddy rule is needed.
- **Correcting records in the web app.** Every record that can be corrected has a Correct button for people with `write` (never tokens): a pest visit (the whole visit again, findings included), a glass check (the Glass page lists recent checks), a storage reading (an out-of-limit one needs `manage`) and a pre-start or changeover check. Each asks for the reason; the original stays and is struck through in the printed records.
- **Four-eyes on a release.** Releasing an area after a glass breakage is refused with `403 four_eyes` to whoever reported the breakage or recorded its clean-down (`canSignOffSiteRecord`, `assertFourEyes`). Someone else with `manage` on `site` signs. **Decision:** like SOPs and labels, the only member with `manage` on the module is exempt, so a one-manager site isn't locked out. A token that raised a breakage counts as its owner.
- **Printed site records** (`/site/records`) now include glass checks and glass breakages (what was quarantined, the clean-down, who released the area; each with a CSV export), and a changeover check prints its allergen warnings. `GET .../glass/breakages` takes `from` and `to`.
- **Pest trends** (`GET /site/pest/trends?groupBy=device|area|pest&from&to`): sightings per visit date from the latest, uncorrected visits (a finding's count, or one for activity with no count; a clear check is zero), with the total, the highest activity and whether the last visit is up or down on the one before (`pestTrendSeries`, core). The pest page shows one small plain-SVG chart per series (`TrendChart`, shown on `/design`), red bars for moderate or high activity.
- **Glass register import** (`POST /site/glass/items/import`, `dryRun` to check first; the Glass page's "Import CSV"; the same dialog and result as the items and suppliers imports). Columns: `area` (HACCP area code or name), `description`, and optionally `kind`, `quantity`, `location`, `check frequency` (monthly, weekly, quarterly, annually, every 2 weeks, …) and `status` (active or removed). **Decision:** an item is identified by its area and description (case-insensitively), so re-importing a file changes nothing and a changed quantity, kind, location or frequency updates the item; nothing is deleted, and nothing is written while any row is wrong. Open to tokens like the rest of register keeping; no MCP tool.
- **Other measures than temperature.** A storage unit has a `measure`: `temperature` (default, °C), `humidity` (%RH, limits between 0 and 100), `pressure` (Pa) or `other` (the unit has to be given). Limits, frequency, judging, the corrective action and review work exactly as for temperature. **Decision:** one unit measures one thing (a chiller with a humidity limit is a second unit), and the measure is fixed once the unit has readings (`409 measure_has_readings`). `GET .../storage/units?measure=`.
- **Allergen risk of a changeover.** A changeover check that names the batch before and the batch after carries `allergenRisk` (`changeoverAllergenRisk`, core): the allergens the earlier batch's approved recipe version contains and the later one does not (`carriedOver`), and of those the ones the later product doesn't declare even as "may contain" (`notDeclaredAfter`); `status` is `review`, `none` or `unknown` (a batch isn't named or has no approved allergen record). **It only warns:** it never blocks recording or signing off, because the clean-down is the control. `GET /site/checklists/allergen-risk?previousBatchId&nextBatchId` gives it before the check is recorded (the check form shows it live); it is on the checks page and in the printed records.
- **MCP**: `get_site_due`, `list_site_registers`, `list_site_records`, `get_pest_trends`, `record_pest_visit`, `record_storage_readings`, `save_site_register_entry`, `report_glass_breakage`, `propose_site_action`. No tools for sign-offs, attestations, corrections or the glass import.

Not built yet: sensor feeds (the API accepts readings from a token, but there is no integration or device registry), device maps, scheduling by production day rather than calendar, notifications for new breakdowns, a work-order cost report, and the reverse link (a "maintained as" line) on the HACCP calibration page.

## Labels and nutrition module

Nutrition on items, rolled up through recipes; the ingredient declaration a UK label needs (PPDS: name, full ingredients list, allergens emphasised); and product label text, drafted from those, edited, and approved per recipe version. Needs `recipes` (and through it `items`). Code: `packages/core/src/labels.ts` (every computation: roll-up, rounding, declaration, staleness), `packages/db/src/schema/labels.ts`, `apps/server/src/{services,routes}/labels.ts`, `apps/mcp/src/tools/labels.ts`, and the web pages `/<workspace>/labels` (labels), `/<workspace>/labels/<id>` (one label) and `/<workspace>/labels/nutrition` (items' nutrition).

- **Where it lives**: the API is under `/v1/workspaces/:slug/labelling/…` because `/labels` is taken by task labels (tasks module); the web pages are under `/<workspace>/labels`, a module of their own rather than a tab of a recipe, because labels are reviewed across products (what needs review) and nutrition is entered per ingredient, not per recipe. `labels` is not a reserved workspace slug: the pages live under the workspace. The tables are `item_label_data` and `product_labels` (not `labels`, the task labels table).
- **Items' labelling data** (`item_label_data`, one row per food item; `PUT /labelling/items/:itemId`, `write`, API tokens allowed): nutrition per 100 g, or per 100 ml for liquids specified that way (energy kJ and kcal, fat, of which saturates, carbohydrate, of which sugars, protein, salt; fibre optional), as `numeric(10,3)` (salt needs three places); the name used in an ingredients list (`declarationName`, default the item's name); for a bought-in compound ingredient its own ingredients from the specification (`components`); and where the figures came from. Validation refuses saturates above fat, sugars above carbohydrate, more than 100 g per 100 g, and kJ that isn't about 4.2 × kcal (the two typed the wrong way round), and polyols plus erythritol above the carbohydrate. **Energy extras** (`polyols`, `erythritol`, `organicAcids`, `alcohol`, g per 100, default none) are optional columns used only by the calculated energy method below; they are never declared. **Vitamins and minerals** (`micronutrients`: vitamins A, D, E, C, thiamin, riboflavin, niacin, B6, folate, B12, potassium, calcium, magnesium, iron, zinc, each in its own unit per 100 g or ml) and **cooking retention** (`retention`: per nutrient, the fraction 0 to 1 that survives cooking this item in a recipe; 0.6 loses 40%; none means no loss) are optional, see the roll-up. Packaging has no labelling data (400), and packaging lines on a recipe are left out of everything below. A full replacement: what is left out is cleared (the MCP tool merges for agents).
- **Nutrition roll-up** (`rollUpNutrition`, computed on read, never stored): each food line's quantity is converted to the unit of the item's basis (g or ml) with `convertQuantity` (density or unit weight to cross kinds), its nutrients summed, and the total divided by what one batch makes (the yield in g, or ml when the product is a liquid, or count × unit weight for a product sold by count): **moisture lost in cooking concentrates the nutrients**, so 1000 g in the bowl baked to 800 g has 1.25 × the nutrients per 100 g. **Sub-recipes** are followed: a line whose item is made by an active recipe with an approved version uses that version's own roll-up (recursively; when several active recipes make an item, the most recently approved is followed); otherwise the item's entered nutrition. **Missing data is reported, never counted as zero**: `missing` lists each item with the reason (`no_nutrition`, `unconvertible`, `no_yield` for a product counted without a unit weight, `cycle`) and the sub-recipes it was reached through (`path`), and `complete` is false; the partial values are still given for information. Fibre is optional: when an ingredient has no fibre figure, fibre is `null` (not understated) and `fibreMissing` names the items. Also given: the mixing-bowl weight per batch and the yield factor. **Nutrient losses beyond the yield (vitamins and minerals only, by decision)**: macronutrients are concentrated by the yield and nothing else (a lab analysis typed onto the label is the answer for fat lost in draining, say). Vitamins and minerals are summed in `micronutrients` (per 100 g or ml of the finished product, in each nutrient's unit) after each ingredient's own retention factors (amount × amount used × retention ÷ yield), through sub-recipes (a sub-recipe's output item's retention applies again when it is cooked on). Ingredients with no figure at all are listed in `micronutrientsMissing` and the totals are then a lower bound (a missing figure is never zero). There are no built-in retention factors: they come from the person's own retention table or analysis (for instance the USDA table of nutrient retention factors). The facts response adds `micronutrientRows` (value and % of the nutrient reference value, Regulation 1169/2011 Annex XIII part A) and the label page shows them in a card marked "never declared": **the declared panel stays to what UK PPDS food needs** (energy, fat, saturates, carbohydrate, sugars, protein, salt, fibre optional).
- **Rounding** (`roundNutrient`, `nutritionPanel`): the European Commission guidance on tolerances and rounding (2012), which the FSA follows. Energy to 1 kJ / kcal; fat, carbohydrate, sugars, protein and fibre to 1 g from 10 g, 0.1 g above 0.5 g, otherwise "<0.5 g"; saturates to 1 g from 10 g, 0.1 g above 0.1 g, otherwise "<0.1 g"; salt to 0.1 g from 1 g, 0.01 g above 0.0125 g, otherwise "<0.01 g"; exactly zero is "0 g". The `<` form is used rather than "0 g" for small non-zero amounts. A panel keeps the raw values beside the declared ones.
- **Ingredient declaration** (`computeLabelFacts`): ingredients in **descending order of weight at the mixing bowl**; the same item on several lines is one entry; lines that can't be weighed (a volume without a density) go last with a warning. **Compound ingredients**: a sub-recipe is either listed by its declared name with its own ingredients in brackets, heaviest first (`bracketed`, default) or expanded into the list, each component placed by its share of the product (`expanded`, merging an item reached twice); a bought-in compound's `components` are always bracketed. A sub-recipe's ingredients count their **ingoing weight**: the quantity of the sub-recipe used ÷ what one batch of it makes (its yield in grams) × their quantity in it, so 400 g of a sauce of 900 g tomato and 100 g oil reduced to 500 g used 720 g of tomato (in the order and in QUID). Only when the sub-recipe's yield can't be converted to grams is its bowl weight used, with a `sub_recipe_yield` warning. The nutrition roll-up already divides each sub-recipe's nutrients by its own yield, so moisture is lost once and the two agree. **Allergens**: the 14 are emphasised in the words that name them (wheat, milk, butter, soya, …; only allergens the item contains, never part of a word, so "butternut", "buckwheat", "coconut" and "nutmeg" never match), except in phrases that aren't that allergen (`nonAllergenPhrases`, checked first and per allergen: "cocoa butter", "shea butter", "cream of tartar", "coconut milk", "coconut cream", plant milks and nut butters, "butter beans" for milk; "pine nuts" for tree nuts; so "peanut butter" emphasises "peanut" and not "butter"), or, when a name doesn't mention an allergen its item contains, the allergen is added in brackets ("Whey powder (**milk**)") with an `allergen_not_named` warning to check the wording (cereals get "gluten", which should be the specific cereal). Text with `**…**` markers is taken as written. The declaration is **text runs** (`LabelSegment[]`: `{ text, emphasis? }`), never HTML; clients render emphasis in bold; the editing form is the same text with `**` markers (`parseSegmentsMarkup`, `segmentsMarkup`). **QUID**: items marked for a percentage get "(31%)" after their name: ingoing weight against the finished weight when moisture was lost (FIC Annex VIII 4(a)), otherwise against the bowl, whole numbers from 10% and one decimal below; over 100% it is stated as "150 g per 100 g". **Allergens** are the recipes module's `rollUpAllergens` over every food item reached through every level of sub-recipe; "may contain" adds the product item's own (cross-contact on site); `allergenMismatch` compares with the product item's declaration. The drafted allergen statement is "Allergy advice: for allergens, see ingredients in bold. May contain …".
- **Computed for any version**: `GET /labelling/recipes/:id/versions/:versionId?compoundStyle=&quid=<ids>&volatile=<ids>&energyMethod=&negligibleAsZero=&portionSize=&portionLabel=` returns all of it for a draft, approved or superseded recipe version, without storing anything (checking a draft recipe before approving it).
- **Preview of a draft recipe version**: the same response carries `preview`: the label as it would read (product name, ingredients, allergen statement, panel, traffic lights and the default storage and date wording from the product item) with `previewOnly: true` and `watermark` (`DRAFT` for a draft recipe version, `SUPERSEDED` for a superseded one, none for the approved). It is never stored, so it can't be approved: a product label is only drafted from the **approved** version. The web page `/<workspace>/labels/preview/<recipeId>/<versionId>` shows it with the watermark across it, linked from a draft on the recipe page.
- **Product labels** (`product_labels`): one per label version of a recipe **variant**, numbered 1, 2, … per recipe and variant, with document control as recipe versions: at most one `draft` (the only editable one) and one `approved` label per recipe and variant (partial unique indexes on the recipe and the lower-cased variant); approving supersedes the approved one; approved and superseded labels never change and are kept. A label holds the product name, the ingredients (text runs), the allergen statement and the rolled-up allergens, whether nutrition is declared and the panel (raw and declared) with where it came from (`panelSource`: `computed` from a complete roll-up, or `entered` by a person), storage instructions, date guidance (use by / best before), net quantity, the **GTIN**, notes, the compound style and QUID items, and a snapshot of what its computed parts were drafted from.
  - **Variants, several labels per recipe**: a recipe's labels are told apart by `variant` (default `Standard`; "400 g tray", "Shop own label"). Each variant is drafted, edited, numbered, approved and superseded on its own, so approving one never touches another, and each has its own text (name, ingredients, storage, net quantity, so a customer's own-label wording is just another variant) and its own options below. The variant is named when first drafted and fixed after (case-insensitive: "shop OWN label" is the same variant). A later draft of a variant carries over from that variant's approved label. A label's page lists the recipe's other labels, and the history shows only its own variant's versions. Stale notifications (`label_stale`) are per label as before.
  - **Drafting** (`POST /labelling/labels` with `recipeId` and optionally `variant`, `write`, API tokens allowed): always against the recipe's **approved** version (`409 no_approved_version`; archived recipes `409 recipe_archived`); one draft per recipe and variant (`409 label_draft_exists`, with the draft's `id`). The ingredients, allergens, statement and nutrition panel are computed; the name, storage, date wording and net quantity come from the approved label when there is one (a correction keeps the reviewed text), else from the product item (its name; its storage condition: "Keep refrigerated at 0–5°C." with "Use by" for chilled food, "Best before" otherwise, with its shelf life; its unit weight when sold by count). **A panel is only drafted when the roll-up is complete** (and recorded as `computed`); otherwise it is left empty and `facts.nutrition.missing` says why.
  - **Label options** (stored on the label, carried to the variant's next draft; set when drafting or refreshing, the portion and zero rule also by `PATCH`):
    - **Portion** (`portionSize` in g, or ml for a liquid, and `portionLabel` such as "1 slice"): the panel gains per-portion values, rounded by the same rules as the per 100 values (from the unrounded per 100 figures), and the percentage of the **reference intake** per portion (Regulation 1169/2011 Annex XIII: energy 2000 kcal, fat 70 g, saturates 20 g, carbohydrate 260 g, sugars 90 g, protein 50 g, salt 6 g), to a whole percent. Energy's %RI is from the kcal. Fibre has none. A portion change on a draft re-rounds from the panel's raw values at once (typed-in panels too), without a refresh.
    - **Negligible amounts** (`negligibleAsZero`): declare amounts below the "<0.5 g" (saturates "<0.1 g", salt "<0.01 g") threshold as "0 g". Whether that is acceptable for the product is the manufacturer's call; the default stays "<0.5 g". Also applied by a `PATCH` without a refresh.
    - **Energy** (`energyMethod`): `declared` (default: the ingredients' declared energy, summed) or `calculated`: kJ and kcal worked out from the rolled-up fat, carbohydrate, protein and fibre with the conversion factors of Regulation 1169/2011 Annex XIV (fat 37 kJ / 9 kcal, carbohydrate and protein 17 / 4, fibre 8 / 2, polyols 10 / 2.4, organic acids 13 / 3, alcohol 29 / 7, erythritol 0; fibre left out when unknown). The declared carbohydrate includes polyols (Annex I), so polyols and erythritol are taken out of it before the 17 / 4 factor and counted at their own, which `calculateEnergy` tests cover with worked numbers (70 g carbohydrate of which 60 g polyols: 770 kJ / 184 kcal; 10 g alcohol: 290 kJ / 70 kcal). Salatrims are not held. The raw energy on the panel is then the calculated one. A typed-in panel keeps its typed energy (`declared`).
    - **Added water and volatile ingredients** (`volatileItemIds`, a per-label list of items, rather than a flag on every recipe line): FIC Annex VII part A lists them by their weight **in the finished product**, not at the bowl. A volatile item's listed weight is its weight at use minus its share of the moisture the product loses between the bowl and the finished weight (bowl weight minus yield in grams), shared out in proportion to the weights of all the marked items (anywhere in a compound too), never below zero; everything else keeps its weight at use, and the entries then re-sort (`finishedG` beside `weightG`). QUID and the allergen and nutrition computations are unaffected. Warnings: `volatile_not_found` (marked but not in the recipe) and `volatile_loss_exceeds` (the loss is more than the marked items weigh: the loss is assumed to come entirely from them, so they are placed as nothing left). **The 5% rule** (FIC Annex VII part A 1(b)): added water that does not exceed 5% of the finished product is left out of the list. It applies to any ingredient named as water (`isWaterName`: "Water", "Drinking water", "Cold water", …, not "coconut water"), by its weight in the finished product when it is marked volatile, else by its weight at use (which can only be more than what is left, so water under the limit at use is under it in the product); exactly 5% is left out, anything above is listed. It needs the finished weight (`water_rule_not_applied` warning when the yield can't be converted to grams, and the water stays). Left-out water is reported in `omittedWater` (code, grams, percent) and the label page says so; nutrition, QUID and allergens are unaffected. To list it anyway, add it back by editing the text.
  - **Front-of-pack traffic lights** (`trafficLights`, computed, on every label with a panel and in the preview): green, amber or red for fat, saturates, sugars and salt by the UK guidance thresholds (per 100 g for food, per 100 ml for liquids: fat 3 / 17.5 g (1.5 / 8.75), saturates 1.5 / 5 (0.75 / 2.5), sugars 5 / 22.5 (2.5 / 11.25), salt 0.3 / 1.5 (0.3 / 0.75)), judged on the declared (rounded) values so the colour agrees with the printed figure ("23 g" of sugars is red, "22 g" amber), and a portion above its own limit (fat 21 g, saturates 6 g, sugars 27 g (13.5 g for liquids), salt 1.8 g) makes it red as well. The thresholds are in `trafficLightThresholds` (`@knotworking/core`); check them against the current FSA guidance. Traffic lights are voluntary and there is no artwork: the label's page shows them as a card.
  - **Barcode (GTIN)**: `product_labels.gtin`, per label variant (a pack size has its own number), set with `PATCH` (or `draft_product_label`'s `gtin`): 13 digits (EAN-13) or a 12-digit UPC-A (stored with a leading zero), spaces and hyphens allowed, check digit validated (`parseGtin`, `gtinCheckDigit`, 400 with the expected digit); empty clears it. It is carried to the variant's next draft. The label preview and the print page draw it as an EAN-13 in SVG (`ean13Layout` in core gives the 95 modules and bar positions, `Ean13Barcode` renders them: black on white, quiet zones, longer guard bars, nominal 37.29 mm wide). GTIN-8 and GTIN-14 are not supported; artwork is still not built.
  - **Four-eyes on approval**: `product_labels.last_edited_by` is whoever created, edited (`PATCH`) or refreshed the draft last. Approving needs someone else with `manage` on labels (`403 four_eyes`), unless the approver is the only member with `manage` (`canApproveProductLabel`, the rule SOPs use). Editing a draft therefore passes the approval to the other manager.
  - **Editing** (`PATCH`, drafts only, `409 label_not_draft` otherwise): any text field; `ingredients` as text with `**` markers; `nutrition` typed in (from a laboratory analysis, rounded the same way, and recorded as `entered`) or `null` to declare none (a product exempt from the nutrition declaration: PPDS food unless a claim is made). A typed-in panel that is just the partial values of an incomplete roll-up (every mandatory value equal to 3 decimal places, which counts the missing ingredients as zero) is refused (`409 nutrition_incomplete`, naming them); the web form prefills only from a complete roll-up and otherwise starts empty, listing what is missing. **Refreshing** (`POST …/refresh`, optionally changing the compound style, QUID items and whether nutrition is declared) redrafts the computed parts from current data and moves the draft onto the recipe's current approved version; the name, storage, date wording, net quantity and notes are kept. **Deleting** (`DELETE`) removes a draft only.
  - **Approval** (`POST …/approve`) is a sign-off: `manage` and a signed-in person, never an API token (`forbidTokens`, in `personOnlyActions` as "Approve product labels"; it can't be opened under Settings → API access) and there is no MCP tool. It records who and when and refuses: a draft made from a version that is no longer the approved one (`409 version_superseded`), a draft that is out of date with the data (`409 label_out_of_date`, with the reasons: refresh it and check it again), no product name or ingredients (400), and a declared nutrition panel that is missing, or computed while the roll-up is incomplete (`409 nutrition_incomplete`, naming the items). A computed panel is only ever stored from a complete roll-up and the staleness check catches data changed since, so the second is a safeguard; an `entered` panel is the person's statement, and the label page and the approval prompt say it was typed in.
  - **Stale** (computed on read, never stored, so nothing edits an approved label): an approved label **needs review** when the recipe has a newer approved version (`recipe_version_superseded`), a sub-recipe was followed through a different approved version since (`sub_recipe_changed`, with `ingredients_changed` for items that appeared or went), or an ingredient's (or the product item's) allergens, an ingredient's nutrition (ignored when the label declares none) or its declared name or components changed (`allergens_changed`, `nutrition_changed`, `declaration_changed`), or an ingredient's (or the product item's) unit, unit weight or density changed (`conversion_changed`: they convert quantities to weight and the yield to grams, so they move the panel, the order and QUID; reported even when no nutrition is declared), each with the item codes. Snapshots stored before conversions were recorded have no `conversion` key and are not compared on it, so existing labels don't all turn stale at once; the next draft or refresh records it. A stale draft is "out of date" and must be refreshed before approval. Superseded labels are history and never stale. The correction of a stale approved label is a new draft, approved in turn.
- **Reading** (`read`): `GET /labelling/items` (food items with their data and `effective` nutrition: `source` `entered`, `recipe` or `missing`, the values, what is missing and `madeBy`; filters `nutrition`, `type`, `status`, `q`; paged), `GET /labelling/items/:itemId`, `GET /labelling/labels` (filters `status` (`current` = draft and approved, default; each status; `all`), `stale`, `recipeId`, `q` on the product name and the recipe's code or name; paged, each with `stale` and `staleReasons`), `GET /labelling/labels/:id` (the label, its recipe and version, the recipe's approved version now, staleness, the facts computed now for comparison, and the history of the recipe's labels).
- **Sync**: writes go to the sync log as `item_label_data` and `product_label` (without the source snapshot), filtered by module access. Nothing is in the bootstrap; the module adds nothing to search or attachments.
- **Notifications**: the sweep sends `label_stale` to members with `manage` on labels once per approved label that needs review (dedupe `label:<id>`), using the same stale check as the list, so each variant is covered; see [notifications](notifications.md). Nothing else is notified (a draft is not).
- **Importing nutrition from CSV** (`POST /labelling/items/import`, `write`, tokens allowed; the same shape as the items import: `{ csv, fileName?, dryRun? }`, `dryRun` previews what would be created or updated and lists every problem by row, a real import writes every row in one transaction or none, `400 invalid_rows`; up to 5000 rows; core `parseNutritionCsv`, server `services/labels-import.ts`). Rows are matched to existing non-packaging items by stock code (`code`), else by exact item name; an import never creates items. Columns (any order, headers matched without case or punctuation): `code`/`name`; `basis` (`100g` or `100ml`, default the item's existing basis, else per 100 ml for a volume unit); `energy kj` and/or `energy kcal` (one is completed from the other); `fat`, `saturates`, `carbohydrate`, `sugars`, `protein` and `salt` (or `sodium`: g, or `sodium mg`, × 2.5; an explicit salt cell wins) are all required; optional `fibre`, `polyols`, `erythritol`, `organic acids`, `alcohol`, the vitamins and minerals, `declaration name`, `components`, `source`. **McCance and Widdowson's (CoFID) columns** are recognised ("Fat (g)", "Total sugars (g)", "AOAC fibre (g)", "Satd FA /100g fd (g)" (per 100 g of food; the per 100 g of fat column is ignored), "Sodium (mg)", "Energy (kcal) (kcal)", "Energy (kJ) (kJ)", "Vitamin C (mg)", …); their own "Food Code" is not a stock code, so add a stock code column (or match by name). Cells: `Tr` or `trace`, `<0.1` read as 0, brackets (estimates) are ignored, `N`, `-`, `n/a` and empty are not given (an error for a mandatory nutrient). The nutrition cross-checks of the items form apply (saturates within fat, sugars within carbohydrate, kJ against kcal, polyols within carbohydrate, 100 g caps). Vitamin and mineral columns replace the item's vitamins and minerals (an empty cell clears that one); a file without them leaves them alone; retention factors are never imported; declaration name and components are kept unless the file has the column; the source is the `source` cell, else "Imported from <file name>". Unchanged rows are counted, not written; every written row goes to the sync log as `item_label_data`. In the web app: Ingredient nutrition → Import CSV (check, then import).
- **MCP**: `import_item_nutrition_csv`, `list_item_nutrition`, `set_item_nutrition`, `get_label_facts`, `list_product_labels`, `get_product_label`, `draft_product_label`. Agents enter nutrition from specifications (including polyols, organic acids, alcohol, `vitamins_and_minerals` and `retention_after_cooking`; fields left out keep their values), import it from CSV (`dry_run` first), compute and draft labels (`gtin`, `variant`, `volatile`, `energy_method`, `negligible_as_zero`, `portion_size`, `portion_label` are options of `draft_product_label`); approval is a person's, and four-eyes applies to it.
- **Web**: Labels lists the current labels (Needs review, Drafts, All versions; paged) and drafts one from a recipe, found by search among the active recipes with an approved version; the draft's compound style and nutrition declaration default to the recipe's approved label's (the server carries them over) and are only sent when changed. A label's page shows the text as it reads on pack (allergens in bold, the nutrition table and where the panel came from), raw against declared values, why it is stale, what nutrition is missing, the wording warnings and the history, and offers editing, typing in a panel, the portion and rounding, refreshing (compound style, QUID, energy method, volatile ingredients, nutrition declared or not), approval and deletion of a draft, or a new draft of an approved label. Ingredient nutrition lists food items with where their nutrition comes from and edits an item's data.

Not built yet: label artwork (the text, panel and barcode print from the label's page); barcodes other than EAN-13/UPC-A (GTIN-8, GTIN-14, 2D codes); losses of macronutrients in cooking beyond the yield, and built-in retention factors (they are the person's own figures); a bulk download of McCance and Widdowson's (the importer reads its columns from a spreadsheet exported as CSV).

## Specifications module

Controlled, versioned specifications for ingredients, packaging and products (Phase 3, milestone 3.2.1). A specification is the agreed standard an item is bought, made and released against: limits, allergens, storage, shelf life, provenance and claims, agreed with the supplier or customer, approved, and reviewed. Needs `items`. Opt-in (`defaultAccess: "none"`). Code: `packages/core/src/specifications.ts` (validation, the version comparison, the item cross-check, approval rules and review state), `packages/db/src/schema/specifications.ts`, `apps/server/src/{services,routes}/specifications.ts`, `apps/mcp/src/tools/specifications.ts`, and the web pages `/<workspace>/specifications` (the register) and `/<workspace>/specifications/<id>` (one specification).

- **Identity and party**: `specifications` holds the document number (`code`, unique among live specifications), title, the item (fixed) and who it is agreed with: a bought-in item's `supplier_id`, a product's `customer_name` (free text until the Customers module is merged), or neither (internal). Its kind (ingredient, packaging, product) follows the item's type. An item can have several (one per supplier, a customer's own). The party is fixed once a version has been approved, because that version records that party's agreement.
- **Document control**: content lives in `specification_versions`, as recipes: at most one draft (the only editable version) and one approved version per specification, enforced by partial unique indexes; approving supersedes the previous approved version, which never changes. Limits are `specification_parameters` rows (category, name, unit, min, target, max as `numeric(18,6)`, a written limit such as "Absent in 25 g", method), kept as rows so test results (3.2.2) can refer to the exact parameter they were judged against. A never-approved specification can be deleted; an approved one is archived.
- **Approval**: `POST …/specifications/:id/draft/approve` needs `manage`, a person (`forbidTokens`, "Approve specifications" in `personOnlyActions`) and four eyes: not whoever last saved the draft, unless nobody else has `manage` (`canApproveSpecification`). `specificationApprovalBlockers` refuses a draft without storage and shelf life (ingredients and products) or packaging details (packaging), without a future review date, or, for a supplier's or customer's specification, without their agreement (who and when, not in the future). The detail response lists the blockers so the page can say why approval is disabled.
- **Changes**: `diffSpecifications` compares a version with the one before it: fields (with "impacting" ones marked: allergens, may contain, storage, shelf life, claims, origin), parameters matched by category and name (added, removed, changed) and allergens newly declared or dropped. "May contain" becoming "contains" counts as an added allergen.
- **Item cross-check**: `itemDiscrepancies` compares an approved specification (and a draft, as "once approved") with the item record that recipes, labels and stock use. Serious: the specification contains an allergen the item doesn't declare as contained, may contain one the item doesn't mention, or the item's shelf life is longer than the specification's. Check: the item declares an allergen the specification doesn't mention, a shorter shelf life, different storage. Nothing rewrites the item; a person decides which record is wrong.
- **Change impact**: `GET …/specifications/:id/impact` lists the recipes that make the item or whose draft or approved version uses it, followed through work-in-progress recipes up to 10 levels (`depthLimited` when it stops early), their draft and approved product labels, and planned or in-progress production batches of those recipes, each capped at 500 (`truncated`). Each part is listed only when the reader can read `recipes`, `labels` or `production`; otherwise it is `null` and named in `hidden`, and the page says records may be missing rather than that nothing is affected.
- **Review**: the approved version's `review_due_on` gives `reviewState` (`overdue`, `due_soon` within 30 days of the workspace's today, `ok`, or `none` when nothing is approved). The register filters by `review=overdue|due_soon|attention` (attention: either, or never approved).
- **Sync and leaks**: the sync log carries `specification` and `specification_version` metadata only (number, status, review and agreement dates, who approved); content, parameters, provenance and the agreement signatory are read through endpoints. Specifications are not in search or notifications. Files hang on the specification (`specification` attachment target: specification sheets, certificates, documents; never locked, so a new issue's sheet is filed beside the old ones).
- **MCP**: `list_specifications`, `get_specification` (`include_impact` for the impact report), `create_specification`, `save_specification_draft` (fields left out are kept from the draft, or copied from the approved version). No tool approves.
- **Not built yet** (later 3.2 milestones): test results against the parameters and risk-based sampling (3.2.2); QA release using the specification, tests and approvals (3.2.3); supplier risk, questionnaires and NPD (3.2.4); customer accounts in place of `customer_name` once Customers is merged; a "review due" notification in the sweep; specification fields copied onto goods-in checks (minimum life on receipt is recorded but not yet enforced at receipt).

## Testing module

Laboratory testing and verification (Phase 3, milestone 3.2.2): laboratories, sampling points, risk-based sampling plans, samples from stock lots and from environmental, water or air points, results judged against limits, out-of-specification investigations and trends. Needs `items` and `stock`. Opt-in. Code: `packages/core/src/testing.ts` (validation, `judgeResult`, `sampleOutcome`, `testPlanDue`, `summarizeTrend`), `packages/db/src/schema/testing.ts`, `apps/server/src/{services,routes}/testing.ts`, `apps/mcp/src/tools/testing.ts`, and the web pages `/<workspace>/testing` (samples), `/testing/due`, `/testing/trends`, `/testing/setup` and `/testing/samples/<id>`.

- **Setup**: `testing_laboratories` (accreditation and expiry, in house or external), `testing_sampling_points` (code unique among live points, kind, the hygiene or risk zone from the site's assessment, location) and `testing_plans` (a lot plan names an item, a point plan names a point; frequency in days, the risk rationale, a default laboratory, and analytes with limits). A point plan needs analytes; a lot plan without them tests against the item's approved specification. Subject, item and point are fixed once a plan is created; plans, points and laboratories are archived, never deleted.
- **Due**: `GET /testing/plans` gives each plan's next due date: its frequency after the last sample taken under it (cancelled ones don't count), or the day it started. `testPlanDue` gives `overdue`, `due`, `due_soon` (within 7 days) or `ok`, in the workspace's time zone.
- **Samples**: `testing_samples` (reference `S-2026-0001`, generated per year under the workspace lock unless given; the lot or point; plan, laboratory; business date, not in the future). Logging copies the analytes and limits onto `testing_results` rows: from the request, else the plan, else (Specifications active) the approved specification of the lot's item. That is the lot supplier's specification, else the internal one, else the only one; it is refused as ambiguous otherwise. Only parameters with a min, max or written limit are copied (a target alone has nothing to fail), each keeping `specification_parameter_id`, and the sample keeps `specification_version_id`. A later change to a plan or specification never re-judges a recorded result.
- **Judging** (`judgeResult`): a numeric value outside min/max fails, whatever else is said; a person may fail a result explicitly; a numeric value within limits passes; a written limit ("Not detected") passes only on a person's verdict. A numeric limit needs the value; a written one needs the verdict. Any fail fails the sample; any pending result keeps it `awaiting_results`; otherwise it is `complete`. Re-recording a result needs a correction reason and appends the previous value, verdict, report and recorder to `history`.
- **Failure**: in the same transaction as the failing result, the sample's investigation opens (`investigation_status = open`) and, for a lot that is `available`, the lot goes to `quarantine` with a status note naming the sample and analytes (`lot_held`), and planners of batches reserving it are notified as for any QA hold. Holding is the safe direction, so API tokens may record results (a laboratory import). Releasing the lot stays the stock module's person-only QA decision. A correction that later passes the result leaves the investigation open and the lot held for a person to resolve.
- **Investigations**: `POST /testing/samples/:id/investigation/close` needs `manage`, a person (`forbidTokens`, "Close test investigations" in `personOnlyActions`), every result recorded, findings and the action taken (root cause optional). It doesn't release the lot. A failed sample can't be cancelled; one awaiting results can, with a reason (lost, spoiled), and then takes no results.
- **Trends**: `GET /testing/trends?analyte=…` lists one analyte's results over time (case-insensitive name; cancelled samples left out; at most 1,000), narrowed to an item, a sampling point or a zone, with `summarizeTrend` totals (results, fails, fail rate, mean, min, max, latest). `GET /testing/analytes` lists the names recorded.
- **Sync and leaks**: the sync log carries laboratory, point and plan rows and sample metadata (reference, subject, status, outcome, investigation status, lot held); results, limits and investigation findings are read through endpoints. Which specification a sample's limits came from is shown only to readers of Specifications; the copied limits themselves are part of the sample. Testing adds nothing to search or notifications of its own. Files hang on the sample (`testing_sample`: certificates of analysis, reports, photos), locked once it is complete or cancelled.
- **MCP**: `list_test_samples`, `get_test_sample`, `log_test_sample`, `record_test_results`, `get_testing_due`, `get_test_trend`. No tool closes an investigation or releases a lot.
- **Not built yet**: QA release that requires passing results before a lot is released (3.2.3: today a failure holds an available lot, but releasing a quarantined lot doesn't yet check pending or failed tests); notifications for overdue plans and failures; batch-level samples (a batch's output lots are sampled individually); charts of trends; laboratory imports or LIMS feeds; recording cleaning and process validation separately from routine monitoring.

## Product development module

New product development (Phase 3, milestone 3.2.5): from the brief to launch. Needs `items` and `recipes`; opt-in. Approved commercial recipes stay in Recipes, and the project links to them. Code: `packages/core/src/npd.ts` (validation, `npdLaunchChecklist`), `packages/db/src/schema/npd.ts`, `apps/server/src/{services,routes}/npd.ts`, `apps/mcp/src/tools/npd.ts`, and the web pages `/<workspace>/npd` and `/<workspace>/npd/<id>`.

- **Projects** (`npd_projects`): a code unique among live projects, the brief (concept, customer, target launch date, target cost per unit, target shelf life, storage, claims, constraints), and links to the output item (finished or work in progress) and its recipe (which must make that item). Stages `brief`, `development`, `trials` and `validation` change freely, and the first trial moves an early project on to `trials`. `launched` and `cancelled` (with a reason) are final, and the project is then read-only.
- **Trials** (`npd_trials`): numbered per project, of kind kitchen, factory, shelf life or sensory. Each records the date (not in the future), what was tried, optionally the version of the project's recipe used, the outcome (pass, needs changes, fail) and findings. A shelf-life study records the life achieved in days.
- **Sign-offs**: HACCP plan reviewed, artwork agreed and customer sign-off (only when the brief names a customer). Each records who signed, the date and a reference, plus who recorded it. Recording one is a person's attestation (`forbidTokens`, "Record product development sign-offs").
- **Launch checklist** (`npdLaunchChecklist`, computed on read):
  - the brief is complete (concept, launch date, shelf life, storage);
  - the item and recipe are linked, and the recipe has an approved version;
  - a factory trial passed;
  - a passing shelf-life study reached the target;
  - an approved specification exists for the item and an approved product label for the recipe (`na` when Specifications or Labels is off);
  - the sign-offs are recorded.

  `POST /npd/projects/:id/launch` needs every check met (`409 not_ready` lists what's missing), `manage` and a person ("Launch new products").

- **Sync and files**: projects and trials sync as `npd_project` and `npd_trial`. Files hang on the project (`npd_project`: brief, trial photos, artwork proofs, the customer's approval) and are locked once it is launched or cancelled.
- **MCP**: `list_npd_projects`, `get_npd_project`, `save_npd_project` (create or update the brief, stage and links) and `record_npd_trial`. There are no tools for sign-offs or launch.
- **Not built yet**:
  - costing the recipe against the target cost;
  - shelf-life studies with intermediate test points linked to Testing samples;
  - a gate-based process with approvals between stages;
  - customer and artwork records beyond sign-offs and attachments;
  - notifications for target dates.

## Food defence module

Food defence (Phase 3, milestone 3.3.2): protecting products from deliberate contamination and other malicious acts (TACCP-style threat assessment). No dependencies; opt-in. Code: `packages/core/src/defence.ts` (validation, `defenceThreatRating`, `defenceApprovalBlockers`, `defenceControlDue`, `diffDefenceThreats`), `packages/db/src/schema/defence.ts`, `apps/server/src/{services,routes}/defence.ts`, `apps/mcp/src/tools/defence.ts`, and the web pages under `/<workspace>/defence` (overview, assessments, controls and checks, exercises).

- **Threat assessments** (`defence_assessments`, `defence_assessment_versions`): a title and scope; the threats live in versions. Each threat has an area, what could happen, a source (employee, contractor, visitor, intruder, supply chain, cyber, other), likelihood and impact on 1–5, the controls in place, further mitigation, and the security controls whose checks verify it. Likelihood × impact rates it: 1–6 low, 8–12 medium, 15–25 high.
  - One draft and one approved version per assessment, enforced by the database. The first version is a draft; later ones start with `POST /defence/assessments/:id/review`, which copies the approved version and records the trigger (scheduled, security incident, site or process change, new intelligence, exercise findings, other) and a reason. The approved version stays in force meanwhile.
  - Approving (`POST /defence/assessments/:id/draft/approve`) needs `manage` and a person ("Approve food defence assessments"). It is refused (`409 not_ready`) without at least one threat, a future review date, a mitigation for every high threat and existing linked controls. The body names the draft the manager reviewed (`{ draftId, revision }`, from the draft's `revision`, a hash of its content); if the draft was edited or replaced since, approval is refused with `409 draft_changed` and the page reloads it, so nobody signs a version they did not see. The page also disables Approve while the form has unsaved edits. The previous approved version becomes `superseded` and is kept unchanged; `history` returns every version, and `draftChanges` lists threats added, removed, re-rated or reworded since the approved version.
  - An active assessment whose review date has passed, or that was never approved, is due for review.
- **Security controls and checks** (`defence_controls`, `defence_checks`): a control (access, perimeter, locks and keys, CCTV, tamper evidence, personnel, cyber, other) has an area, a frequency in days and what to check. It is due its frequency after the last check; never checked counts as overdue, and it is due soon within a week, or a quarter of the interval when shorter. A check is a person's attestation ("Record security checks"): the date (not in the future), pass or fail, notes (required on a fail) and action taken. A failure stays open until a person resolves it with what was done ("Resolve failed security checks").
- **Exercises** (`defence_exercises`): an incident scenario tested, the date, the assessment it tested, participants, outcome (effective, partly effective, ineffective), findings and actions. Recording one is a person's ("Record food defence exercises"). An exercise is due a year after the last one.
- **Overview** (`GET /defence/overview`): controls overdue, never checked or due soon; open failures; assessments due for review; the last exercise and when the next is due; and, only for readers of Attendance, the visitors signed in now from the kiosk register (flagged after 12 hours as possibly not signed out). Visitor records are read, never copied.
- **Sync and files**: metadata only. `defence_assessment`, `defence_assessment_version` (number, status, threat count, review date), `defence_control`, `defence_check` (date, outcome, resolved) and `defence_exercise` (title, date, outcome) sync; threats, check notes and exercise findings describe the site's weak points and are read through the endpoints by members with access. Files hang on exercises (`defence_exercise`: plans, debrief notes, photos).
- **MCP**: `get_food_defence_overview`, `list_defence_assessments`, `get_defence_assessment`, `list_defence_controls`, `draft_defence_assessment` (create, or save the open draft), `start_defence_review` and `add_defence_control`. There are no tools for approval, checks, resolving failures or exercises.
- **Not built yet**:
  - contractor permits and competence (Phase 3.4 HSE); contractors sign in as visitors today;
  - raising a quality incident or CAPA from a failed check or an ineffective exercise (record it in Quality and refer to it);
  - a separate log of security incidents (use Quality incidents);
  - a second approver for assessments;
  - notifications when a control or review falls due.

## Management review (governance) module

Governance (Phase 3, milestone 3.3.3): the management review cycle and what feeds it. No dependencies; opt-in, except that any member can raise a confidential concern. Code: `packages/core/src/governance.ts` (validation, `governanceDueStatus`, `governanceCultureState`, `governanceSignBlockers`, the standing agenda `governanceAgendaTopics`), `packages/db/src/schema/governance.ts`, `apps/server/src/{services,routes}/governance.ts`, `apps/mcp/src/tools/governance.ts`, the web pages under `/<workspace>/governance` (overview, reviews, objectives, culture plan, requirements, concerns), and `/<workspace>/concerns` for raising one.

- **Objectives** (`governance_objectives`): food safety, quality, legality, culture or other; how they are measured, the target, an owner (a member), a due date and status (open, achieved, missed, withdrawn). Progress reports (date, on track / at risk / off track, measured value, note, who) are kept in order on the objective.
- **Culture plan** (`governance_culture_activities`): surveys, briefings, communications, recognition, workshops, leadership walks; the planned date, owner, how the effect is measured and the objective it supports. Recording it done adds the date, how many took part and what it showed. State is planned, overdue (past its date, not done), done or cancelled.
- **Confidential concerns** (`governance_concerns`): any member raises one at `POST /concerns`, a platform route that only needs Governance to be on. Category, description, and optionally anonymous, in which case nothing records who raised it. References run `CON-0001`, `CON-0002`, …. A person raises them, never a token.
  - Only people with `manage` on Governance read and handle them (`GET`/`PATCH /governance/concerns`, never a token: "Read and handle confidential concerns"). Handling sets the status, a confidential investigation, and the outcome, which closing requires.
  - The reporter sees their own named concerns' status and outcome at `GET /concerns/mine`, not the investigation.
  - Concerns are never written to the sync log, search, notifications, attachments or MCP. Reviews and the overview count them without content.
- **Requirement register** (`governance_requirements`): legislation, customer codes of practice, standards and certification requirements, with reference, customer, description, owner and next review date. A compliance review is a person's ("Record compliance reviews"): the date, compliant / partly / not compliant, notes and the next review. Requirements never assessed, not compliant, or due for review show on the overview.
- **Standards gap analysis (G1)** (`governance_standards`, `governance_standard_clauses`, `governance_clause_assessments`, `governance_clause_tasks`; sync entities `governance_standard`, `governance_standard_clause`, `governance_clause_assessment`, `governance_clause_task`). Rules in `@knotworking/core` `governance-standards.ts`.
  - **No standard text ships with the software.** Standards such as BRCGS are published under licence, so each workspace imports clauses from its own licensed copy.
  - **Library** (`manage`): `POST /governance/standards` adds a standard and issue (code, name, issue, publisher; code and issue unique). A new issue is a new record, so assessments against the old one stay; `PATCH` archives it (archived standards can't be assessed). `POST /governance/standards/:id/clauses/import` takes CSV with a header row: clause and title (required), then text, section (default: the clause's first number), section title and fundamental (yes/no), in any order and under common names. New clause numbers are added, existing ones take the new title and text (a correction), missing ones are left alone; any error is reported by line and changes nothing; `dryRun` checks only.
  - **Assessment** (a person's judgement: "Assess compliance against a standard", never a token): `POST /governance/clauses/:id/assessments` appends compliant, partly compliant, gap or not applicable with a note (required unless compliant), and for a gap or partial compliance an owner (a member) and a date to close it by. The latest is the clause's state; `GET /governance/clauses/:id` gives the history, newest first.
  - **Gap analysis**: `GET /governance/standards/:id?status=&section=&fundamental=&q=` returns the summary (counts by status overall and per section, assessed %, open and overdue findings, fundamental clauses not yet compliant) and the clauses in clause order (1.1.2 before 1.1.10) with their latest assessment and linked tasks (for tasks readers). Filters narrow the clause list, never the summary. `GET /governance/standards/:id/report.csv` exports every clause with its status, note, owner, due date and assessor. `GET /governance/standards` lists the library with each standard's progress.
  - **Closing findings**: `POST /governance/clauses/:id/tasks` (`write`, tasks module on) raises a task for a clause with an open finding, titled with the standard and clause and taking the finding's owner and date.
  - **Web**: Management review → Standards (the library, adding a standard) and each standard's gap analysis (summary, section table, filters, clauses with assess, history and raise task, clause import, CSV). **MCP**: `list_standards`, `get_gap_analysis`, `get_standard_clause`, `import_standard_clauses`, `raise_clause_task`; assessing stays a person's.
- **Standards gap analysis: evidence checks (G2)** (`governance_clause_evidence`; sync entity `governance_clause_evidence`). Check definitions live in `@knotworking/core` `governance-standards.ts` (`evidenceChecks`); evaluators in `apps/server/src/services/governance-evidence.ts` reuse each module's own read models (quality summary, HACCP due and plan reviews, SOP acknowledgements, supplier compliance, training via `services/ports/training`, food defence, authenticity, management reviews, the requirements register).
  - **Checks** (16): traceability test passed in the last 12 months, internal audits on schedule, no overdue non-conformance actions, HACCP plans reviewed, CCP monitoring up to date, cleaning on schedule, measuring equipment calibrated, SOPs reviewed, SOPs acknowledged, specifications reviewed, supplier approvals and certificates current, training and competence current, food defence assessed, food fraud vulnerability assessed, management review held and signed, legal and customer requirements reviewed. Each gives met, gap, no data (nothing on record yet) or unavailable, with a detail line and up to 10 records behind a gap. Never-done calibration, cleaning and CCP checks count as gaps (a per-batch CCP on a batch in progress is only due); outdated training counts as a gap, training awaiting sign-off does not; SOP acknowledgements show counts per SOP, not who (that list stays with SOP managers). A check that fails reads "Couldn't be checked" and the reason goes to the server log.
  - **Mapping** (`manage`; configuration like clause import, so tokens may do it, and it never changes a clause's assessed status): `PUT /governance/clauses/:id/evidence` replaces a clause's set of checks. `GET /governance/evidence-checks` lists every check with its current result and the clauses mapped to it in active standards.
  - **Gap analysis**: each clause carries its evidence results, an evidence state (none, met, gap, or partial view when some checks are hidden from the reader) and `conflict` when it is assessed compliant or not applicable while its evidence shows a gap. The summary adds clauses with evidence, evidence gaps and conflicts; `?evidence=gap|conflict|none` filters the clauses. The CSV adds Evidence, Evidence gaps and Conflict columns.
  - **Access**: a check's result shows only when its modules are switched on and the reader can read them; otherwise it reads `unavailable` with no detail or records. Checks are evaluated on read; assessment stays a person's (evidence never changes a clause's status).
  - **Web**: evidence chips per clause with the records behind a gap, an "Evidence contradicts" badge, Evidence gaps and Conflicts tiles, an evidence filter and a mapping dialog. **MCP**: `list_evidence_checks`, `map_clause_evidence`, and the `evidence` filter on `get_gap_analysis`.
- **Standards gap analysis: drafted findings and snapshots (G3)** (`governance_clause_drafts`, `governance_gap_snapshots`; sync entities `governance_clause_draft`, `governance_gap_snapshot`).
  - **Drafted findings** (`write`, agents included): `POST /governance/clauses/:id/drafts` proposes compliant, partial, gap or not applicable with the finding, its basis (evidence, records, documents looked at) and an optional owner and date. Drafts made with an API token are marked as an agent's. A draft changes nothing: the gap analysis shows it beside the clause (`draft`, summary `openDrafts`, `?drafts=open`) and the clause keeps its status. A new draft replaces the clause's open one (`superseded`), except that an agent can't replace a person's draft (409), and an agent can only dismiss agents' drafts.
  - **Accepting** (a person: "Assess compliance against a standard", never a token): `POST /governance/drafts/:id/accept` takes the person's own assessment (same rules as recording one; idempotent with `commandId`), records it as theirs and marks the draft accepted with a link to it. The web form offers the draft's wording, owner and date but never pre-selects its finding. `POST /governance/drafts/:id/dismiss` (`write`) closes it with an optional reason; the status is unchanged. A decided draft can't be decided again (409). `GET /governance/clauses/:id` lists a clause's drafts with its history.
  - **Snapshots** (`manage` to take): `POST /governance/standards/:id/snapshots` freezes every clause's status, finding, owner, due date, assessor and evidence results as the taker sees them now, with a name and the workspace date. Snapshots are never edited. `GET /governance/standards/:id/snapshots` lists them; `GET /governance/snapshots/:id` returns one with its summary and what has changed since (clauses whose status differs now, clauses added or removed; a reassessment to the same status with a new note or owner isn't listed); `GET /governance/snapshots/:id/report.csv` exports it. On read, evidence from modules the viewer can't read is hidden again and the evidence summary recomputed, so a snapshot never shows more than the live analysis. Only the header is synced, not the frozen payload.
  - **Web**: draft cards on clauses with Review draft and Dismiss, a Drafts to review tile and filter, a Snapshot action and list, and a read-only snapshot page with what changed since. **MCP**: `draft_clause_finding`, `take_gap_snapshot`, `list_gap_snapshots`, `get_gap_snapshot`, and `drafts_only` on `get_gap_analysis`; accepting stays a person's.
- **Management reviews** (`governance_reviews`): a title and the period looked back over.
  - Evidence is assembled live for a draft from the modules the reader can see. A module that is off or unreadable is marked unavailable rather than shown as zero. The evidence covers:
    - actions from the last five signed reviews and their task state;
    - objectives, the culture plan and requirements;
    - concern counts;
    - Quality: complaints, non-conformances, audits and audit findings, incidents;
    - Testing: samples, failures, open investigations;
    - Food defence: overdue controls, open failures, assessments due, exercises.
  - The review records when it was held, attendees, notes per agenda topic, resources, decisions (each with an optional owner and due date) and the follow-up team.
  - Signing (`POST /governance/reviews/:id/sign`, `manage` and a person: "Sign management reviews") is refused (`409 not_ready`) until it was held, attendees are recorded, owned decisions have due dates, and (with Tasks on) a follow-up team is chosen.
  - Signing snapshots the evidence, so a signed review shows what was reviewed, and creates a task in the follow-up team for each owned decision, assigned to the owner with the due date, in the same transaction. A signed review no longer changes. The stored snapshot holds what the signer could read, but every read filters it for the current reader (`evidenceForReader`): Quality, Testing and Food defence sections are `available: false` and earlier actions' task states `unknown` for anyone who can't read those modules now.
- **Sync**: `governance_objective`, `governance_culture_activity`, `governance_requirement` and `governance_review` sync. Reviews sync as metadata only: title, period, held, status and signed. Notes, decisions and evidence are read through the endpoint.
- **MCP**:
  - read: `get_governance_overview`, `list_governance_objectives`, `list_culture_activities`, `list_governance_requirements`, `list_management_reviews`, `get_management_review`;
  - write: `save_governance_objective` (with progress), `save_culture_activity`, `add_governance_requirement`, `save_management_review` (prepare the draft and decisions);
  - no tools for signing, compliance reviews or concerns.
- **Not built yet**:
  - culture survey questions and scoring (record the results as text or attach them);
  - an agenda beyond the standing topics;
  - meeting minutes as a document;
  - notifications for due objectives, activities and reviews;
  - escalating a concern into a non-conformance automatically.

## Sales module

Knotworking stores sales orders and prices. `sales` depends on Customers and Items; access is opt-in. The guarded Sales pages provide quotes/orders, customer price agreements, daily delivery commitments, printable documents, retained revisions and external-reference mappings.

- **Quotes and orders:** draft a document against the shared customer and optional delivery site, with a customer reference, snapshotted currency, commercial terms, notes and up to 200 product lines. Each line has a stable UUID, product, entered quantity/unit, base quantity/unit, daily delivery date and price per entered unit. Active finished and WIP items can be sold; only finished products can physically leave through Despatch. Quantities are stored at four decimal places, prices at eight, with decimal multiplication and currency rounding once per line. Line values exclude tax; sales documents are not tax invoices.
- **Price agreements:** a signed-in Sales manager records a customer/product/currency price, price unit, inclusive validity dates, contract reference and terms. Overlapping active agreements are rejected even when units differ. A blank draft price selects the agreement effective on that line's delivery date and converts it to the entered unit. An explicit price (including zero for free of charge) overrides this; the signing reason records the commercial decision. Archiving retains the agreement, actor and reason. Existing document prices are not repriced by subsequent agreement changes. An amendment that leaves a line's price out keeps its agreed price; on a draft a blank price selects the agreement in force, as for a new line. A kept price keeps its agreement link only while that agreement still applies (same customer, currency and item, valid on the line's date, same price in the line's unit); otherwise the price stands as an explicit one with no link.
- **Lifecycle:** a signed-in Sales manager issues a saved quote or confirms a saved order, with a reason and the exact revision reviewed. Customer/site/item status and saved recipient/item/unit facts are revalidated; master-data changes require a refreshed draft. Quote validity uses the workspace business date. One issued, unexpired quote can create one live draft order, preserving its source and terms; the draft keeps the quote's customer, delivery site and currency. If that order is cancelled while still a draft, the quote can be converted again; an order that was ever confirmed (`confirmed_at`) keeps the quote converted even once cancelled, so a cancelled balance can't be re-ordered from the same quote (enforced by a partial unique index). Confirmation of that order is a separate signed decision. Issuing a quote does not send it to anyone.
- **Amendments and cancellation:** confirmed orders can be amended by a signed-in manager, with a reason, stable line IDs and a new retained signed revision. Customer, delivery site and currency stay fixed after confirmation. Lines linked to confirmed despatches cannot be removed or have their item/unit/conversion changed, and quantities cannot fall below shipped amounts; a link to a since-voided despatch no longer fixes a line, and is closed automatically if its line is removed or changes item or unit. An active invoice reservation also fixes the line’s item, unit, quantity and unit price and prevents removal. A line whose item has since been archived can stay unchanged through an amendment, but changing its quantity, unit or date needs an active item. Cancel the remaining balance rather than deleting history; cancellation does not undo a real delivery. Draft edits and each commercial decision append full snapshots with actor, action, revision and reason. History is paginated and accessible from the document.
- **Fulfilment and returns:** with additional Despatch read access, a Sales manager can assign whole confirmed despatch lines to order lines. The existing customer/site/item/base unit must match; a despatch line can have only one live assignment, and assignments cannot exceed the remaining order quantity. Link corrections retain their original evidence and require a signed reason; a cancelled order's links can't be corrected (nothing can be linked to it again). Despatch remains the only stock-posting workflow. Voided despatches cease to fulfil orders; signed physical return receipts are visible as returned quantities but do not automatically reopen a commercial order or create a financial credit. Replacement orders and credits require separate decisions. The Despatch module and its QA, stock, packing and load controls remain authoritative.
- **Demand boundary:** `/sales/commitments?from=&to=` is a paginated **confirmed-sales-only** daily read model, with ordered/outstanding quantities in each item's base unit. Quotes and cancelled orders are excluded; linked confirmed deliveries reduce outstanding. It excludes forecasts and commercial prices. **Sales orders are not copied to, added to, or automatically reconciled with the existing weekly Demand/Production plan.** Planners must currently maintain that plan separately. A future combined planner needs an explicit forecast-consumption policy and shared customer-ID mapping; a customer name match must not silently consume an unrelated forecast. This separation avoids introducing duplicate order/forecast writes, but is not a combined sales-driven production plan.
- **Privacy and agents:** all sales routes, views and MCP tools require Sales access. Commercial prices, terms, notes and historical customer/address snapshots are never added to general bootstrap, search, notifications or other modules' sync. Sales-owned sync events contain only IDs and revision metadata. Agents may read, create/edit drafts and (with manage access) map external IDs; they cannot issue, confirm, amend/cancel commitments, agree/archive prices or assign/correct deliveries. Fulfilment evidence requires Despatch read as well. Sales commitment quantities are a Sales read model and contain no despatch contacts or lot details.

## Health, safety and environment (3.4.3)

Two opt-in permissions separate ordinary operational evidence from personal incident records:

- **`hse`** uses Training. Contractor permits additionally require Assets read access (a permit is raised against an asset of the register; Assets is a soft dependency). HSE provides controlled registers, checks/measurements, contractor work permits and retained evidence files. Read sees operational evidence; write prepares drafts and records a person's checks; manage approves/archives assessments, corrects checks and signs permit decisions. These sign-offs permanently refuse API tokens.
- **`hse_incidents`**, labelled **HSE incidents (restricted)**, depends on HSE and must be granted separately. All routes require a signed-in person, including reads; write records/investigates incidents and manage signs closure. General HSE/People/Training/Attendance access does not confer incident access. No private incident metadata, narratives or audit snapshots enter workspace sync, search, bootstrap, ordinary notifications, attachments or MCP tools.

### Controlled registers and verification

The HSE register covers risk assessments, COSHH/SDS, contractor competence, statutory inspection schemes, emergency exercises, waste handling, utilities and environmental obligations. Each has a stable identity and title, an accountable workspace member, scope, an applicable requirement/procedure and rationale, controls, failure/emergency response, evidence references and site-defined review/check dates. No legal deadline, statutory interval or automatic certification judgement is assumed.

Risk assessments record hazards, people at risk, initial and residual likelihood/severity (1–5), controls and the residual-risk rationale. COSHH records a substance, a dated SDS reference, safe-use/emergency arrangements and optional SDS attachment reference; a linked file must belong to that register in this workspace. Contractor records reuse an existing Items service provider with approved status, competence evidence/expiry and any required courses from the existing Training matrix. Waste registers record the classification/code, measurement unit, existing service provider and authorisation reference; environmental registers record the site's applicable obligation and reporting procedure. Utility records name the resource/meter and unit. These are site-maintained evidence, not external licence/SDS verification or automatic environmental returns.

Approval validates kind-specific completeness and current master references and records the reviewing person/reason. The signed version continues while a review draft is prepared. Approving the next version supersedes, rather than overwrites, preceding evidence. Archive is a signed, retained history entry; archived scopes cannot authorise new work. Referenced Tasks are existing workspace records, checked on linking with additional Tasks read access. HSE stores task references, not copies of protected task narratives, and creates no second action tracker.

A signed-in HSE writer records a business-dated check/exercise result with pass/fail, findings, actions, evidence reference and optional next-due override. The latest non-corrected check by performed date (then creation/ID) drives the due date for the current assessment version; a new review needs its own checks. A failure stays open across later unrelated passes and assessment reviews. An explicit passing verification with action evidence resolves it; correcting that pass restores the failure. A manager corrects erroneous entries with a dated reason, retaining the original. Future performed dates are refused. Waste/resource entries use `recorded`, positive numeric quantities, the approved measurement unit and, for utilities, the resource-use period; they do not post stock or mix units into an invented total. Waste transfer and inspection certificate references belong in the entry's evidence field.

List, entry, version and permit-history queries are paginated; attention is calculated for the records on the requested page, not a site-wide all-clear. Unscheduled and overdue checks, overdue assessment reviews and unresolved failures are distinguished. Supporting files on `hse_record`, `hse_entry` and `hse_permit` reuse the existing attachment mechanism. Files are retained from upload and cannot be deleted/replaced; corrected evidence is a new file and revision/reference. General HSE evidence must not contain personal accident/health files.

### Contractor permits and isolation

A draft references an existing asset of the register, a contractor-competence register, at least one risk assessment (optional COSHH assessments too), work dates/scope/precautions, isolation points and induction/PPE/emergency checks. Required courses use an existing registered worker; permit issue/resume reuses Training's current-SOP, competency sign-off and expiry rules, valid through the work's end, with qualifying training record IDs/sign-off references in the evidence snapshot. Optional visitor sign-in references reuse Attendance and require additional Attendance read access when linked or issuing/resuming; no visitor contact/name snapshot is copied into HSE.

A signed-in HSE manager issues the reviewed draft. The asset must be active; referenced records and competence must be signed/current through the work; linked contractor/assessment failures must be explicitly resolved; the service provider must remain approved and its recorded review/certificate dates current. Existing Items supplier-control blocking policy applies without an HSE override. A linked visit must still be on site. The permit retains the contractor, assessment, asset and training evidence reviewed at issue/resume. Issued permits cannot be edited; suspend work, recheck/resume, or close and replace them.

Issued **and suspended** permits requiring isolation block Site's maintenance hygiene clearance for that asset. This persistent check runs under the workspace lock and applies even when HSE is disabled or hidden from the Site caller. Expiry does not release an isolation. Only a signed return-to-service decision closes an issued/suspended permit: work complete, tools removed, protections restored, isolation safely removed and the asset safe to return, with person/time/reason and the assertions retained. Multiple permits must all relinquish their own hold. Cancelling is limited to drafts. Site hygiene clearance remains separate and still applies its existing checks.

This is a recorded work-authorisation/isolation workflow and a guard on Site clearance; it does not actuate machinery or implement a production-line hardware interlock. Supervision, changes after issue, physical lock-off and actual safe-working controls remain site operations. No approval silently creates maintenance completion, training sign-off, stock release or an external report.

### Restricted incident investigations

Record accidents, near misses and environmental incidents with the business date/place, affected existing staff, restricted health narrative, immediate actions, investigation owner, evidence/findings, root cause, corrective actions and effectiveness verification. Each update appends a private snapshot with actor, reason and revision. Reportability is explicitly `not_assessed`, `required` or `not_required`, with an applicable requirement/rationale and a site-determined deadline when required. Closure requires a completed investigation, action/effectiveness evidence and assessed reporting; required reporting also needs submission evidence. Nothing submits a report or assumes a statutory timetable. Closed investigations are immutable; preserve a later factual correction as a new referenced incident.

Personal evidence files stay in the site's approved restricted document store; the incident records its reference. There is deliberately no generic attachment target for private incidents in this increment. This avoids exposing health files through general HSE file listings, agent links or attachment sync. Private records do not appear in a general HSE attention count. API-token and MCP access remains prohibited even for a token owned by a workspace administrator.

#### Incident follow-up: actions, people hurt, reports and the overview

Code: `packages/core/src/hse-incidents.ts`, `packages/db/src/schema/hse-incidents.ts`, `apps/server/src/services/hse-incident-follow-up.ts`, routes in `apps/server/src/routes/hse.ts`, and `apps/web/src/app/[workspace]/hse/incident-follow-up.tsx`.

- **Kinds and outcome**: incidents are accidents, near misses, dangerous occurrences, work-related ill health, unsafe conditions or acts, or environmental incidents, with a worst outcome (`none`, first aid, medical treatment, lost time, major). The outcome is set only together with the people hurt; records made before it existed read as `none`.
- **People hurt** (`hse_incident_injuries`) are restricted beyond the module. Each records the name (and People record, when there is one), employee / agency / contractor / visitor / other, the injury or ill health, part of the body, treatment and days away.
  - Only a signed-in `hse_incidents` manager reads them, and the incident's free-text health details, in the incident, its saves and its history. Everyone else with access sees `injuryCount` and a "Restricted" placeholder.
  - A non-manager's save keeps the stored health details.
  - `PUT /hse/incidents/:id/injuries` (manager) replaces the people hurt and sets the outcome in one step, so harm found after the report is recorded on the original incident. Replaced entries are soft-deleted, not removed. A near miss or unsafe condition has nobody hurt. While people are recorded as hurt, nobody can change the incident's kind to near miss or unsafe condition. Non-managers see a neutral reason on people-hurt history events, because a manager's reason may name people.
- **Corrective actions** (`hse_incident_actions`): a description, an owner (a member with `hse_incidents` write access, so they can see it) and a due date, added with write access (`POST …/actions`). The owner or a manager completes one with a note, and only a manager cancels one (`POST …/actions/:actionId/complete`). Every add, completion or cancellation needs the reviewed revision, advances the incident's revision and is recorded in its history, so a closure signed against an earlier revision is refused. Closure is also refused while any action is open. The free-text "corrective actions" summary is still required.
- **Reports** (`POST /hse/reports`, HSE `write`, API tokens too): a near miss, unsafe condition, dangerous occurrence or environmental incident in which nobody was hurt, with the date, place, description and immediate action. It becomes a restricted incident owned by the reporter (for a token, its owner) until an investigator reassigns it; a reporter without incident access can't see it, and only its id and reference are returned. The HSE page has "Report a near miss"; MCP has `report_hse_near_miss`.
- **Overview** (`GET /hse/incidents/overview`, the restricted incidents page): open incidents, those without a reporting decision, overdue actions, days since the last lost-time or major injury, and the last twelve months by kind (from a clamped date a year back, so 29 February works).

All commands require a durable `commandId`; revisions and the shared workspace lock reject stale decisions and serialize concurrent work. Private incident writes/audit events intentionally omit `recordChanges`, like other confidential platform records. Operating owners must populate/approve their own scopes, methods, schedules, reporting criteria, contractor evidence and environmental obligations. This milestone does not establish a complete legal/compliance register or replace Finance/HR (3.4.2/3.4.4).

## Invoicing module

Enable `invoicing` alongside `customers` in the installation and workspace. Invoicing is opt-in and defaults to no member access. Grant **read** to view and download issued documents, **write** to draft and edit, and **manage** to approve, issue or discard a draft. Approval and discard require a person's session.

- Open **Operations → Invoicing** to draft a manual invoice. Choose a customer, currency, invoice date and due date. Enter quantities, unit prices and tax rates. Tax is calculated per line on the rounded net amount.
- Use **Draft customer invoice** on a confirmed sales order, confirmed despatch or signed-off client-service quote when the source modules are available and readable. With Despatch enabled, sales drafts use unbilled delivery lines linked to the order and price them from the agreed order. With Despatch disabled, a confirmed order with no delivery links can bill its order lines. The Despatch draft includes only standalone lines, uses the workspace currency and needs a reviewed price for every line. Draft order-linked deliveries from Sales to use the order’s currency and agreed price. Lines drafted from a source start without a tax decision: choose each line's tax rate, even 0%, and save before issuing. Returns recorded before approval reduce the billable quantity. Discard and redraft if a return changes the quantity after drafting.
- A service draft copies the fixed fee or the exact amount for approved hours. The separate **Download billing draft** CSV remains available. Choose one billing workflow for each job to avoid re-entering an invoice raised from the CSV.
- Issuing assigns the next workspace sequence number and enables PDF download. The PDF embeds the bundled Noto Sans font, so Latin (including accented and Polish letters), Greek and Cyrillic names, addresses and descriptions print as entered. Other scripts, such as Chinese or Arabic, are not covered yet. To correct an issued invoice, open it and **Draft credit note**, select whole lines to reverse, and record the reason. The original and its linked credit notes keep their amounts and decision history. Before voiding a reserved dispatch or return receipt, discard the invoice draft or issue credits for all affected invoice lines. Credits retain the original source reservations and do not reopen billable quantities. To replace a credited invoice for the same quantities, raise a manual invoice.
- A manager can record the workspace's own tax registration number above the invoice list (`GET/PUT /invoicing/settings`; the PUT is `manage` and a person, "Change the seller tax registration number printed on invoices"). New drafts snapshot it and the customer's tax number, and the PDF prints both. Drafted and issued documents keep the numbers they were drafted with.
- Accounting is optional. With it enabled, an administrator can [prepare issued documents for Xero](accounting.md#preparing-customer-invoices-and-credit-notes). Invoicing content and history use dedicated permission-checked routes and stay out of workspace sync.
- Open questions, not yet decided:
  - **Four-eyes approval.** A manager who drafted an invoice can also issue it, as for purchase invoices. Payroll, SOPs, HACCP and quality sign-offs use four-eyes; confirm whether customer invoices should too.
  - **Service-only orders with Despatch on.** With Despatch enabled, Sales drafts bill only delivery lines linked to the order, so an order with no deliveries (services, fees) cannot be drafted from Sales. Raise a manual invoice for now.
  - **Database-level immutability.** Issued documents are protected by the application, not by a database trigger or rule, as for supplier bills.
  - **Chronological numbering.** Numbers follow issue order, not invoice date, so a backdated invoice can carry a higher number than a later-dated one. Check local rules where numbering must follow date order.
  - **Client Services CSV.** The billing-draft CSV does not mark quotes that are already on an active invoice; choose one billing workflow per job.
